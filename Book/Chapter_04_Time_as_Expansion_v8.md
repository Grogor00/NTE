## *Was → Now → Next*

> *Was is written in the geometry. Now is where expansion runs. Next is what has not yet unfolded.*

---

The previous chapter established that we live on a closed three-dimensional space — an S³, expanding, with no interior to access and no exterior to escape into. The space itself is what is expanding, and the parameter that counts the expansion is *N*, the number of e-folds. That picture is now in place. What remains is the second of Chapter 3's two framework commitments. Surface-only — no ambient embedding — was the first, and did its work in the previous chapter. Time emerging from expansion is the second, and this chapter develops it directly: showing how time arises from the expansion, and what that emergence implies about the nature of past, present, and future.

This is the chapter where the book's title earns its keep. *The Shape of Time* is not a metaphor. Time has a shape — a specific geometric relationship to the expanding manifold — and that shape is what we are about to draw.

## What Time Is Not

Before describing what time is in the framework, be clear about what time is not. The standard textbook picture treats time as a background dimension. Space has three of them; time has one. They form a four-dimensional spacetime, and the universe sits in this spacetime, with events occurring at definite spatial-and-temporal coordinates. Time, in this picture, is a pre-existing parameter that ticks regardless of what is happening. The universe could be empty, full, expanding, contracting, or stationary — time still passes.

This picture works for almost every calculation a physicist or engineer ever does. It's built into general relativity, into quantum mechanics, into thermodynamics. It's so deeply embedded in how physics is taught that suggesting time might not be fundamental can sound, at first, like wordplay rather than physics.

But the picture has one specific failure mode, which Chapter 1 already identified. The photon does not fit it. From a photon's own frame, time does not pass between emission and absorption. The photon's proper time is identically zero. If time is a background dimension we move through, the photon should be moving through it like everything else — and it isn't. The textbook picture handles this by saying that the photon's worldline is a *null geodesic*, on which the spacetime interval vanishes, and treating this as a curiosity rather than a clue.

The framework treats it as a clue. If time were truly a background dimension, the photon could not behave the way it does. The photon's null-time is not a curiosity but a signal that time is something else — something that the photon participates in differently from the way the rest of us do.

## What Time Is

In the framework, time is what emerges from the universe's expansion when the expansion is registered by matter — and the registration has two parts, played by two different fields.

The cleanest way to introduce the picture is by the parallel the reader will carry for the rest of the book. The Standard Model Higgs is the field that gives particles their mass: an electron has mass because it couples to the Higgs field; a photon has no mass because it does not. In the framework, that same Higgs coupling does double duty in a second ledger: it is what determines whether a particle *participates in time at all*. A particle's Higgs coupling sets how deeply it is embedded in the expanding boundary, and embedded matter accumulates proper time as the boundary expands. A particle with zero Higgs coupling — no mass — is also a particle with zero embedding depth, and it accumulates no proper time. One coupling, two consequences: mass in the Standard Model's books, participation in time in the framework's.

The second field is the framework's own addition, the *boundary expansion field* |Φ_NTE| — and its job is different in kind. |Φ_NTE| does not couple to particles at all; giving fermions their masses is the Higgs's role, and the framework leaves it there. What |Φ_NTE| does is set the *rate*: its strength governs how fast the boundary is expanding, and therefore how much proper time each e-fold of expansion is worth for the matter embedded in it. The division of labour, in one line: the Higgs decides *who rides*; |Φ_NTE| decides *how fast the ride runs*. In the parallel the book will keep using — the Higgs is the field that gives mass; |Φ_NTE| is the field that gives time — the "gives" works at the level of the whole ride: the field whose strength makes expansion into duration.

The expansion itself is geometric. The S³ is growing, and we count the growth in e-folds *N*. *N* is a pure number. It does not require a clock. You can count expansions without time existing, the way you can count the rings of a tree trunk without time existing for the tree. *N* is the framework's primary parameter, and everything else — including time — is derived from it.

The connection between *N* and *t* runs through the boundary expansion field |Φ_NTE|. This field is, in the framework's picture, the geometric quantity whose strength sets the rate at which the universe is expanding. The relationship is one equation, and it's the central equation of the entire framework:

H(N) = β_NTE|Φ_NTE(N)|²

The left side is the Hubble parameter — the rate of expansion at e-fold *N*. The right side is the boundary expansion field's strength squared, multiplied by a constant β_NTE whose value sets the overall scale. The equation says: the rate of expansion is set by the strength of |Φ_NTE|. Where the field is strong, the universe expands rapidly. Where it's weak, expansion is slow.

This is not an obscure equation. It will appear in every remaining chapter of this book in some form, because almost everything the framework explains follows from it. But the meaning that matters most is its inversion. Rearranging:

dt/dN = 1/(β_NTE|Φ_NTE|²)

This says: for each e-fold of expansion, an interval of proper time accumulates. The interval depends on the strength of the boundary expansion field. The stronger the field, the smaller the time interval per e-fold. The weaker the field, the larger the interval. Time is not a thing that ticks along independently of expansion. Time is what accumulates with expansion at the rate that |Φ_NTE| sets — for the matter whose Higgs coupling has embedded it in the boundary.

You can, if you like, integrate this equation along the universe's history to recover the familiar time coordinate:

t(N) = ∫₀ᴺ dN' / (β_NTE|Φ_NTE(N')|²)

This is the formal statement of how *t* emerges from *N*. Time, in the framework, is the integrated inverse of the Hubble rate along the e-fold history. The "rate at which time passes" is just another way of describing how rapidly the universe is currently expanding.

A clarifying note before going further. This chapter and others will sometimes use production-flavoured language — "the field that gives time," "what causes time to pass," "the rate at which time accumulates." That language is a working picture for the reader. The framework's fundamental statement is more careful: H = β_NTE|Φ_NTE|² is a *geometric constraint* relating two quantities in the manifold's structure (the local expansion rate and the local field strength), and the proper-time coordinate t is then defined by integrating the inverse of this constraint along the e-fold history — t is a proportionality definition, not a separate dynamical law. The production-flavoured language is an effective description; the underlying claim is that time and the boundary expansion field are two readings of the same geometric structure, related by the constraint. Two readings, one structure — the framework's signature move applied here to time itself.

## Two Coordinates, One Process

A reader trained in physics might object that *N* and *t* are just two different coordinates for the same dynamics. Cosmologists routinely use *N* as a convenient parameter; physicists in other contexts routinely use *t*. The choice is conventional. So why call one of them primary and the other derived?

The answer is geometric, and it's the reason the framework uses *N* and not *t* throughout.

When you write the universe's history in *t*, you are committing yourself to a coordinate that already presupposes what the framework is trying to derive. *t* is what we observe; it's what our clocks measure; it's what we experience as the passage of time. But it's also a *consequence* of the more basic process — the expansion — that the framework treats as primary. Writing physics in *t* and asking how time relates to expansion is putting the cart before the horse. You can do it, and the equations work out, but you've buried the explanation under coordinate choices.

When you write the universe's history in *N*, you are using a coordinate that is geometrically meaningful regardless of whether time exists. *N* is a count. It does not require duration. It does not require a metric signature. It does not require any of the apparatus that *t* presupposes. You can use it pre-temporally, when the framework explicitly says no temporal structure has yet emerged, and the equations still make sense. You cannot do this with *t*. *t* requires the very thing it would be describing.

So the framework's commitment is not arbitrary. *N* is the coordinate in which the framework's claims can be stated cleanly without circularity. *t* is the coordinate in which most of physics has been done. The relationship between them — *t* emerging from *N* via the boundary expansion field |Φ_NTE| — is the framework's specific structural claim about how time arises.

Two coordinates, one process — the framework's signature move applied to time itself. *N* and *t* are not two parameters but two readings of one expansion, registered from outside as e-folds counted and from inside as duration accumulated. Chapter 1 made the move for the photon; Chapter 3 made it for the universe's surface; here it lands on time itself.

## The Pre-Temporal Epoch

This brings us to a strange piece of the picture, which Chapter 3 deliberately deferred. The framework distinguishes two regimes.

The *post-temporal* regime is everything we usually mean when we talk about the universe. It's the era from very shortly after the Big Bang to the present. In this regime, *t* has emerged, the relationship dt/dN = 1/(β_NTE|Φ_NTE|²) is well-defined and stable, and ordinary time-based physics works. General relativity, quantum mechanics, thermodynamics, nuclear physics — all of them apply normally. Using *t* in this regime is correct, not an approximation. The deeper structure (*N*) is always there, but the emergent description (*t*) is complete and self-consistent within its domain of validity. The relationship is exactly the same as the relationship between Newtonian gravity and general relativity: both are correct, one is the deeper picture, and using the surface picture in its proper domain is not wrong.

The *pre-temporal* regime is everything before time emerged. In the framework's picture, this is not "the early universe before the Big Bang" in any conventional sense. It is, geometrically, a region of e-fold parameter *N* (specifically, *N* less than a value the framework calls N_lock, around 0.1) at which the boundary expansion field has not yet stabilised into a form that produces the well-behaved dt/dN relationship. In this regime, the equation dt/dN is undefined, which means *t* itself cannot be cleanly defined. Only *N* is meaningful.

This is hard to picture, because our intuitions are built around time. Saying "before time" is itself a temporal phrase, and it presupposes the very thing being denied. The framework is explicit about this: the pre-temporal epoch is defined by a range of *N*, not by a temporal interval. There is no "moment" at which the pre-temporal epoch existed. There is a span of e-folds, geometrically prior to the gauge birth at N_lock, during which time as we know it had not yet condensed out of the underlying expansion.

What kind of physics happens in such an epoch? The honest answer is that the question contains a category error. *Happens* is a temporal verb. The pre-temporal regime is not a region in which things happen; it's a region of geometric structure. The framework's technical corpus describes this regime using the language of *Euclidean geometry* — geometry without a distinguished time direction — as opposed to the *Lorentzian geometry* of ordinary spacetime, which has time as a special direction distinct from the spatial directions. The two are related by a mathematical operation called a Wick rotation. The pre-temporal regime, in the framework's reading, is the Euclidean section of the manifold; the gauge birth at N_lock is the signature change that produces the Lorentzian regime we live in. Readers who know the cosmology literature will recognise the shape of this picture: it is the *no-boundary* geometry that James Hartle and Stephen Hawking proposed in 1983, and the framework's identification is deliberate and credited — in the framework's reading, the no-boundary condition is not a separate postulate but a consequence of taking *N* as primary, with the Euclidean section being what "pre-temporal" looks like when drawn.

What this means for the reader: pre-temporal structure is not a sequence of events arranged along a time axis. It's a set of geometric features arranged along *N* — the closed S³ at varying scales, the boundary expansion field |Φ_NTE| at varying strengths, certain symmetry constraints — and the right way to think about it's the way one thinks about a static three-dimensional object, holding all of *N* in mind at once rather than tracing a path through it. Reading these structures as if they were temporal events — as if the universe was *doing* one thing at one *N* and *then* another thing at a later *N* — is the kind of conceptual mistake the chapter's discipline asks the reader to avoid. There is no "doing" pre-temporally. There is structure.

A note on what is *not* present pre-temporally. The Standard Model Higgs — the field that gives mass to electrons and quarks — does not exist as a quantum field in this epoch, because the gauge structure it lives in hasn't yet switched on. The boundary expansion field |Φ_NTE| is present, but without the post-locking dynamics it later acquires; it's the geometric scalar whose square sources H(N) through the core equation. Both fields' identities crystallise at the gauge birth as separate identities — the SM Higgs as the mass-giving Standard Model field, |Φ_NTE| as the geometric source of H — and the framework keeps this distinction clean permanently: two fields, two jobs. (Their *values* happen to coincide at one cosmic moment, ours; the next chapter explains why that is a matter of how the framework's one inherited constant is calibrated, and why the framework declines to read anything deeper into it.)

The reason any of this matters is that the gauge birth — the structural junction at *N* = N_lock — is a feature whose content the framework places at a specific *N*. The gauge birth is where the Euclidean pre-temporal geometry joins the Lorentzian post-temporal regime, where the boundary expansion field's configuration becomes such that dt/dN is well-defined for all *N* > N_lock, and where the Standard Model gauge symmetries are present (with the SM Higgs's identity as the field that gives mass to electrons and quarks among them). The structural content at that *N* is the basis of everything in the post-temporal regime we inhabit. If we want to understand why our universe has the structure it does, we need to understand what the gauge birth is — and that requires being able to say things about the pre-temporal regime even though time, in the ordinary sense, is not present there. The gauge-birth chapter is where this work is done.

## Time Dilation as Boundary Field Weakening

Here is where the framework's picture starts to do work that the standard picture cannot.

In general relativity, gravitational time dilation is a real effect. Clocks run more slowly in stronger gravitational fields. Near the surface of a neutron star, time passes more slowly than far from the star. At the event horizon of a black hole, time effectively stops, from the perspective of a distant observer. Standard relativity treats this as a property of the metric — the spacetime geometry distorts in the presence of mass, and clocks measure that distortion.

In the framework, gravitational time dilation has a specific structural reading: the boundary expansion field |Φ_NTE| is *weaker* in regions of higher gravitational potential. The relationship dt/dN = 1/(β_NTE|Φ_NTE|²) means that where |Φ_NTE| is reduced, the rate of proper time accumulation per e-fold increases. Or, putting it the other way around: where mass-energy concentrates curvature locally — which, under the framework's structural reading, is the same fact as the local weakening of the boundary expansion field — the local rate of proper time accumulation per e-fold changes, and clocks register the change as time dilation. The framework's technical corpus makes the identification precise rather than rhetorical: the boundary's local *thickness* in the e-fold direction, the local Hubble radius, and the inverse of β_NTE|Φ_NTE|² are one and the same quantity — a single geometric object carrying three names — so a gravity well is a place where the manifold's thickness varies, and gravitational time dilation is the time-side measure of that thickness variation. One structure, read once as geometry and once as field strength. The framework's signature move once again. What the equation pins down is the relationship between this local structure and the local rate of proper time accumulation.

This is more than just a redescription. The framework's reading is consistent with the gravitational time dilation general relativity predicts and that has been confirmed to high precision in every experiment from atomic clocks at different altitudes to the operation of GPS satellites. The boundary expansion field's local configuration in a Newtonian gravitational potential recovers the standard time-dilation factor to first post-Newtonian order — the regime where the effect has actually been tested. The reading is structural: time dilation is what happens to the dt/dN conversion rate when curvature is locally concentrated, which is the same fact as |Φ_NTE| being locally weaker.

One quiet payoff to notice: the boundary expansion field's variation in the presence of mass-energy is exactly the kind of behaviour the SM Higgs does *not* do. The SM Higgs sits at 246 GeV everywhere in the universe; it does not vary spatially in the presence of stars and galaxies, because the Standard Model's potential V_SM holds it locked at its vacuum value. |Φ_NTE| is the field that varies — across cosmic history and across regions of gravitational potential — and that variation is what the framework reads as the source of gravitational time dilation. Two fields, two distinct behaviours. The textbook account of the SM Higgs is unchanged; the framework's account of |Φ_NTE| is what supplies the structural reading that the standard picture leaves abstract.

Two pieces of honesty are in order here. First, the framework's account is consistent with the observed time dilation; it does not yet *derive* it from first principles. Reproducing the standard prediction at first post-Newtonian order is not the same thing as having a complete derivation of how the boundary expansion field's local configuration produces the dilation effect. On the framework's two-field reading, the Standard Model Higgs (which gives mass to electrons and quarks and would otherwise be the natural candidate for "the field that responds to mass-energy") is *not* the field involved; the boundary expansion field |Φ_NTE| is, and |Φ_NTE| does not give mass to particles. The structural identification is committed; the full derivation is technical-corpus work the framework continues to develop.

Second, even with the derivation open, the structural reading does real work. Standard relativity treats gravitational time dilation as a property of the metric, with no further structural reading specified. The framework reads it as a consequence of how the boundary expansion field distributes itself across the manifold, with mass-energy's local concentration of curvature being the same fact as the local weakening of |Φ_NTE|, and the dt/dN conversion rate registering the change as clocks slowing in gravity wells.

The framework picks its empirical battles deliberately. Time dilation is a reading it commits to; the dark-matter ratio κ — a later chapter's territory — is the number it stakes its case on.

## The Epigraph's Work

The chapter epigraph names three parts of time in the framework — **was**, **now**, and **next** — without treating them as coordinates on a pre-existing timeline.

**Was** is written in the geometry. The events of the universe's history did not occur on a timeline that has now moved on. They occurred at e-folds that the universe has now expanded beyond, but the e-folds themselves are not erased by the expansion. The structure of what happened — every photon emitted, every entanglement formed, every particle locking, every gauge transition — is part of the geometric record of the manifold. The universe has not forgotten any of it.

The framework's relationship to the philosophical question of whether time "really flows" is worth stating cleanly. In the *block universe* picture that some interpretations of relativity take seriously, all of spacetime exists at once — past, present, and future are parts of a single four-dimensional structure, and the sense of "now moving forward" is an artefact of conscious experience rather than a feature of physics. The framework is naturally close to this picture for what concerns the **was**: every event that has occurred is geometrically present in the manifold, retained in the structure of how the expansion has unfolded. The past is not somewhere else. It's the geometric record of expansion-already-completed.

But the framework breaks the block-universe symmetry between past and future. **Next**, in the framework, is not a place we are travelling toward through pre-existing spacetime. It's what the manifold has not yet expanded into — and the framework does **not** treat that openness as a hidden track already laid down. *N* is unidirectional — the universe expands, and geometry accumulates as it does — but what has not yet been expanded into has not yet been determined in detail. The past is geometrically present; the future is being generated, not retrieved.

**Now** is where expansion is actively running and proper time accumulates. It is **not** a sharply defined classical "edge of expansion" — no mechanistic frontier where the universe behaves like a clockwork film advancing frame by frame. Quantum superposition, probabilistic collapse, and sensitivity to conditions mean the fine structure of what unfolds is not fixed in advance. How far that openness runs — through chaos, through the fuzz of infinite waveforms collapsing into outcomes, through whatever room remains for agency and an unpredictable future — is honest open work in the framework. This chapter establishes the **was / now / next** asymmetry at the level expansion and geometry can honestly support; a fuller account of open **next** is queued for development elsewhere in the book's arc.

The asymmetry between was and next is structural, built into the fact that *N* increases — but the epigraph's **next** should not be read as a deterministic script.

This is the framework's position at this chapter's scope, and it's what the epigraph catches. The triad does not say *everything that will happen has already happened* — which is the strong block-universe claim, and is not the framework's position. It says that what has occurred is geometrically present in the structure, while what has not occurred has not yet been generated by the expansion — and that the generation is not necessarily predictable in classical detail. The book will return to this distinction in later chapters when we discuss entropy, the arrow of time, and the universe's far-future geometry — and may develop it further in a dedicated chapter on the open future.

For now, the picture to hold is this: the boundary expansion field does not just regulate the rate of proper time accumulation. It does so in a way that creates a directional structure — *N* increases, and time accumulates with it — and the structure that has accumulated does not unwind. The universe is becoming, in the epigraph's sense, in a way that does not allow what has happened to un-happen.

## Where This Leaves the Photon (Again)

Both Chapter 1 and Chapter 3 returned to the photon at their close, because the photon is the framework's first and clearest test case. Here, with the time-emergence picture in place, we can give the cleanest version yet.

The photon, Chapter 1 established, is not a thing in flight. It's the geometric connection between emission and absorption — one object with two readings: from outside, length in space and time; along the connection itself, both extents vanish together. The two-field picture gives this its mechanism, and gives it with striking economy. The photon has exactly one relevant zero: its Higgs coupling. In the Standard Model's ledger, that zero is why the photon is massless. In the framework's ledger, the *same* zero is why the photon accumulates no time: zero Higgs coupling means zero embedding depth in the expanding boundary, and a structure with no embedding does not enter the dt map at all. It sits on the boundary itself, riding the expansion at the wavefront rather than within the thickness, and the null-time the photon-line registers is exactly that non-participation.

So the famous correlation the book opened with — everything massless is timeless, everything massive is temporal, no exceptions — is not two facts that happen to travel together. It is one coupling read in two ledgers. The framework did not have to arrange the correlation; it had to *notice* that the Standard Model's mass dial and the framework's time dial are the same dial. Other species acquired non-zero settings of that one dial at the gauge birth — the cascade event a later chapter develops — and with them acquired both their masses and their participation in time, in one assignment. The heaviest species are the most deeply embedded; the neutrino, at a coupling of roughly one part in a trillion, barely participates at all, a marginality whose consequences become one of the framework's central threads.

For us, made of atoms whose constituents carry their full couplings, time passes at the rate it does because our matter is embedded in the boundary, and the boundary's expansion — at the rate the local strength of |Φ_NTE| sets — converts e-folds into duration for everything embedded in it. The years we count, the seconds our clocks tick, the moments we experience — all of these are local realisations of dt/dN = 1/(β_NTE|Φ_NTE|²), integrated across the matter we are made of and the e-folds we have lived through.

This is not a metaphor. It's the framework's specific claim. Time, for any physical system, is the integrated outcome of that system's embedding in the universe's expansion. A different coupling pattern would give a different time.

## What Comes Next

We have now seen three pillars of the framework. The first, from Chapter 1, was that the photon's null-time is not a curiosity but the seed observation from which the whole framework grows. The second, from Chapter 3, was that the universe is a closed three-dimensional space without interior or exterior, expanding through e-folds *N*. The third, from this chapter, is that time emerges from this expansion via the boundary expansion field, with H = β_NTE|Φ_NTE|² as the central equation and dt = dN/(β_NTE|Φ_NTE|²) as its inversion.

What remains to be developed is the nature of the boundary expansion field itself — what it is, how it sets the rate, and why it deserves the central role the framework gives it. The reader has met the structural parallel — the Higgs is the field that gives particles mass; |Φ_NTE| is the field that gives them time. The framework's central scalar is |Φ_NTE|, the geometric scalar whose strength regulates the rate at which proper time accumulates per unit expansion. The next chapter takes this seriously and works out what it means — including why |Φ_NTE| and the SM Higgs cannot be the same field, and what to make of the one moment in cosmic history when their values cross.

There is also a question that this chapter has deferred and that the next one will address directly. The framework has been speaking as if the relationship dt/dN = 1/(β_NTE|Φ_NTE|²) is well-defined throughout cosmic history. But in the very early universe, during the pre-temporal epoch, this relationship is exactly what the framework says was *not* well-defined. How does the universe transition from the pre-temporal regime to the post-temporal regime? What event marks that transition? And what does it mean for time to "switch on" if there is no time in which the switching could occur?

These questions take us into the gauge birth, which the framework places at the heart of how everything we observe came to be. That is a later chapter's territory. For now, the picture from this chapter is enough to carry forward.

The shape of time, in the framework, is the shape of expansion. The universe has been expanding, unfolding, and accumulating proper time at every e-fold since whatever counts as the beginning. The geometry of expansion is the record of what has occurred.
