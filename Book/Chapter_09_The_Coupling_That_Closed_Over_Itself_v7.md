## §1 — The Empirical Mystery (still unsolved here)

In our universe, dark matter outweighs ordinary matter by roughly 5.4 to 1. Planck 2018 pinned the cosmic ratio at 5.38 ± 0.15. Independent gravitational evidence corroborates that something gravitating, not described by the Standard Model, is present at that scale.

The Standard Model has nothing to say about this number. Candidate extensions *fit* it by tuning masses, decay constants, or abundances. None of them *predicts* it from inputs that were not chosen to make it come out right.

The framework of this book believed, for several years, that it did something different: that a bilateral closure over κ₀ predicted this ratio without parameters. **That belief is withdrawn.** The framework does not currently account for the cold, collisionless, co-located component cosmologists measure. It joins the list of theories without a deposited dark-matter candidate rather than ending it. What it still has that most candidates on that list do not is a bare coupling derived without parameters — and a forced Majorana consequence that does not run through cosmology at all.

A note about what bilateral structure still constrains, independent of any amplifier. The bilateral picture makes a cross-orientation contribution non-negative. The requirement that the bare coupling sit below one — κ₀ < 1 — is what forces exactly three light neutrino eigenmodes and the Majorana condition. Those remain live structural claims. What is no longer claimed is that a closed series over κ₀ yields the sky's dark-matter ratio, or even that the closed series is a deposited framework quantity.
