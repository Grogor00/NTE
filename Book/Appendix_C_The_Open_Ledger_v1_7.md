## C.1 Where the Framework Is Currently Losing

Six results currently sit on the wrong side of observation, or below the tier the framework would want for them. They are live, they are not footnotes, and none has been tuned away. The first is new to this edition and is the largest single loss the ledger has recorded.

**The observed cold dark matter — no account, and a structural reason why.** Until October 2026 this ledger did not carry an entry here at all, because the framework believed it had an account: the gravitational footprint of the conjugate orientation, with a ratio fixed at κ = 5.50 by an amplifier and agreeing with Planck's 5.38 ± 0.15. **That identification was withdrawn (2026-10-06), and the amplifier / 5.50 itself is withdrawn (2026-10-09, broad).** What remains is the bare coupling κ₀ = 3/√(4π) (Chapter 9's Hopf step). The framework has nothing that accounts for observed CDM. Three findings closed the identification, and each would have been enough on its own.

*The conjugate sector is not cold.* The symmetry that relates the two orientations fixes them as exact mirrors, with equal masses and coincident field values — a mirror, not a cooled copy. At equal temperature the other side recombines later than our own matter–radiation equality, so its matter is still supported by radiation pressure and oscillating acoustically throughout the epoch in which the dark-matter ratio is fixed. Its density contrast inside the sound horizon comes out at a few per cent of what a cold collisionless component would give, and the implied clustering amplitude at roughly a quarter. The mirror-matter literature allows mirror baryons to be all the dark matter only when the mirror sector is substantially colder than ours; here, by symmetry, it is exactly as warm.

*The radiation budget refuses the equal-temperature reading outright.* A conjugate sector in thermal contact adds to the count of relativistic species at a level the microwave background excludes by tens of standard deviations — a minimum of nearly four extra species, and on the framework's own fixed-point relation around forty, against a measured value slightly below three.

*And the coupling is blind to location, which is the κ derivation's own premise.* The shared curvature binding transmits the magnitude of the other side's gravitational wells, not their positions. The measured ratio is defined against a component that sits where the galaxies sit. A location-blind contribution is not that quantity, and this is not an auxiliary assumption the framework could drop — it is the property that made the bilateral coupling derivable in the first place.

The bind is structural, and the ledger states it as such rather than as a defect awaiting repair: the symmetry that gives κ its footing through equal masses is the same symmetry that forces the equal temperatures making the sector un-cold. On the framework's present state the two cannot be separated. Nor is there a replacement candidate inside the deposit — the singlet in the octonionic decomposition is already spoken for as the electroweak vacuum direction, the heavy right-handed states decay by design to make the baryon asymmetry, and the far-future fragmentation of Part 2's late de Sitter endpoint is at the wrong cosmic time by the entire history of the universe. External routes to stable right-handed dark matter need either an extra symmetry the framework does not have or a keV-scale lightness its own fourth-neutrino falsifier forbids. A heavier stable singlet outside the light three is not forbidden, and is named as a post-deposit construction lane rather than a pathway with progress to report — and even if it worked it would supply a *different* dark matter and leave κ's referent open regardless.

Two cautions belong with this entry. The withdrawn 5.50's resemblance to the ratio that literature targets is a numerical coincidence and the framework claims no map. And no comparison with the observed dark-matter density was performed at any stage of this assessment, because no candidate ever reached an abundance the framework derives — running the comparison first would have been the wrong way round.

What this costs, after two withdrawals rather than one: one of the three strictest predictions, the framework's only *passed* line at that tier, one of its two remaining observational channels — and then, three days later, the amplified number itself. **What it does not cost: κ₀ = 3/√(4π), the bilateral architecture, w = −1, the forced Majorana character (which rests on κ₀ < 1 and is untouched), or the closed S³.**

This ledger said, on 6 October, that the framework had a derived number and no demonstrated referent for it, and that both halves were true at once. **The first half did not survive the week.** It is left standing above, struck through in substance rather than deleted, because the sequence is the instructive part: the identification went when the lanes examined what the measurement measures, and the number went when they examined whether anything in the deposit performs the amplification. **Neither step was forced by a critic, and the second was reached only because the first had been stated honestly enough to look underneath.**

**The atmospheric neutrino mass ratio — overshooting by ~1.5×.** The framework's flux-line structure fixes the *shape* of the ratio between the atmospheric and solar mass scales, and with a single heavy scale M_R the solar leg is reproduced tightly while the atmospheric leg overshoots by roughly a factor of 1.5. The two legs anti-correlate in the relevant flux, so no single choice fits both — a genuine structural tension, presented as such. The fallible content is the (flux)^(2/3) shape itself; if the tension hardens as oscillation data sharpen, the flux-line reading of the mass sector fails at its own load-bearing joint.

**Dark energy — the naked prediction under pressure.** The framework predicts that the dark-energy equation of state is exactly w = −1, with no freedom to adjust it: the late universe's acceleration is geometric unloading toward a de Sitter endpoint, and that endpoint has one value. DESI's second data release, combined with the microwave background, prefers an evolving equation of state over a constant one at roughly 3.1 standard deviations. That figure is worth unpacking, because this ledger has quoted it more strongly than it deserves. **It is a comparison between two models on a joint likelihood, not a direct measurement of w, and not an exclusion of −1 by DESI alone** — DESI's baryon acoustic oscillations on their own are well described by a flat universe with a constant equation of state, and on the same joint data the Bayesian evidence modestly favours the constant. The supernova-based route softened too: a 2026 recalibration of the DES sample brought it from about 4.2σ to 3.2σ. The measurement side is itself moving, and so is our reading of it. **The tension is real and the framework has no answer to it; it is simply not as sharp as this ledger previously implied.** This is the framework's sharpest current disagreement with data, and Chapter 13's honesty applies here too: there is no internal adjustment available. Section C.5 explains why the framework's own void mechanism cannot be recruited to rescue it — and why the framework says so rather than quietly leaving the option open. The verdict window is 2027–2029.

**The solar mixing angle — a claim withdrawn under measurement.** Until 2026 the framework carried sin²θ₁₂ = 1/3 as a structural leading-order value for the solar mixing angle, presented as awaiting correction terms. JUNO's first dedicated measurement gives 0.3092 ± 0.0087, which excludes 1/3 at between 3.5 and 3.8 standard deviations. Pressed on provenance, the value proved thinner than its billing: it followed from assuming an even-handed democracy among the generations within the plane that the μ–τ reflection leaves untouched — a natural default in neutrino model-building, but not something the deposited structure derives. So the framework has not softened the claim; it has withdrawn it, and it appears again in C.3's withdrawn ledger. Solar mixing is now an *open* problem, and a 2026 investigation sharpened how open. It was thought to be gated on one piece of structural homework — the dictionary connecting the framework's geometric labels to the three generations. That dictionary turns out to be necessary but not sufficient. It fixes which slot the solar angle lives in; it does not fix the angle. The remaining freedom is a single number in the way the symmetry is broken, with the two candidate mixing patterns sitting at its two endpoints, and **the algebra does not choose between them**. The framework fixes the atmospheric angle and the magnitude of the CP phase, and does not determine the solar angle at all.

Losing a prediction cleanly is better than replacing it with a fitted one.

**Primordial lithium-7 — a factor of 1.58, improved but not resolved.** The framework's treatment moves the predicted ⁷Li/H abundance from the standard picture's factor-2.9 discrepancy to 2.53 × 10⁻¹⁰ against an observed (1.6 ± 0.3) × 10⁻¹⁰ — a residual factor of 1.58. **The baryon asymmetry's magnitude — order-one only.** The *existence* of the asymmetry is a zero-parameter consequence of the maximal CP phase, and the leptogenesis scale passes its cross-check. But the precise magnitude, η ≈ 6 × 10⁻¹⁰, the framework reproduces only at order-one structural consistency; a zero-parameter derivation of the number itself would require closing the heavy-scale derivation fully, and that has not been done. The asymmetry's *sign* is not fixed either — the phase's sign is free spectral data within our sector, correlated with the mirror's opposite sign but not pinned on its own.

One further honest entry, of a different kind: the **solar-mass branch fork**. The striking 0.993× match of the second-generation neutrino mass lands on the 8π branch of an open normalisation choice; the 4π branch fits the ratio between the oscillation scales instead, and no single choice currently fits both legs. Until the fork is closed from the geometry, the match is carried as branch-contingent — a caveat the framework states wherever the number appears.

## C.3 The Withdrawn Ledger

Under the framework's no-numerology discipline — no quantity may be assembled from measured inputs to match a measured output — three constructions previously carried in the framework's derived ledger were withdrawn: a characterisation formula for β_NTE, a formula for the reactor angle θ₁₃, and a formula for the Cabibbo angle. Each took observed masses plus an interpretive coefficient and reproduced a target observable — the signature of number-fitting, however pretty the coefficients. Their withdrawal removed nothing structural; each had already been flagged as not derived from first principles.

A third route appeared in October 2026, and it is the most instructive of the three: **κ's identification with the measured dark-matter ratio** (C.1). It was not withdrawn by discipline, because nothing in it was assembled from measured inputs — the derivation is as clean as it ever was. It was not withdrawn by data either, in the ordinary sense, because no measurement of κ came back wrong. It was withdrawn because the framework's own lanes established that the two numbers being compared were never measurements of the same quantity. A prediction can fail; so can a derivation; and, less often, so can the sentence joining a derivation to an observable while both survive. The ledger keeps that third shape visible, because a reader who only knows the first two will mis-read this one as a defeat for κ.

A fourth entry joined them in 2026, and it was withdrawn under a different pressure again: **sin²θ₁₂ = 1/3**, the leading-order solar mixing angle, excluded by JUNO's first measurement at 3.5–3.8σ (C.1). The first three were withdrawn by discipline, before any measurement forced the issue; the solar angle was withdrawn by data; κ's referent was withdrawn by a structural bind. All three routes matter, and the ledger keeps them distinguishable.

These are recorded here because a framework that quietly deletes its retractions has not made them. The discipline also runs in both directions, and in both directions on the *same* quantity: the spectral index was demoted from the chord (Chapter 12), the gauge-birth temperature was promoted to the derived tier when its derivation closed — and then demoted again to conditional in 2026 when the argument under it was found to rest on a truncation artefact (C.2). Grading that moves both ways, including backwards on something the framework had already claimed, is how the reader knows it is grading.

## C.4 Unexpected Neighbourhoods

Several of the framework's structural commitments connect it to areas of physics and mathematics traditionally unrelated to cosmology or neutrino physics. Each connection is a place where progress made by entirely different communities, for entirely different reasons, bears directly on NTE — and where NTE's structure makes commitments those communities could test.

**Holographic entanglement entropy.** The framework's boundary-only ontology must reproduce the Ryu–Takayanagi relation between entanglement and geometry, coefficient included. A structure developed for string-theoretic holography becomes a consistency requirement on a framework that never invokes strings.

**Bimetric gravity.** The mathematics of consistent two-metric theories — developed to explore massive gravity — is the consistency frame for the framework's two orientations. Results proved or broken in that programme transfer directly.

**The CPT-symmetric universe.** The closest published predecessor the framework has (Boyle, Finn, and Turok, 2018, with its dark-matter companion): a universe–antiuniverse pair from the bang, the matter–antimatter asymmetry sorted to opposite sides rather than annihilated, and dark matter stabilised by a Z₂ symmetry on the neutrino sector — architecture Chapter 14 credits in full. The live discriminators are the kind experiment can read. Their dark matter is a sterile right-handed neutrino of definite mass, with its abundance selected by the vacuum. The framework makes no corresponding claim either way: its bilateral geometry produces a gravitational footprint and fixes κ, but whether that footprint accounts for the observed dark matter is open (C.1), and the framework neither asserts nor excludes a particle account. The contrast with their programme is therefore no longer a contrast between a particle and a geometric ratio; it is a contrast between a candidate and an open question. Their cosmos is inflation-free with an essentially vanishing primordial tensor signal; the framework carries a conditional tensor ratio of about five parts in a thousand — within reach of the coming experiments, and a clean separation if it is seen. And their twin is genuinely time-reversed where the framework's two orientations share one forward time — a structural fork that the paired observables above are the empirical shadow of. Progress and constraints in their programme bear directly on this one, in both directions.

**Null-hypersurface quantisation.** The closest external relative on the emergent-time axis rather than the cosmological one: a programme in which time appears as a quantum effect on a null surface, and the area form has to be treated as a discrete operator (Ciambelli, Freidel and Leigh, 2024). The resemblance is real and the framework credits it as convergence rather than confirmation — that construction starts on a surface where the light-cone structure already exists, and yields a preferred clock downstream of the moment this framework is trying to describe. The two are stacked layers, not rivals. Which is the more interesting relationship: independent programmes arriving at overlapping structure from different directions is weak evidence that the structure is real, and it is the only kind of evidence available for a claim that makes no post-lock observable.

**The exceptional-Jordan and octonionic algebra programme.** The framework's particle content stands on algebra developed as pure mathematics a century ago and revived by a small community connecting division algebras to the Standard Model. Progress there — particularly the hypercharge verification — is progress here, and the framework credits the lineage explicitly.

**Spectral geometry.** The Connes–Chamseddine spectral action supplies the framework's instrument-and-chord machinery, and one of its established results (the vanishing fourth heat-kernel coefficient on the framework's substrate) is simultaneously the reason the cosmological-constant suppression is structural and the reason the spectral index left the chord. A theorem from noncommutative geometry doing double duty in cosmology.

**The μ–τ symmetry literature.** The maximal atmospheric angle and maximal CP phase arrive through a reflection symmetry with a long history in neutrino model-building; the framework's contribution is the geometric seat, and the credit is stated. The same literature is where any future solar-angle commitment would have to be argued, which is part of why the framework will not take one on loan.

**Charged-lepton flavour violation.** A conditional signature of a distinctive kind: if CLFV is ever observed at all, the framework fixes the amplitude ratio between channels — |A(μ→eγ)|²/|A(τ→eγ)|² = tan²θ₂₃ — even though the framework's own heavy scale makes observation unlikely. It is worth writing that ratio out, because the trigonometry disguises how sharp it is. The framework does not leave θ₂₃ free: it predicts exactly π/4, at which tan²θ₂₃ is exactly 1. So the claim is that the two channels come with *equal* amplitude, and any observed inequality between them falsifies either the maximal atmospheric angle or the structure that carries it into the flavour sector. Two facts keep that from being more than it is. The heavy scale makes observation almost certainly unreachable, so this is a prediction written for the record rather than one anyone expects to collect. And equality is less rare than it sounds: drawing the relevant mixing structures at random produces a ratio this close to one about one time in ten. That is substantial, and it is not overwhelming, and the framework says so rather than leaving the reader to supply their own odds. What the claim is finally worth is that the framework committed to a definite value of a ratio it was under no obligation to speak to at all — stated plainly rather than in disguise, because stating it costs nothing and a surprise observation would score it.

**A gauge-birth gravitational-wave background.** The locking transition should leave a stochastic background with a spectral shape distinct from both inflationary and phase-transition templates — a target for the gravitational-wave observatories of the 2030s and beyond, sitting at the intersection of the framework's pre-temporal structure and an observational programme built for entirely other purposes.

## C.5 The Experimental Decade

The instruments that will strengthen or falsify the framework, and what each one rules on:

**JUNO** (taking data now) — has already ruled once, against the framework: the solar mixing angle measurement that forced the withdrawal in C.1. Still pending, and sharper: the neutrino mass ordering. The framework requires normal; inverted falsifies outright.

**DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande** (late 2020s onward) — the CP phase (framework: maximal, |δ| = π/2, zero-parameter) and the atmospheric octant (framework: exactly π/4). Hard tests of both of the framework's strictest predictions — both, now that the third has left the tier (C.1).

**KamLAND-Zen, LEGEND and successors** — neutrinoless double beta decay in the few-meV effective-mass window that normal ordering implies. Observation confirms the unresolved ontology; a firm null across the window rules it out.

**CMB-S4** (early 2030s) — sharpens *n_s* and bounds *r* for whatever early-universe pathway the open origin sector eventually selects (C.2). This entry previously led with the dark-matter ratio at twice Planck's precision, as the decisive test of κ, and later with a conditional native (n_s, r) fuse; **both filings are withdrawn**. CMB-S4 will measure Ω_DM/Ω_b more precisely than anyone has, and the framework now has no stake in that answer either — two losses worth naming where the reader expected tests.

**LiteBIRD** — the tensor-to-scalar ratio from orbit, closing the same net from the other side for the open origin sector. A historical audit-trail value r ≈ 0.0053 once sat inside the detection window; it is not a framework prediction.

**DESI full survey** — the dark-energy equation of state, and the framework's hardest current test. Framework: w = −1 exactly, currently in tension with the DESI-plus-CMB combination — stated at its proper strength in C.1. One point deserves stating plainly, because the framework has an obvious escape route available and is refusing it. The framework's void mechanism — underdense regions expanding slightly faster, preferentially sampled by low-redshift measurements — was for a time read as possibly explaining the DESI result as an artefact. Forward-modelling in 2026 killed that reading. The bias makes low-redshift sources look *brighter*, and when a survey fitted with that bias is analysed the standard way, the inferred equation of state moves to the phantom side, today's value below −1 — the opposite quadrant from where DESI actually sits. Reproducing DESI's pattern would require low-redshift sources looking *fainter* by a few hundredths of a magnitude, which void-enhanced expansion cannot do. And the mechanism is a sampling effect on distance indicators, so it cannot shift the supernova-independent constraint at all. If a real void bias is present, correcting for it would *strengthen* any measured departure from −1, not weaken it. The same mechanism still does its original job on the Hubble tension, where the brighter direction is the correct one; it simply cannot do both, and the framework now says which. The mechanism's own test is separate and live: supernova residuals should be brighter along void-dominated sightlines, testable by crossing existing supernova catalogues with void maps. Verdict window on the equation of state: 2027–2029.

**Rubin Observatory and Euclid** — the transition in the dark-matter-fraction scatter at the neutrino free-streaming coherence mass, mapped across hundreds of millions of systems; and the void anatomy of the Hubble tension (framework: directional scatter of 2–4 km/s/Mpc correlated with line-of-sight density). Two honest qualifications on the transition. The free-streaming scale is standard neutrino physics rather than an NTE derivation — what the framework claimed was its combination with the M^(−1/2) scaling and the mean of 5.50 — and **the mean is withdrawn twice over**: its identification with the measured ratio in C.1, and then the amplifier that produced 5.50 at all (2026-10-09, with only κ₀ = 3/√(4π) deposited). So this is not a prediction about the measured dark-to-ordinary ratio, and there is no longer a framework value for the scatter to converge on. The scaling's own logic survives for whatever the conjugate footprint does contribute; the scatter's amplitude within a single coherence patch was already carried as open. And the threshold is not fixed: free-streaming scales inversely with the neutrino mass, so the transition sits at a cluster-scale mass of order 10¹⁴ suns for a standard reference neutrino mass and moves as that mass moves. A survey result on the transition has to be read against whichever neutrino mass turns out to be right.

**High-luminosity LHC** — the absences: no fourth generation, no W_R, with bounds rising. The WIMP direct-detection programme (XENON, LUX-ZEPLIN, PandaX) continues to find nothing, but the framework has withdrawn its stake in that result: with no account of the observed dark matter, it cannot assert that dark matter is not a particle (C.1, Chapter 15 §5). Two absences, not three.

**JWST and successors,** peripherally — early-structure statistics that stress or support the framework's closed-geometry growth history.

The decade's shape is worth stating plainly: by the mid-2030s, the mass ordering will be settled, the CP phase measured, the equation of state resolved, and the double-beta window largely explored — while CMB-S4 will sharpen early-universe bounds for an origin sector the framework has left open, and will **not** test a withdrawn κ = 5.50. The framework does not get to wait out its tests. Most of its ledger converts from "pending" to "passed" or "broken" within the working life of this book's first edition — and more than one entry has already converted while the book was being written.

## C.6 Reading This Ledger

The entries above are the framework's exposure, kept current deliberately. The tensions of C.1 may resolve — the atmospheric ratio may find its correction structure, the branch fork may close on the matching branch, the equation of state may return to −1 as the measurements sharpen — or they may harden into the failures that end the framework. The gaps of C.2 may close into theorems or into refutations. That is what it means for the working bet to be a bet.

There are now two worked examples of what this appendix is for, and they fail in different ways, which is the more useful lesson.

The observed cold dark matter is the newer one and the harder one. The framework had a derived amplified number, a measured number, and a sentence joining them; the sentence turned out to be false, and then the amplified number itself left the deposit for want of an action. Nothing was tuned, nothing was fitted, and no measurement of Planck's ratio came back "wrong" for the framework — the framework's own structural checks established that the amplified κ and Planck's ratio were never measurements of the same quantity, and the symmetry that had seemed to underwrite the identification turned out to be the same symmetry responsible for the mismatch. A reader is owed the plain version: the framework lost a passed prediction, an observational channel, and then the amplifier; it gained an open CDM sector; **κ₀ survived**.

The solar angle is the older example and the simpler one. It was carried as a leading-order structural value; a measurement arrived; the value was wrong; the framework checked what the claim had actually been resting on, found it thinner than advertised, and withdrew it rather than reaching for the nearest surviving number. The book you are holding says so in three places, and this is one of them.

What this appendix commits the framework to is narrower and firmer: that the ledger stays public, that losses are recorded at the volume of wins, that withdrawn claims stay withdrawn and visible, and that when the experiments of C.5 report, the scorecard of Chapter 15 gets rewritten to match the sky — whichever way the sky rules.
