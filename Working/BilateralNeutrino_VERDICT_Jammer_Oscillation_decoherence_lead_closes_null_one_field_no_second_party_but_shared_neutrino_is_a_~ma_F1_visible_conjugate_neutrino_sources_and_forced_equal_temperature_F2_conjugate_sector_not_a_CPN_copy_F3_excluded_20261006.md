## 1. What the corpus says

- One object, both sides. Companion 5 §6: the unresolved neutrino "has no geometric basis for distinguishing the two sides of the Janus boundary — its stress-energy sources curvature on both sides and both sides' curvature acts back on it." Part 1 §III: "bilateral support across both CPN-conjugate orientations."
- Standard propagation. Companion 7 §6: the neutrino "has a wavefunction and evolves unitarily"; §3 gives the standard phase Δm² L / 2E.
- Exactly three light states. Companion 7 §7: a fourth light Majorana neutrino "falsifies the framework."
- Gravity only across sectors. Companion 5 §6: "We cannot see conjugate-sector matter electromagnetically … or through the weak force; we detect only its gravitational footprint." Part 2 §IX: coupling "purely gravitational/geometric."
- The conjugate sector has its own weak force. Part 2 §8.5: "CPN combined with parity assigns SU(2)_R to the mirror sector."
- Symmetric content. Companion 5 §6: "M_A = M_B = M by CPN symmetry", used in the κ closure.

## 2. The decoherence lead: null

- **No second party.** My W-state analogy needed something on the far side to lose. With one field and three states there is no "which side" degree of freedom: an odd partner would be a fourth light state, which Companion 7 excludes. A single mode supported on both sides is one party.
- **No measurable medium effect.** If the neutrino scatters weakly on conjugate matter, the forward-scattering potential at the local dark-matter density (0.4 GeV/cm³) is about 5 × 10⁻³⁸ eV. The oscillation scale Δm²₂₁/2E is about 4 × 10⁻¹⁷ eV even at 1 TeV. Twenty orders of magnitude short.
- **Landing:** oscillation damping is not a prediction of NTE. Existing decoherence limits neither help nor hurt.

## 3. What replaces it: the cross-sector weak channel

The conjugate sector's weak force needs a neutral lepton. The corpus has exactly one neutrino and says it does not distinguish the sides. Three ways out:

**F1 — the shared neutrino couples weakly to both sectors.** This is what CPN symmetry plus "no basis for distinguishing the sides" implies. Chirality does not block it: for a Majorana neutrino the right-chiral component that SU(2)_R would use is the conjugate of our left-chiral field, and creates and absorbs the same two helicity states we detect as neutrino and antineutrino. Consequences:
- (a) **We would see the conjugate sector through neutrinos.** Conjugate-sector stars and supernovae would emit neutrinos our detectors can absorb. Dark matter is 5.5 times baryons; if it forms stars as ours does, there should be neutrino bursts with no optical counterpart and a diffuse supernova neutrino background several times the standard expectation. Detectors have watched for Galactic bursts for roughly four decades and seen only SN 1987A, and the diffuse-background limits are, as far as I know, within a small factor of the standard prediction. Unfetched; could already exclude F1 with star formation, or could be a live prediction.
- (b) **The two sectors are in thermal contact** down to about 1 MeV, so they share a temperature. The usual escape in the mirror-matter literature, a colder mirror sector, is unavailable. Conjugate photons then double the radiation, and conjugate matter is coupled to its own photons until its own recombination, so it does not behave as cold collisionless dark matter at the CMB epoch. As far as I know, mirror matter can be all of the dark matter only if its temperature is below roughly 0.3 of ours. **This bears directly on κ.** It sharpens the "radiation footprint" gap I flagged on 2026-09-26.
- (c) "Detected only by its gravitational footprint" is false as written.

**F2 — the neutrino couples weakly to our sector only.** Then the conjugate sector's SU(2)_R has no neutral lepton to couple to. Its beta decay, its nucleosynthesis and its stellar burning differ from ours, or do not happen. The conjugate sector is then not a CPN copy in its particle physics, and "M_A = M_B by CPN symmetry" needs re-justifying. It also contradicts "no geometric basis for distinguishing the two sides."

**F3 — each sector has its own neutrino, combined into even and odd states.** That is the standard mirror-neutrino picture with maximal ordinary–mirror mixing. Excluded by Companion 7's own fourth-neutrino falsifier and, as far as I know, by solar neutral-current data.

**The corpus does not say which.** In the files read, I found no statement of whether conjugate-sector weak interactions couple to the neutrino (scope in §0).
