## A calculation done before reading (back-of-envelope, mine, unreviewed)

When does the trigger "one photon per Hubble volume" fire?

- Inputs: T_CMB today 2.725 K = 2.35×10⁻⁴ eV. H₀ = 67.6 km/s/Mpc gives ħH₀ = 1.44×10⁻³³ eV. With Ω_Λ ≈ 0.69, ħH_dS ≈ 1.2×10⁻³³ eV.
- Part 1 §V: T_reset = 1.60 ħH_dS ≈ 1.9×10⁻³³ eV.
- Photon temperature falls as 1/a. Ratio today/reset ≈ 1.2×10²⁹, so the trigger fires about **ln(1.2×10²⁹) ≈ 67 e-folds from now**, roughly **10¹² years** at a de Sitter Hubble time of 17 Gyr.
- Cross-check by counting: today about 4×10⁸ photons per m³ in a Hubble cube of about 4×10⁷⁸ m³, times e⁻²⁰¹, gives order one. Consistent.

What else is in a Hubble volume at that moment, on the same dilution:
- **Relic neutrinos:** number density today is comparable to the photons' (about 3×10⁸ per m³). So about **one massive neutrino per Hubble volume** as well.
- **Baryons:** about 10⁻⁹ per Hubble volume on average, and not uniformly spread: bound structures do not dilute.
- For comparison, the Gibbons–Hawking temperature is H/2π ≈ 0.16 H, a tenth of T_reset; the trigger is reached before the horizon's own thermal floor.
