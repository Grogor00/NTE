# Paper II source check — the rank obstruction is already published

**Lane:** Cursor Main · **Date:** 2026-10-26 · **Under:** `Handoffs\Directive_AC_The_No_Go_Papers_Three_Papers_Scoped_Standards_Set_Axiom_Reexamination_Deferred_20261026.md`
**Rule used:** every quotation below was taken from a page fetched this session. A search snippet that was not fetched is marked unverified and is not a citation.

**Register:** the claim "G₂ does not contain SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1)" is **established in the literature**. Our 2026-10-16 centraliser computation **confirms** it. It is not a new no-go.

## Fetched

**Todorov and Dubois-Violette, arXiv:1806.09450**, HTML fetched from `https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1806.09450`.

> We first note that the intersection of the maximal connected subgroups of \(G_2\) is \(G_{G_2}=SU(3)\cap\frac{SU(2)\times SU(2)}{\mathbb{Z}_2}=U(2)\) (4.2) — that is, the gauge group for the Weinberg-Salam model. This however destroys our rational for introducing the octonions: the unbroken \(SU(3)\) color symmetry. … A maximal rank subgroup of an exceptional Lie group will be called admissible if it contains the color \(SU(3)\) subgroup. Thus the only admissible subgroup of \(G_2\) is \(SU(3)(=SU(3)_c)\) itself.

They then take the full Standard Model group from an intersection inside \(F_4\), not inside \(G_2\). Equation (1.4) in the same fetch: \(G_{F_4}=S(U(2)\times U(3))=(SU(2)\times SU(3)\times U(1))/\mathbb{Z}_6\).

**Carone and Rastogi, arXiv:0712.1011**, HTML fetched from `https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0712.1011`.

> The only groups of rank \(2\) that contain an SU(3) subgroup are the groups G2 and SU(3) itself.

The model they then build is \(G_2\times SU(2)\times U(1)\): the electroweak factors sit beside \(G_2\), not inside it.

**Masi, Scientific Reports 11, 22528 (2021)**, HTML fetched from `https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-01814-1`.

> The Standard Model gauge group SU(3) is not isomorphic to the group of automorphisms of the octonions, which is G(2). Nonetheless, it is possible to fix one of the octonion basis elements to obtain seven possible subalgebras, each of which has a subgroup of automorphisms isomorphic to SU(3). For example, SU(3) itself may be defined as the subgroup of G(2) which leaves the octonionic unit \(e_7\) invariant.

**Furey, arXiv:1910.08395**, HTML fetched from `https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1910.08395`.

> The derivations of \(\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{O}\) are given by the complexified 14-dimensional exceptional Lie algebra \(g_2\).

The same paper's construction of three generations under \(su(3)\oplus u(1)\) is inside the 64-complex-dimensional space generated by left actions, identified there as \(\mathbb{C}l(6)\), not as a subgroup of \(G_2\).

**Baez, Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 39 (2002) 145–205, arXiv:math/0105155**, HTML fetched from `https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/math/0105155`.

Fetched statements actually present in that HTML: \({\mathfrak{g}}_2\) is 14-dimensional; \(G_2\) is the automorphism group described by its action on basic triples, and an automorphism may send a chosen imaginary unit to any point of the 6-sphere of unit imaginary octonions. **This fetch does not contain the sentence "the stabiliser is SU(3)".** That sentence is not cited to Baez.

**Günaydin and Gürsey, J. Math. Phys. 14 (1973) 1651.** The AIP landing-page abstract was returned by search. The article body was not fetched (paywalled). Abstract as returned: the groups SO(8), SO(7), and \(G_2\) are represented by octonions, and "reduction is made through the physically important subgroups SU(3) and SU(2)⊗SU(2) of \(G_2\)." **Body not cited.**

## What is ours

The 2026-10-16 lane computation: centraliser of \(\mathrm{Stab}(e_1)\) in \(\mathfrak{g}_2\) is 0, and neither SO(4) factor commutes with that stabiliser. That is a numerical confirmation of the Todorov–Dubois-Violette statement that the only colour-admissible maximal subgroup of \(G_2\) is \(SU(3)\) itself. It does not add a constraint the 2018 paper does not already state.
