## What Furey–Hughes actually do (source-verified, with quotes)
- **Their "complex structure" is an internal operator:** "We define here a *complex structure* to be a real linear map J on a real vector space for which J² = −1." The specific ones (L_{e₇}, R_{e₇}, L_{ε₃}, R_{ε₃}, L_i=R_i) act on Ψ ∈ 𝔸 and are used to select the commuting subalgebra that drives the breaking chain Spin(10) → Pati-Salam → Left-Right → SM+B-L. They note the focus on complex structures is "due to the succinctness of their presentation" — a presentational device.
- **They start from an already-complex algebra:** 𝔸 := ℝ⊗ℂ⊗ℍ⊗𝕆; since ℝ⊗ℂ = ℂ, the complex field is a tensor factor from the start, and the projectors use the unit directly: P_L := ½(1 + i L_{e₇}). Conclusion restates 𝔸 = ℂ⊗ℍ⊗𝕆.
- **They do NOT derive the quantum complex field.** ℂ is an input. Whether the complex structures could be "chosen by 𝔸 itself" is posed as an **open question, not answered.** No reference to operational/probabilistic reconstruction (Hardy, CDP, Masanes–Müller) or local tomography anywhere in the body/references shown.

## Answer: YES — by a level-distinction, not by contesting their result
Furey–Hughes's "𝕆 → ℍ → ℂ" is a **descent of internal complex structures** (J²=−1 symmetry-breaking operators) **within an algebra that already contains ℂ**. It is **not** a derivation of the operational / Hilbert-space complex field — the i of quantum theory — which the ℂ-selection obstruction concerns. That field they take as given (𝔸 ⊃ ℂ; i in the projectors) and explicitly flag as an open question.

So their work does **not** go "the opposite direction to our obstruction" in the sense that would defeat it. It sits **downstream** of the assumed ℂ. The obstruction answers it precisely: the division-algebra programme's "complex structures" are internal gauge-organizing maps presupposing the complex field; the operational ℂ — the one reconstruction selects by local tomography — remains unsupplied, and the bridge to the gauge group remains blocked (Jordan route excluded; this complex-algebra route presupposes ℂ, §2 of the draft).

**Adversarial self-check (I have a stake — this is "my" gap/paper).** If Furey–Hughes derived the QM complex field from a strictly real/non-complex base, the obstruction would fail. They do not: ℂ ∈ 𝔸 from the outset, i is used in the projectors, and they themselves leave "could ℂ be chosen by 𝔸 itself?" open. So the YES is grounded in their text, not in wanting the paper to survive.

**Bonus for Paper II's novel section:** Furey–Hughes's own open question — whether the complex structures could be selected by the algebra itself — is direct textual acknowledgement, from a leading practitioner of the division-algebra programme, that the ℂ-selection gap is real and unfilled. That is the strongest possible support for the novel section being a genuine open problem, not a strawman.
