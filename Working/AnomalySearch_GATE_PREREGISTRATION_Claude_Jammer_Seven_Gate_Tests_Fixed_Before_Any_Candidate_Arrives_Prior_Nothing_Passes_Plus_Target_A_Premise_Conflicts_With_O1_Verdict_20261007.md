## The seven tests

A candidate must clear every one. Each failure is reported with which test and why.

1. **Independence.** A citation, by authors with no knowledge of NTE, that calls the thing anomalous. The citation is reported, not our judgement.
2. **Current status.** The most recent literature assessment, not the discovery paper. Significance with band width, and the *global* figure after look-elsewhere correction where the literature gives one. An anomaly that has since been resolved or has faded is not a candidate.
3. **Selection accounting.** The search must report how many anomalies were surveyed and how many showed contact. One loose contact out of a dozen surveyed is what chance gives. The neutrino sector was chosen *because* NTE calls it special; that is a mild prediction-first selection and counts against, not for.
4. **Deposited structure only.** The contact must follow from text in Core at current state, quoted. It may not use anything withdrawn or found defective: κ as CDM, the amplifier, z → −z as the CPN map (O2 landed C-3 per its title; unread by me), the hand-imposed self-consistency constraint.
5. **Sign and size first.** What NTE's structure implies for the anomaly — direction and magnitude — is derived and written down before it is compared with the measurement. No tunable strength. No f.
6. **It could have failed.** State what observation would have counted against the contact. If every outcome would have been "consistent", the contact carries no information. (This is the W-state lesson from earlier this month: consistent, not informative.)
7. **No forbidden content.** No fourth light neutrino. Nothing that endangers the forced-Majorana channel.

**For an A-2 candidate (a mathematical structure):** it must supply at least one of the three items O1 names as missing — an interaction potential replacing the hand constraint; matter coupling without a second kinetic term; a well-posed constraint structure off g⁺ = g⁻. A structure that merely also has two sheets is an analogy, not a find.
