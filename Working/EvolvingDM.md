# The 2026 Observational Status of Evolving Dark Energy: How Hard Is w = −1 Squeezed?

## TL;DR
- As of mid-2026, the combination of DESI DR2 BAO + CMB + supernovae prefers an evolving (CPL) dark-energy equation of state over a cosmological constant at **2.8σ–4.2σ** depending on the supernova sample; DESI+CMB alone (no SNe) gives **3.1σ**. A theory committed to w = −1 exactly is **in mild-to-moderate tension but not falsified** — the signal sits below the 5σ discovery threshold and its strength depends heavily on contested supernova calibrations.
- The best fit consistently lands at **w0 > −1, wa < 0** (a "quintom-B" / thawing-like track that was phantom in the past and crosses w = −1 near z ≈ 0.4–0.5), but the single biggest supernova-driven pillar has weakened in 2026: the DES team's own recalibration ("DES-Dovekie") dropped the DESY5 significance from 4.2σ to **3.2σ**.
- The community treats this as an **intriguing but unconfirmed hint, not a discovery.** The verdict will be settled by the full DESI 5-year dataset, Euclid (first cosmology results now expected ~mid-2027), Rubin/LSST (which began the survey on June 30, 2026), and Roman — with the first decisive independent checks expected in 2027.

## Key Findings

**1. The DESI signal has held steady and modestly strengthened from DR1 to DR2, but has not crossed into discovery territory.** DESI DR1 (2024) preferred evolving dark energy at 2.6σ (DESI+CMB), 2.5σ (+Pantheon+), 3.5σ (+Union3), and 3.9σ (+DESY5). DESI DR2 (March 2025, more than 14 million galaxies/quasars, three years of data) tightened these to 3.1σ (DESI+CMB) and 2.8σ / 3.8σ / 4.2σ for the three SN compilations. Roughly 0.3σ was added to each combination between DR1 and DR2 — the preference "does not diminish" with more data, but it also did not leap forward.

**2. The cosmological-constant point (w0, wa) = (−1, 0) sits ~2–4σ from the best fit,** in the quadrant w0 > −1, wa < 0. The exact DESI DR2 best-fit values (Abdul-Karim et al. 2025, Phys. Rev. D 112, 083515) are:
- **DESI+CMB:** w0 = −0.42 ± 0.21, wa = −1.75 ± 0.58 (3.1σ)
- **DESI+CMB+Pantheon+:** w0 = −0.838 ± 0.055, wa = −0.62 (+0.22/−0.19) (2.8σ)
- **DESI+CMB+Union3:** w0 = −0.667 ± 0.088, wa = −1.09 (+0.31/−0.27) (3.8σ)
- **DESI+CMB+DESY5:** w0 = −0.752 ± 0.057, wa = −0.86 (+0.23/−0.20) (4.2σ)

**3. The robustness of the signal is genuinely contested, and the strongest single criticism — supernova systematics — has been partially vindicated in 2026.** The DES collaboration's own recalibration reduced its supernova sample's ΛCDM-exclusion from 4.2σ to 3.2σ.

**4. Independent probes have not yet confirmed the signal, and the decisive tests are still ~1–2 years out.** DES Y6 weak lensing (Jan 2026) is consistent with ΛCDM in its own right; Euclid, Rubin/LSST, Roman, and the full DESI survey are the key forthcoming discriminators.

## Details

### 1. DESI results and their evolution

The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument fits the CPL (Chevallier–Polarski–Linder) parametrization w(a) = w0 + wa(1−a), where ΛCDM is the special case w0 = −1, wa = 0. DESI DR2 measures BAO as a "standard ruler" from more than 14 million galaxies and quasars over three years of operation, combined with Lyman-α forest BAO.

The core DR2 result (Abdul-Karim et al. 2025, arXiv:2503.14738, Phys. Rev. D 112, 083515): DESI BAO data are well described by flat ΛCDM, but the DESI-preferred parameters are in mild 2.3σ tension with Planck CMB (though consistent with the well-measured acoustic angular scale θ*). Allowing w0, wa to vary alleviates this tension, with the favored solution in the w0 > −1, wa < 0 quadrant. In the paper's own words: *"This solution is preferred over ΛCDM at 3.1σ for the combination of DESI BAO and CMB data... Unless there is an unknown systematic error associated with one or more datasets, it is clear that ΛCDM is being challenged."* Adding SNe raises the preference to 2.8σ–4.2σ.

The exact best-fit values are listed in Key Findings #2. Note how the DESI+CMB-only fit (w0 = −0.42, wa = −1.75) is far from ΛCDM in raw parameter values but only 3.1σ significant because its error bars are large; adding SNe pulls w0 toward −1 but shrinks the errors, keeping or increasing the significance.

**Full-shape and newer analyses:** DESI DR1 full-shape (2024) gave 2.5σ / 3.4σ / 3.8σ for Pantheon+/Union3/DESY5. Analyses adding DR2 BAO to ACT DR6 CMB (rather than Planck) find similar values: 2.9σ (DESI+ACT), 4.2σ (+DESY5) — indicating the result is not solely a Planck artifact. A December 2025 analysis combining Planck+DESI DR2 BAO+DESI Y1 full-shape+SNe found the constraints tighten slightly and continue "running away from ΛCDM." As of mid-2026 there is **no DR3/full-survey cosmology paper**: DESI completed its planned five-year sky survey in April 2026 (finishing early), and a July 2026 DR2 Lyman-α key paper refined those constraints, but the full-survey dark-energy cosmology results have not yet been released.

### 2. What the best fit actually says: the phantom-divide crossing

The DESI-preferred region (w0 > −1, wa < 0) describes dark energy that was **phantom** (w < −1) in the past and is **quintessence-like** (w > −1) today, crossing the "phantom divide" w = −1 near z ≈ 0.4–0.5. This is the "quintom-B" scenario.

This is physically significant because a **single, minimally-coupled, canonical scalar field cannot cross w = −1** — doing so requires either two fields (quintom models, e.g., one canonical + one phantom), non-canonical kinetic terms (higher powers of the kinetic energy), a coupling between dark energy and dark matter (which can produce an *effective* crossing without a true one), or modified gravity. The null energy condition (w ≥ −1) is satisfied by all known forms of matter and saturated only by a cosmological constant, so a genuine phantom phase would itself be exotic.

Several groups quantify the crossing significance at 96.2%–99.97% (≈2–3.5σ) depending on the SN sample. Critically, some analyses show the "crossing" may be an artifact of the CPL parametrization or a superposition of components rather than a true single-field crossing: Gómez-Valent & González-Fuentes (2025) show the z ≈ 0.4–0.5 peak in dark-energy density can be reproduced by "negative quintessence" without a real phantom crossing, at 3.27σ preference over ΛCDM (comparable to CPL). A Monte Carlo test found that a ~2σ phantom-crossing signal arises by statistical fluctuation and model flexibility in 3.2% of mock realizations drawn from a non-phantom fiducial.

### 3. Robustness and criticism

This is where the case is weakest and most actively debated.

**(a) Supernova calibration systematics.** The three SN compilations disagree in what they imply, and the highest-significance one (DESY5) is the most questioned:
- **Efstathiou (2024/2025)** compared the ~20% of SNe common to Pantheon+ and DES-SN5YR and reported evidence for a ∼0.04 mag offset between the low- and high-redshift distance measurements of that overlapping subsample. If this offset is subtracted from the whole DES-SN5YR sample, the evolving-dark-energy preference largely vanishes.
- **DES/Vincenzi et al. (2025, arXiv:2501.06664, MNRAS 541:2585)** responded that the offset is expected and mostly due to different Malmquist-bias corrections, host-galaxy modelling, and light-curve fitting improvements between the two samples — finding "43% of the offset is due to DES-SN5YR improvements in the modelling of supernova intrinsic scatter and host galaxy properties," whose uncertainties are already captured in the DES-SN5YR error budget. In their reanalysis the DES-SN5YR evidence for evolving dark energy is "only reduced from 3.9σ to 3.3σ" — i.e., not eliminated. They maintain the result is robust.
- **The decisive 2026 development — DES-Dovekie:** The DES team itself recalibrated the DESY5 sample (improved photometric cross-calibration, DA white-dwarf calibration, retrained SALT3, fixed a host-galaxy color-law approximation). The recalibrated "DES-Dovekie" sample (Popovic et al., arXiv:2511.07517, MNRAS stag632), combined with CMB (Planck+ACT+SPT) + DESI DR2, gives w0 = −0.803 ± 0.054, wa = −0.72 ± 0.21 and reduces the ΛCDM-exclusion from **4.2σ to 3.2σ**; the authors state their "updated data exhibits only a weak preference for evolving dark energy" (~5:1 Bayesian odds). Recent reanalyses of Pantheon+ and Union3 (Union3.1) similarly land near 3.2σ and 3.4σ. Independent work (Notari et al. 2025; the "biased by low-redshift supernovae" paper, arXiv:2502.04212) found that removing the low-z SNe or debiasing the low-z offset can drop the significance from ~3.5–3.7σ to ~1.6σ.

**(b) Redshift-bin / tracer dependence.** In DR1, the LRG1 (z_eff = 0.51) and LRG2 (z_eff = 0.71) BAO points were flagged as anomalous — LRG1 differed from BOSS DR12 at ~1.6σ and LRG2 at ~2.2σ, and Colgáin et al. and Wang et al. showed these two points disproportionately drive the DDE preference. In DR2, the influential feature moved slightly to higher redshift and tightened; DESI DR2-only analyses find the evolving-DE preference is "primarily driven by the LRG1-2 tracers." So the concern persisted in modified form rather than disappearing — a small number of intermediate-z BAO points still carry outsized weight.

**(c) CMB prior / sound-horizon calibration.** BAO measures ratios like DH/rd and is nearly "scale-free" until the sound horizon rd is anchored by external CMB (or BBN) data. The CMB's main role is to fix Ωm and rd, breaking the w0-wa degeneracy. Analyses using compressed CMB likelihoods, ACT DR6, or Planck PR4 find broadly similar significances, suggesting the result is not purely a Planck artifact — though some argue Planck's "excess smoothing" (the lensing anomaly) contributes to the pull away from ΛCDM.

**(d) Neutrino-mass degeneracy.** There is a deep degeneracy between the dark-energy EoS and the neutrino mass sum Σmν, both acting on the late-time expansion and growth. Within ΛCDM, DESI+CMB pushes Σmν to implausibly low or **effectively negative** values (the DR2 paper reports Σmν < 0.064 eV at 95% assuming ΛCDM, in tension with the ~0.059 eV oscillation floor). Allowing w0waCDM relaxes the bound (Σmν < 0.16 eV in the w0wa model) and can restore positive neutrino masses — but crucially, the preference for evolving DE and the preference for negative effective neutrino mass are two faces of the *same* enhanced-clustering / expansion mismatch (Elbers et al., "Negative neutrino masses as a mirage of dark energy"). Some argue this is evidence the anomaly is a systematic rather than genuinely dark-energy in origin.

**(e) Parametrization dependence (CPL).** Nesseris, Akrami & Starkman (2025, "To CPL, or not to CPL?", arXiv:2503.22529) argue that truncating the w(a) Taylor expansion at first order (CPL) injects information not in the data; marginalizing over higher-order terms shows we know neither w0 nor wa well enough to exclude a constant w at several σ. Cortês & Liddle and others make related points about the CPL prior volume. Conversely, DESI notes that CPL matches exact dark-energy background evolution to O(0.1%) for viable models, and other two-parameter forms (Barboza–Alcaniz, exponential, logarithmic) give similar or slightly better fits — so the *qualitative* preference is not unique to CPL even if the *quantitative* significance is parametrization-sensitive.

**(f) Model-independent / non-parametric reconstructions.** These broadly *confirm* a redshift-varying w(z) but at more modest significance. The DESI extended-DE paper (Lodha et al. 2025) using flexknot and Horndeski-motivated correlation-prior reconstructions finds w(z) varies with redshift and that the DR2 preference "does not diminish relative to DR1"; their non-parametric reconstruction reaches >3σ support for dynamical DE once SNe are included, though DESI-DR2-only leaves ΛCDM viable at ~1.5σ. Crossing-statistics reconstructions (DR1) put ΛCDM outside the 95% interval over some redshift ranges. However, symbolic-regression and Gaussian-process reconstructions with honest error propagation (e.g., arXiv:2502.10506) conclude that claiming any particular model is "premature" given the large uncertainties, and that two-parameter models already capture all the trends (no evidence for extra complexity).

**Statistical-combination critique.** Efstathiou (2025, "On DESI's DR2 exclusion of ΛCDM," MNRASL 544:L121) argues the 2.8/3.8/4.2σ values cannot be averaged or cherry-picked because the datasets overlap almost completely; a principled combination yields ~3.1σ, and the most secure inference is the 3.1σ from DESI+CMB alone. He also notes DESY5's SNe alone actually pull DESI+CMB *toward* ΛCDM, not away.

### 4. Independent and forthcoming data

- **DES Year 6 (Jan 2026):** The full six-year DES 3×2pt (cosmic shear + galaxy clustering + galaxy-galaxy lensing) over ~5000 deg² yields, in ΛCDM, S8 = 0.789 ± 0.012, Ωm = 0.333 ± 0.023–0.028, consistent with ΛCDM and ~1.8σ from CMB (2.6σ in S8 only). In wCDM it gives w = −1.12 (+0.26/−0.20) — consistent with a constant w = −1. DES Y6 3×2pt does not independently confirm evolving DE; its constraining power on w0-wa is weak on its own.
- **eBOSS / SDSS:** Earlier BAO (BOSS/eBOSS) alone gave much weaker DDE hints (~1.7σ with Pantheon+); replacing DESI's anomalous LRG points with BOSS values weakens the DESI signal, which is part of the tracer-dependence concern.
- **Euclid:** Launched July 2023, science survey began Feb 2024. Quick Release 1 (March 2025) was legacy/non-cosmological science. ESA/NASA messaging pointed to first cosmology data around late 2026, but the revised timeline (as of June 2026) indicates full DR1 with the LE3 higher-level products needed for weak-lensing cosmology will not arrive before **mid-2027**. Euclid weak lensing + BAO should reach sub-percent w0/wa precision over its mission and is a primary independent discriminator.
- **Rubin/LSST:** The NSF–DOE Vera C. Rubin Observatory officially began the Legacy Survey of Space and Time on **June 30, 2026**. A June 2026 commissioning analysis (Léget, arXiv:2606.09938) states that "after a full year of data from LSST, these measurements are expected to reach precision comparable to recent Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI), providing an independent test of hints that Dark Energy may evolve over time." Over ten years LSST will measure hundreds of thousands of SNe Ia.
- **Roman Space Telescope:** A dedicated high-z SN Ia and BAO/weak-lensing dark-energy mission; its launch has reportedly been moved up. It will provide the cleanest high-z SN Hubble diagram, directly addressing the low-z/high-z calibration concern.
- **DESI full 5-year data:** The survey finished in April 2026; the full-dataset BAO cosmology (roughly doubling DR2 statistics again toward the planned ~40 million spectra) is the most immediate high-impact test and should sharpen the ~3σ signal one way or the other.

**Timeline for resolution:** The first genuinely independent high-precision checks (Rubin Y1 weak lensing, Euclid cosmology, DESI full-survey BAO) land in 2027; a definitive 5σ-level statement — or a collapse of the signal — is plausible on a 2027–2029 horizon.

### 5. Verdict for w = −1 exactly

**A theory committed to w = −1 exactly (ΛCDM) is currently in mild-to-moderate tension with the combined data, but is NOT falsified and remains viable.** Quantitatively:
- The most conservative, SN-independent measure — DESI DR2 BAO + CMB — disfavors w = −1 (w0 = −1, wa = 0) at **3.1σ**. This is the number most cosmologists regard as the "cleanest."
- Adding SNe raises this to 2.8σ (Pantheon+), 3.8σ (Union3), 4.2σ (DESY5), but these are not independent and cannot be stacked; a principled combination gives ~3.1σ.
- The single strongest SN pillar (DESY5) was reduced by the DES team's own 2026 recalibration to **3.2σ** (DES-Dovekie), and multiple reanalyses converge near 3.2–3.4σ.
- 3σ is "evidence," not "discovery" (5σ). ΛCDM is a nested special case that has *not* been excluded at high confidence, and several plausible systematic explanations (SN calibration, LRG BAO points, neutrino-mass degeneracy, CPL prior) each individually can reduce the signal to ~1.5–2σ.

**A useful nuance for a w = −1 theory:** the tension is really with the *past* (phantom) phase, not the present. All three SN combinations give w0 > −1 today, and at the SN pivot redshifts (z ≈ 0.26–0.33) w is close to −1. If your theory is a canonical scalar field that forbids w < −1, you are actually aligned with the present-day value and only in tension with the inferred historical phantom excursion — which is precisely the part most vulnerable to being a parametrization/effective artifact.

**Community consensus (mid-2026):** Evolving dark energy is treated as a genuinely exciting, persistent, but **unconfirmed hint** — not an established discovery. The DESI collaboration's own framing is that "ΛCDM is being challenged... unless there is an unknown systematic error." The prevailing posture is cautious optimism paired with active scrutiny of systematics, awaiting independent confirmation.

## Recommendations

**For a physicist whose theory predicts w = −1 exactly:**

1. **Do not abandon or heavily hedge the theory now.** At ~3σ (and with the strongest SN input having just weakened to 3.2σ), w = −1 is disfavored but fully alive. This is precisely the significance range where many historical "hints" have evaporated. Frame the theory as consistent with the most robust, SN-independent constraint: DESI+CMB's 3.1σ is a <99.9% exclusion — not decisive.

2. **In publications, cite the SN-independent 3.1σ (DESI+CMB) as the fair benchmark** rather than the headline 4.2σ, and explicitly note the DES-Dovekie recalibration (4.2σ → 3.2σ), the Vincenzi et al. reduction (3.9σ → 3.3σ), and the parametrization-dependence critique (Nesseris et al.). Emphasize that non-parametric reconstructions leave ΛCDM inside or near the allowed bands over much of the redshift range.

3. **Stress-test against the phantom-divide point specifically.** If your theory forbids w < −1, argue that the apparent phantom crossing may be an effective/parametrization artifact (Gómez-Valent & González-Fuentes; the 3.2% fluctuation result) rather than physical — and that you match the well-measured present-day w0 > −1.

**Benchmarks that should change your assessment:**
- **Abandon confidence in w = −1 if:** the DESI full 5-year BAO + CMB (SN-independent) rises to ≳4σ, OR Euclid/Rubin weak lensing *independently* find w0 > −1, wa < 0 at ≳3σ. Two independent probes each at 3σ in the same direction would be close to decisive.
- **Grow more confident in w = −1 if:** DESI full-survey BAO+CMB stays flat or drops below 3σ; OR Roman/Rubin SN Hubble diagrams with homogeneous low-z anchoring (DEBASS-style, same-telescope low-z + high-z) eliminate the low-z/high-z offset and the SN-driven significance collapses; OR Euclid finds w consistent with −1.
- **Decisive window:** 2027–2029, when Rubin Y1, Euclid cosmology, and DESI full-survey results all mature.

## Caveats

- **Significances are not additive and overlap heavily.** The 2.8/3.8/4.2σ values share the same DESI and CMB data and largely overlapping SNe (Pantheon+ and Union3 share ~1360 SNe); quoting the maximum (4.2σ) overstates the evidence. ~3.1σ is the defensible combined figure.
- **The signal's strength is a moving target and SN-sample-dependent.** It has drifted between ~2.5σ and ~4.2σ across releases and samples; the 2026 recalibrations pushed the SN-based numbers down, not up.
- **"Evolving dark energy" ≠ "phantom crossing confirmed."** The w0waCDM fit implies a crossing, but the crossing itself may be a CPL/parametrization artifact or a multi-component effect; a true single-field phantom phase is not established.
- **Degeneracies muddy interpretation.** The same data feature manifests as either evolving DE or negative effective neutrino mass; disentangling requires the forthcoming growth-of-structure probes.
- **Some sources are speculative or forward-looking.** Rubin/Euclid "will nail it" statements and precision forecasts are projections, not results; Euclid and Roman timelines have slipped before and may slip again (Euclid DR1 cosmology now looks like mid-2027, not 2026).
- **No peer-reviewed DR3/full-survey DESI cosmology exists yet** as of mid-2026; the strongest current DESI statement remains DR2 (March 2025), and the full-survey result is the single most important near-term test.
