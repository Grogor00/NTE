## 3. What the deposited formula is

C7 §2: a resolved fermion has a definite **(unobservable)** phase along the fibre. On resolved matter, a pure fibre half-turn is therefore a **gauge transformation**. A gauge transformation contains **no C, no P, and no N**.

Jammer's geometric claim about the formula stands.

But Part 1 §2.1.2 places N-thickness **orthogonal** to the Lorentzian / spatial S³: t is the metric distance between the two orientations of the N-thickness. The formula (z₁,z₂)→(−z₁,−z₂) acts **on S³ coordinates**, not on N. Calling both "CPN" does not make one map. A reading that equates fibre half-turn with N-reversal **conflates orthogonal directions**. That is not a misread by Jammer; it is a deposit inconsistency.

**Candidate reading B — "Charge conjugation is antipodal (C7), so C is present."**
C7 identifies charge conjugation with exchange of antipodal points. For a *resolved* fermion, particle vs antiparticle is not an unobservable fibre label — it is a physical distinction after Hopf projection / locking. The fibre-half-turn formula cannot be both "unobservable gauge" (C7 §2 phase) and "charge conjugation that sorts baryons" (§8.4) without a deposited rule that says when the same map is gauge and when it is C. **No such rule is deposited.**

**Candidate reading C — "Parity rule (−1)^ℓ shows P is in the map."**
The parity selection rule is a consequence of how harmonics transform under the antipodal map. It governs cross-boundary *mode* coupling. It does not supply N-sorting of matter/antimatter or SU(2)_R assignment.
