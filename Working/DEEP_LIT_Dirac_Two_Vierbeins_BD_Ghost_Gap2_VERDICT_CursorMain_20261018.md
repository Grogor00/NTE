## 4. What Melville–Noller does and does not close

**Does:**
- Couples a Dirac field to an **effective** matter vielbein \(\tilde{E}\) assembled from the two bigravity vielbeins (rank-2 / rank-0 / mixed constructions).
- In mini-superspace, derives the **Dirac-specific** necessary conditions \(\partial_N^2\tilde{N}=0\) and \(\partial_N^2(\tilde{N}\tilde{a})=0\) (eq. 4.21), and shows only **rank-2** constructions satisfy vacuum + boson + fermion conditions simultaneously (Table 1).
- Explicitly exhibits the conjugate momentum \(\pi \propto (\det\tilde{E})/\tilde{N}\) and a Hamiltonian **linear in \(\tilde{N}\)** after the cancellation (4.17–4.18).

**Does not:**
- Analyse naive **double kinetic** Dirac coupling to \(E_{(1)}\) and \(E_{(2)}\) separately.
- Give a **full** nonlinear ADM primary/secondary constraint chain for Dirac (they say full ADM for generic matter is beyond scope; §4.3 is scalar).
- Prove absence/presence of the BD ghost for double-kinetic spinors on arbitrary backgrounds.

## 5. Second question — is lapse-linearity “textbook”?

**Verdict: standard classical result in the research literature since 1977–78; not located as a named chapter in a standard GR textbook.**

Evidence:
1. **Nelson & Teitelboim (1977/1978)** — canonical Einstein–Dirac; time-gauge tetrad; Hamiltonian constraints with spinors. This is the classic reference, not a textbook chapter title.
2. **Melville–Noller (4.17)–(4.18)** — in the effective-vielbein mini-superspace, \(\det\tilde{E}\sim\tilde{N}\,\tilde{a}^3\) cancels the \(1/\tilde{N}\) in \(\pi\), leaving \(\mathcal{H}_{\rm mat}\) linear in \(\tilde{N}\). Same cancellation Jammer describes.
3. Modern ADM+fermion papers (e.g. [1110.3416](https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.3416), [1706.06471](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06471)) treat the structure as known.

**Implication for a paper:** lapse-linearity of **single-metric / single-tetrad** Dirac is **not novel**. Novelty, if any, lives in (i) applying that structure inside **bimetric** constraint counting, and/or (ii) the **naive double-kinetic two-vierbein** case that Melville–Noller never wrote down.
