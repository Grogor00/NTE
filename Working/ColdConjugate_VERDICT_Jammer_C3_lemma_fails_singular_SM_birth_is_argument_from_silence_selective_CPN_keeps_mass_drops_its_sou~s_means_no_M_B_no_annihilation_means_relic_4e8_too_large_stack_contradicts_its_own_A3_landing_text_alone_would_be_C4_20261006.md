## 3. The seven tests

### Q1. Textual force: **permits at most; does not force**

- Part 2 §8.1 says gauge bosons exist "only with t". That is a necessary condition on any gauge boson. It does not count sets. Both orientations have t (Companion 5 §5: "emergent proper time accumulates in the *same* direction in both"; the stack's own premise P2). So §8.1 makes both faces eligible. `L1_AXIOM_REEVAL` §2.1 says exactly this.
- The step from "one list is written" to "one set exists" is an argument from silence. O4\*\* names it: "**Not deposited:** a second crystallisation".
- **Forcing test (pre-registered): does the alternative reading contradict any deposited passage? No.** Conjugate photons contradict nothing.
- **Does the lemma's reading contradict deposited passages? Yes, three.**
  1. Part 2 §8.2–8.3: "The pre-temporal modes are assigned to gauge representations (acquiring colour, isospin, hypercharge), and the species count opens to 90." Ninety is both sectors (Companion 16 §4.3: "45 Weyl species per sector; with conjugates, 90"). So the conjugate 45 acquire colour, isospin and hypercharge at gauge birth. A colour or hypercharge representation with no gluon or hypercharge field is not a gauge representation.
  2. Companion 5 §6: "We cannot see conjugate-sector matter electromagnetically (photons are confined to one direction)". The reason given is that photons do not cross. If the conjugate sector had no electromagnetism the reason would be that there is nothing to see by.
  3. Companion 5 §5: the pair is nucleated "with total energy, charge, and baryon number exactly zero". Charge on the conjugate side presupposes a field it is the charge of.

### Q2. CPN compatibility: **fails**

CPN is charge, parity and N-direction reversal. Part 2 §8.4 sorts "antimatter to −N … not annihilated". Antimatter is defined by opposite gauge charges; "not annihilated" has content only if annihilation is possible. Companion 16 §3.3: "CPN conjugation combined with spatial parity maps left-handed modes in one sector to right-handed modes in the conjugate sector". That is a map of the full mode content. A conjugate sector with fermions and no gauge fields is not the image of ours under that map.

**The stack uses Companion 16 §3.3 in both directions.** O3 (R3a, graded "forced") needs SU(2)_R to be the conjugate sector's weak gauge symmetry, so that conjugate weak physics is on the other face. O4\*\* §2 needs SU(2)_R to be "the parity / representation assignment … not a second entry in the 'comes into existence with t' … list", so that it has no bosons. It cannot be both a gauge force and not a gauge field.

**"Single e₁" cuts the other way.** O4\*V says one vacuum direction e₁ excludes a conjugate vacuum value. But the deposit has one manifold with two orientations (Part 1 §VI; Companion 5 §5: "not a separate manifold but the same S³"). One direction on one manifold is shared by both orientations. The natural reading is the same vacuum value on both sides, not none on one.

### Q4. Entropy: **fails on every branch I can construct**

The stack accepts an equal energy dump at locking (R3e). On the conjugate face that energy must end somewhere.
- **All fermions massless (no Higgs, no confinement):** the conjugate sector is 45 Weyl species of pure radiation for all time. It is hot in the plainest sense and contains no matter.
- **Gluons kept, no photons, quarks massless:** confinement gives massless pions. Radiation again.
- **Gluons kept, quarks massive, no photons and no light weak bosons:** the lightest conjugate hadrons cannot decay or annihilate into anything lighter. See Q5.
- O4\*\* §5 says the energy "can still land in non-relativistic conjugate rest-mass modes by MeV". That is the overclosure case.

### Q5. Relics: **fails by eight to nine orders of magnitude**

Our baryon density is what survived annihilation of a symmetric population roughly 10⁹ times larger. That annihilation needs gauge interactions and light products to carry the entropy away, which is a hot plasma. Remove them and nothing annihilates.

Estimate, conjugate fermions free and massive:
- Number density today per fermionic degree of freedom, for a species decoupled above the electroweak scale: (3/4) × (411/2) cm⁻³ × (3.91/106.75) ≈ 5.6 cm⁻³.
- 45 Weyl species, two states each: about **500 cm⁻³**.
- Observed dark-matter density: Ω_c h² = 0.12 → 1.26 × 10⁻⁶ GeV cm⁻³.
- Mean mass that would match: **about 2.5 eV.** At 1 GeV the density is too large by about **4 × 10⁸**.

Good to a factor of a few (decoupling epoch, species count). The conclusion does not depend on that factor. A 2.5 eV relic would be hot dark matter, which is excluded on other grounds (background knowledge).

**So a cold, interaction-free conjugate sector cannot have M_B = M_A.** To get there it must annihilate, and to annihilate it must be hot.
