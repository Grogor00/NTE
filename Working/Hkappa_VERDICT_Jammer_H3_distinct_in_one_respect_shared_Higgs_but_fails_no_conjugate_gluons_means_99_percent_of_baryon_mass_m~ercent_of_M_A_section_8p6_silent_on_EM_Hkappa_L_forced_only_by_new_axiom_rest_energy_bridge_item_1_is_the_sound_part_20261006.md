## 3. T-M, the mass-source test: **fails, twice**

**3.1 The Higgs is 1% of the answer.**
- Part 1 §III: "99% of proton mass is gluon binding energy".
- Hκ items 3–4 and Hκ-L step 5 remove the conjugate gluon field ("no twin light gluon plasma"; "free gluons are null-time (face-local)").
- Without gluons there are no conjugate hadrons. The conjugate partner of a proton is three free antiquarks with Higgs masses: 2.2 + 2.2 + 4.7 ≈ 9 MeV, against 938 MeV.
- Hκ's sentence "Confinement energy on +N is carried as embedded hadron mass; CPN sets M_B = M_A" restates the equality. It does not supply conjugate confinement.
- If conjugate gluons are kept after all, they are a relativistic plasma above about 150 MeV, which Hκ-L forbids, and the resulting hadrons meet §4.

**3.2 A shared Higgs gives conjugate fermions our charges.**
- A fermion mass from the Standard-Model Higgs comes from a term of the form (left doublet)·(Higgs)·(right singlet). It is invariant under SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y only if the fermions carry the matching weak isospin and hypercharge.
- So conjugate fermions massed by *our* Higgs carry *our* hypercharge and weak isospin. After symmetry breaking they have electric charge under *our* photon. They scatter light. They are not dark, and they can annihilate against our matter.
- The escape is a Higgs that transforms under both SU(2)_L and SU(2)_R. That is the left–right (Pati–Salam-type) construction. Part 2 §8.5 and Companion 16 §3: "it is not a Pati–Salam left-right construction."
- Part 1 §2.3 also commits to leaving "the SM Higgs untouched".

This is the pre-registered sub-question in a sharper form than I anticipated. I expected the difficulty to be the photon surviving the Higgs mechanism. The actual difficulty is that sharing the Higgs shares the gauge charges.
