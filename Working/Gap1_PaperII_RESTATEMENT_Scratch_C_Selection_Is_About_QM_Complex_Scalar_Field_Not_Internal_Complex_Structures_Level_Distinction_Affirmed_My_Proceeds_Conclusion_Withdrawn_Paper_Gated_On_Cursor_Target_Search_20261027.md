## The restatement — at the correct level
**Two different objects, same word "complex":**
- **Internal complex structures J** — real linear maps with J² = −1 (Clifford volume elements: L_{e₇}, R_{e₇}, …), used by the division-algebra programme to organize gauge-symmetry breaking (Furey–Hughes: Spin(10) → … → SM). These live inside the representation algebra and are a model-building device. Furey–Hughes's own input is ℝ⊗ℂ⊗𝕆 (already complex), and they pose the *choice* of J as open.
- **The complex scalar field of quantum theory** — the Hilbert-space unit i: the field over which amplitudes are taken and the Born rule is defined. This is the object the ℂ-selection argument is about.

**The argument, restated purely at the scalar-field level:**
Operational reconstruction selects a *complex* scalar field for quantum theory — this is **established and published** (Masanes–Müller 2011; Barnum–Müller–Ududec "Higher-order interference and single-system postulates…", which answers "why ℂ" operationally; CDP 2011), via local tomography / composition. That same selection:
- **(a)** excludes the exceptional-Jordan arena of the F₄ = Aut(J₃(𝕆)) route — J₃(𝕆) admits no nontrivial composite and fails local tomography (Barnum–Graydon–Wilce 1606.09331; arXiv:2001.11421); and
- **(b)** is *presupposed* by the complex-algebra routes (Furey Cl(6) ≅ M₈(ℂ); Furey–Hughes ℝ⊗ℂ⊗ℍ⊗𝕆), which take the complex scalar field as input and say nothing about why it is complex.

**Net, at the correct level:** the division-algebra SM programme never engages the complex *scalar field* question — it either uses an arena the operational selection forbids (Jordan route) or assumes the complex scalar field as input (complex-algebra routes). The internal-J descent (Furey–Hughes) is orthogonal to this and does not touch it.
