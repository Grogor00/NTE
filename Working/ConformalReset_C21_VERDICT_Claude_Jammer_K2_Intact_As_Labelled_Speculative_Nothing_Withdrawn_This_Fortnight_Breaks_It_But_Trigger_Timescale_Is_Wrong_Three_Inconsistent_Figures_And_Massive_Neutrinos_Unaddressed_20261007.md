## Finding 1 — the trigger timescale is wrong, and the paper carries three inconsistent figures. Claim-level (numeric)

§2: "The one-photon-per-Hubble-cube crossing sits at roughly 10²²–10²³ years …, about 200 e-folds past today". §10: "At the trigger epoch Ht ≈ 5.7 × 10¹¹".

From the paper's own numbers:
- T_reset = 2.213 × 10⁻²⁹ K (§3). Photon temperature today 2.725 K. Ratio 1.23 × 10²⁹. Temperature falls as 1/a, so the relic photons reach T_reset after **ln(1.23 × 10²⁹) ≈ 67 e-folds**.
- At a de Sitter Hubble time of about 17 Gyr, that is about **1.2 × 10¹² years**.
- Count check: about 4.1 × 10⁸ photons per m³ today in a cube of side c/H_dS ≈ 1.65 × 10²⁶ m gives about 1.9 × 10⁸⁷ photons; ln of that is 201. Density falls as a⁻³, so 201/3 = 67 e-folds.

The paper's "about 200" is the logarithm of the photon *count*, not the number of e-folds. And the three figures do not agree with each other: 200 e-folds is about 3.5 × 10¹² years, not 10²²; 10²² years is 5.7 × 10¹¹ e-folds, not 200. AC's verification "independently re-checked Ht ≈ 5.7 × 10¹¹" — that confirms 10²² years × H, not that 10²² years is when the trigger fires.

I pre-registered 67 e-folds before reading. I have not read the two cited papers; they may give 10²²–10²³ years for some other late-universe milestone. On the trigger as §3 defines it, applied to the relic photons, the answer is 67.

What moves with it:
- §10's no-hair residue "one part in 10^(10¹¹)" becomes about e⁻²⁰¹ ≈ 10⁻⁸⁷. Still far below anything observable; the conclusion holds, the number does not.
- §2's "much earlier than the ~10¹⁰⁰-year black-hole-evaporation horizon" holds more strongly.
- **New consequence:** at 10¹² years stars are still burning and no matter has decayed. See Finding 2.

Minor, same section: ℓ_dS is given as 1.4 × 10²⁶ m, which is c/H₀. With the H_dS that §3 uses it is about 1.65 × 10²⁶ m. Wording.

## Finding 2 — the massless-content premise is not met at the paper's own trigger, and the neutrino is not addressed. Claim-level

§3 opens: "Matter decays to photons". §5: "A universe of purely massless content has no scale to lose."

- At 67 e-folds nothing has decayed. Bound structures do not dilute; any Hubble cube containing one is flooded with starlight and cannot satisfy the trigger (the paper's own §10 argument about black holes applies to stars). Empty cubes satisfy it. So the trigger first fires in the voids while structure persists elsewhere. The paper's picture of a boundary that "can no longer hold itself together" as a whole does not describe that.
- **Relic neutrinos.** Their number density today is comparable to the photons' (about 3.4 × 10⁸ against 4.1 × 10⁸ per m³). They dilute the same way. At the trigger there is on average about one massive neutrino per Hubble cube alongside the one photon. NTE requires three massive Majorana neutrinos, and the lightest has nothing to decay into. The paper does not mention relic neutrinos at all (searched the full text: "neutrino" appears only in the struck §8 row and the §11 mixing note).

What softens this, stated fairly: NTE's reset is *local*. Roughly half of all cubes will hold a photon and no neutrino by chance. So the local variant is better placed than Penrose's global one, which needs all mass gone everywhere. But then "matter decays to photons" is not the premise the mechanism uses, and the paper should say what happens in a cube that holds a neutrino.

## Finding 3 — "no scale to lose" against a map defined by two scales. Wording, with a claim underneath

§2 insists the field does not vanish: |Φ_NTE|² → v_EW²√Ω_Λ, "Finite, constant." §5 defines the conformal factor as Ω ≡ R_H/ℓ_P. So the end state has an electroweak scale, a Hubble scale and a Planck scale, and the map is built from two of them. The stated rationale — a universe with no scale — is not the situation described. The paper is candid that Ω is a definition and that the Planck-scale arrival is "one assumption seen twice"; it is not candid that the rationale and the definition pull against each other. Penrose's version needs rest mass to fade; this one explicitly keeps the field finite.
