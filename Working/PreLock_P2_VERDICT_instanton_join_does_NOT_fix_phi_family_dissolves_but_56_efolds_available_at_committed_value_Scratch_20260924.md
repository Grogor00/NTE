## Verdict

**Third fork — the join does not fix φ determinately — with a quantitative rider from the first.** Two independent reasons the join is not a single φ: (i) the Hartle–Hawking de Sitter instanton is a **one-parameter family**, one saddle per nucleation scale / field value, standard for the no-boundary construction; (ii) NTE's own saddle profile "depends sensitively on V_NTE *off* the post-locking trajectory — the range of Φ the sub-phase explores — which is precisely the quantity Companion 8's F3 found **under-constrained**" (Companion 9 §2). So there is no single "the join," and "how many e-folds from it" has no unique answer. **The question dissolves.** The rider: at the corpus's **committed** on-trajectory join value, φ ≈ 16 M_Pl, the α = 2 map gives N_e ≈ 63.4 ≥ 56 — so enough inflation is **available** at the value the corpus already uses. Availability is not a prediction; *which* member of the family is realised (hence whether the available-and-sufficient one is selected) is the measure question, P3.

## 1. The join is a family, not a point

Companion 9 §1: the saddle closes regularly at τ = 0 (the pole, H → ∞, Φ → ∞, a Planck-scale instanton radius) and continues Euclidean→Lorentzian "at the equator." But the **scale** of the instanton (its H, hence its Φ via the constraint Φ² = H/β) is not fixed by the no-boundary condition — the no-boundary construction admits one regular instanton per nucleation scale. This is the standard HH situation: a one-parameter family of saddles, parameterised by the field value at the join. Nothing in the classical instanton picks one member.

NTE adds a *second*, independent source of non-determinacy: the reduced saddle equation (C9 §2, eq. 2) needs V_NTE across the **sub-phase** range of Φ — off the post-locking trajectory — and that off-trajectory potential is under-constrained (C8 F3). So even for a *fixed* nucleation scale, the profile from pole to equator, and hence φ_join and the e-folds delivered, are not pinned by deposited structure.

**Illustration of the sensitivity (C9 §3, honest negative):** take the simplest off-trajectory extension V_NTE = (3β²/8πG)Φ⁴ read globally. The Φ⁴ term cancels the 3M²H² term in eq. (2) exactly, leaving Ḣ² = 24β²M²H/a² → H → const as a → ∞: a pure-de-Sitter instanton, n_s = 1 (≈ 8σ off). A *different* off-trajectory commitment (the α = 2 plateau) gives a different join and a different e-fold count. The join is a choice of off-trajectory potential, not an output.

## 2. At the committed value, ≥ 56 is available (the rider)

The corpus commits, on-trajectory, to the α = 2 attractor (Companion 17: V_E = λΦ⁴/(1+Φ²/6M²)², ξ = 1/6), with φ* = 16.35 M_Pl ↔ N_e = 63.4, and Companion 9 §5 independently lands φ ≈ 15.9 M_Pl ↔ N_e ≈ 63.5 on the Φ²-chaotic reading it supersedes. Both sit at φ ≈ 16 M_Pl on an **unbounded plateau**. Delivering N ≥ 56 needs a marginally smaller field (the plateau is monotone in N vs φ), well inside the deposited range. So **at the value the corpus already uses, the available inflation (≈ 63 e-folds) comfortably exceeds N_* ≈ 55–56** (the horizon-matching requirement, per my C21 T1 verdict). The ~7 surplus e-folds are unobservable N_pre.

**But this is availability, not derivation.** It holds *because* the corpus committed to a high-plateau join; the instanton did not force that commitment (§1). A low-plateau member of the same family would deliver < 56 and fail — and nothing in the classical instanton excludes it. So P2 does not upgrade N_e or the (n_s, r) conditional; it confirms the committed trajectory is *self-consistent on the e-fold budget*, and hands the "why this member" question to P3.
