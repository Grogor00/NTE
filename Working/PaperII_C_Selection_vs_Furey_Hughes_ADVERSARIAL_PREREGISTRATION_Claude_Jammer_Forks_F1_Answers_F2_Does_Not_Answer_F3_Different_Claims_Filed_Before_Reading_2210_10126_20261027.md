**The ℂ-selection argument under test**, as stated in AC's 10-26 plan (the only statement of it I have read; it is not yet read in any draft):
- (a) the ℂ ⊂ ℍ ⊂ 𝕆 chain runs the wrong way for a derivation;
- (b) Hurwitz needs a normed-division-algebra premise as strong as the conclusion;
- (c) a descent that takes its norm from the Hilbert structure it is meant to explain is circular.

**Stake:** none in Paper II existing. AC has said that if the answer is no, there is no paper.

## Forks
- **F1: it answers them.** Furey–Hughes's 𝕆 → ℍ → ℂ derivation uses a premise that (b) or (c) identifies as importing what it derives. One example would be using the octonion norm or inner product to define the complex structure, together with a Hilbert-space framing. Paper II then has one novel section.
- **F2: it does not answer them.** Their derivation selects ℂ by a mechanism none of (a)–(c) touches. For example, the complex structure might be defined algebraically by symmetry breaking, with the norm used only as already given by the octonions themselves. Then there is no paper.
- **F3: the claims are different.** Furey–Hughes derive *complex structures inside* 𝕆 (a choice of imaginary unit), not *why quantum theory is over ℂ*. Then our argument and theirs do not conflict, and our argument must be reworded. Whether a novel section survives depends on the rewording.

**Prior:** F3 0.50 · F2 0.30 · F1 0.20.

**Reason for the F3 prior:** the octonion norm is intrinsic to 𝕆, since 𝕆 is a normed division algebra by definition. So (b) and (c) do not obviously bite on a construction that starts from 𝕆. And "a complex structure from 𝕆" is plausibly not "why the Hilbert space is complex".

2. For each of (a)–(c), state whether it applies, with quotes.
3. Check whether their result says anything about the *Hilbert space's* scalar field, or only about an internal complex structure.
