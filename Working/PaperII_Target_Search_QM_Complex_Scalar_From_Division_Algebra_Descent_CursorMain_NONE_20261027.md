**Question:** Does any published framework claim to derive quantum theory's complex scalar field from an octonionic or division-algebra descent?
**Verdict:** **NONE.**
A citation below is used only where the page was fetched this session. The Günaydin 1976 body was not fetched.

## What was not counted

Using octonions inside quantum theory is common. Replacing the complex scalars by octonion coefficients is the opposite claim. Choosing an imaginary unit inside an algebra that already contains \(\mathbb{C}\), or inside \(\mathbb{O}\) in order to break a gauge symmetry, is the internal-\(J\) object the ruling already separated from the Hilbert-space scalar field.

## Nearest sentence, not a target

Günaydin, *J. Math. Phys.* 17 (1976) 1875. Abstract fetched from the AIP page, OSTI, and INSPIRE. Body not fetched; the journal page is paywalled.

> The choice of a direction in the space of octonion units leads naturally to a representation of the Poincaré group in terms of complex scalar products and complex scalars. … product states associated with color singlets … form a physical quantum mechanical Hilbert space.

The stated aim is colored quarks. The abstract does not say that the complex scalar field of quantum theory is what the descent explains. De Leo and Abdel-Khalek, hep-th/9609032, fetched, read that programme the other way: an attempt to extend the number field from the complex numbers to the octonions, with a complex scalar product required as an input. Their sentence: "Without it we cannot define a consistent tensor product."

A 2025 paraphrase (arXiv:2511.09395, fetched) says Günaydin's direction "determines the complex structure and a color decomposition" on the imaginary octonions. That is an internal structure, not this question.

## Fetched, and not the claim

- **Baez, arXiv:1101.5690, PDF fetched.** Quantum theory may use \(\mathbb{R}\), \(\mathbb{C}\), or \(\mathbb{H}\). The octonions are set aside as nonassociative. The paper's claim is that the three associative cases are one structure: "Nature did not choose one: she chose all three." It does not derive \(\mathbb{C}\) from \(\mathbb{O}\) or from a Cayley–Dickson descent.
- **Dixon, arXiv:1012.1304, fetched.** Clifford algebras are represented over \(\mathbb{R}\), \(\mathbb{C}\), and \(\mathbb{H}\) from the first table. \(\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}\) and then \(\mathbb{O}\) are inputs. No derivation of the scalar field.
- **Köplinger and Dzhunushaliev, arXiv:0910.3347, fetched.** A nonassociative prototype on complex octonions. One case reduces to ordinary complex numbers when the imaginary units coincide. Complex octonions are \(\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{O}\) (Köplinger, arXiv:0812.0212, fetched). \(\mathbb{C}\) is in the algebra.
- **Gogberashvili, hep-th/0505101, PDF fetched.** Dirac equations with real split-octonion wave functions "and not bi-spinors." That replaces the complex wave function.
- **De Leo and Abdel-Khalek, hep-th/9609032, fetched.** Complex geometry is the tool that makes octonionic quantum mechanics consistent. It is not an output of a descent.
- **Horwitz, quant-ph/9602001, fetched.** An attempt to use octonion coefficients on the Hilbert space, blocked by nonassociativity, then turned to Clifford modules. Generalising past \(\mathbb{C}\), not deriving it.
- **Manogue and Dray, arXiv:0911.2253, fetched.** Choose one octonion direction to be special, then a \(2\times 2\) block, inside \(E_6\), to describe leptons. Hurwitz is stated as the classification of the four algebras. The scalar field of quantum theory is not the thing derived.
