## Node A — doubly-coupled matter revives the BD ghost
`doubly_coupled_bd_ghost` (Yamashita–De Felice–Tanaka, [arXiv:1408.0487](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.0487); VERIFIED): matter with **two kinetic couplings** to two metrics generically **reintroduces the Boulware–Deser ghost**; worked examples are **scalar / perfect-fluid**, with the conjugate momentum depending on both lapses. Companion: `hassan_rosen_bimetric` ([arXiv:1109.3515](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.3515); VERIFIED), ghost-free vacuum framework, β free.
Scalar double-kinetic case also in Soloviev [1505.00840](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.00840) §5.3 (minisuperspace: BD ghost returns).

## Node B — Dirac/spinor matter on two vierbeins
**Partially addressed (this is the v2 correction).** Melville–Noller, [arXiv:1511.01485](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.01485) (JHEP 01 (2016) 094), couple a Dirac field to **one effective matter vielbein Ẽ built from the two bigravity vielbeins**, derive the Dirac-specific minisuperspace lapse conditions (eq. 4.21), and exhibit a Dirac Hamiltonian **linear in the lapse** (π ∝ det Ẽ / Ñ; eqs. 4.16–4.18). What they do **not** do: analyse a Dirac field with **two independent kinetic terms**, one on each vierbein separately; nor give a full nonlinear ADM primary/secondary constraint chain for the fermion (their full ADM is scalar-only, §4.3).

## The object they share, and the narrowed gap
Both bear on **the BD ghost in the matter sector of ghost-free bimetric gravity.** Node A settles the scalar double-kinetic case (ghost returns); Melville–Noller settles the **effective-vielbein** Dirac case (lapse-linear, conditions derived). **The remaining gap is specifically the *naive double-kinetic* Dirac coupling** — ∼ det e · ψ̄ e⁻¹∇ψ + det f · ψ̄ f⁻¹∇ψ — whose Hamiltonian/constraint analysis **no paper performs.** The physics question: does the BD secondary constraint survive for that first-order fermionic double coupling, as it does *not* for scalars (1408.0487, 1505.00840)?
