## Node A — division-algebra Standard-Model structures
- `g2_su3_branching` (Günaydin–Gürsey; computed 2026-10-16): G₂ ⊃ SU(3), **7 → 1 ⊕ 3 ⊕ 3̄** — theorem.
- `g2_cannot_contain_sm` (computed 2026-10-16): rank(G₂)=2 < rank(SM)=4 — **no-go**; G₂ gives colour only.
- `sm_in_f4` (Dubois-Violette–Todorov): SM-related group inside **F₄ = Aut(J₃(𝕆))** — framework.
- `sm_from_cl6` (Furey): SM representations from **Cl(6)** minimal left ideals — framework.
**What they establish:** division-algebra / exceptional-group structures reproduce (parts of) the SM gauge group and one generation's representation content — **working inside an already-complex Hilbert space** (ℂ⊗𝕆, Cl(6) over ℂ, J₃(𝕆) with a complex structure). Complex QM is an **input** to all of them.

## Node B — operational reconstruction of complex QM
- `complex_qm_from_operational_axioms` (Hardy 2001; Chiribella–D'Ariano–Perinotti 2011; Masanes–Müller 2011): the **complex** Hilbert-space structure of QM is **derived** from operational / informational axioms (continuity-of-reversible-transformations, purification, local tomography, …).
**What it establishes:** *why* amplitudes are complex (vs real/quaternionic), from premises that say nothing about gauge groups or division algebras.

## The object they share
Both bear on **the complex Hilbert space of quantum theory**: Node A *assumes* it and finds gauge/generation structure inside division algebras over it; Node B *derives* it from operational axioms and is silent on gauge/generation structure.

## What would be needed to bridge (NOT proposed here)
A derivation connecting the two directions: either (i) an operational/informational axiom set whose reconstruction *outputs* the division-algebra / exceptional-group structure (i.e. explains the SM gauge content as part of the reconstruction), or (ii) a demonstration that the octonionic SM structures *require* complex (not real/quaternionic) amplitudes for an operational reason — turning "we work over ℂ⊗𝕆" from an input into a consequence. Neither exists.
