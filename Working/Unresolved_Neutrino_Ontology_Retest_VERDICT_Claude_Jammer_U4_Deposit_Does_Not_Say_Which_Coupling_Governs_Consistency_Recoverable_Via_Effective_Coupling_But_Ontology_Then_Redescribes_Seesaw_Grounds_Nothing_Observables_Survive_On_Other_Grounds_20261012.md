## Landing: **U-4.** The deposit never says which coupling governs. Either answer leaves the ontology grounding nothing.

**The two numbers are different objects. AC's U-1 mechanism is real.**
- **Y_ν ≈ 0.34 (and Y₃ = 1)** is the **Dirac Yukawa**, coupling the light doublet to the heavy singlet (C13 §3–§4).
- **≈ 10⁻¹²–10⁻¹³** is the **light neutrino's own coupling to the Higgs**, which in type-I seesaw is of order m_ν/v (arXiv:1001.5215).

So "10⁻¹² versus 0.34" is not by itself a contradiction. **This corrects my B2 verdict.** There I wrote that y_ν ∼ 10⁻¹² "is the value it has under the hypothesis C7 §7 says the ontology excludes". The number is right, but the sentence is incomplete: the same number is also the seesaw's light–light coupling. My B2 phrasing overstated the finding.

**The deposit does not draw the distinction, and its texts point both ways:**

**The conflict does not depend on the open Yukawa normalisation.** C13 itself says the magnitude is open (8π vs 4π; "the finite-Dirac computation … derives no Yukawa magnitude at all"). But any type-I seesaw with the deposit's heavy scales fixes the order of the Dirac Yukawa. My arithmetic, Y² = m_ν·M/v²:
- With m_ν ≈ 0.0086 eV and M = M₁ ≈ 5.4 × 10⁹ GeV (the lightest deposited scale): **Y ≈ 9 × 10⁻⁴**, about 300 times y_e.
- With M = M_R ≈ 8.2 × 10¹⁴ GeV: **Y ≈ 0.34.**

**Band: 10⁻³ ≲ Y ≲ 1, never 10⁻¹².**

### Why U-4 does not resolve to "the ontology survives"

**Take the U-1 reading (effective coupling at EWSB).** Consistency comes back, but **grounding goes**:
1. **The ground becomes circular.** The light state's coupling is ∼ m_ν/v *because* M_R is a large **Majorana** mass. "Unresolved because minimally coupled" then reads "unresolved because the seesaw made the light state light". The ontology is downstream of the Majorana seesaw, so it **cannot ground Majorana**. That is the circularity the pre-registration flagged. On Part 1 §II.0's own scale, it **re-describes** rather than explains.
2. **C7's account of the small mass is withdrawn on this reading.** C7 §2 says "the neutrino's tiny mass is the geometric tension … the cost of being almost, but not quite, unresolved". The seesaw attributes the small mass to a large M_R with an O(10⁻³–1) Yukawa. On the U-1 reading the causation runs the other way.
3. **Part 1 §VII's override fails on epoch.** It invokes non-resolution at the locking epoch (N ∼ 10⁻², pre-EWSB) to stop a neutrino that "formally exceed[s] the locking threshold" from locking. By C7 §3, the binary resolution and the m_ν/v coupling that grounds non-resolution exist only at EWSB. A status that does not yet exist is being used to override a criterion that does.

**Take the U-3 reading (Lagrangian Yukawa at locking).** The neutrino doublet, with Y₃ = 1 on C13's own assignment, is the **most strongly Higgs-coupled lepton** in the deposit (y_τ ≈ 10⁻²). By C7 §2's own logic it is pinned harder than the electron, and the ontology does not start.

**So:** the deposit can be made **consistent** (choose the U-1 reading and state it), but not in a form where the ontology **does work**. Either it re-describes the seesaw, or it fails. Choosing the reading is Greg's ruling (U-4). The landing does not change with the choice.

**Is there a U-2 replacement ground in the deposit? Not found.** I searched C7, Parts 1–3, C5, C10 and C13 for a coupling-independent ground:
- **Part 2 §8.6** ("the CPN-even neutrino stays delocalised over the full S⁷") asserts Z₂-evenness. It does not derive it.
- **Electric neutrality** would be a standard ground: Majorana character is open only to a fermion with no conserved U(1) charge, and post-EWSB the neutrino is the only Standard Model fermion with Q = 0. It is **not in the deposit** ("neutral": no hits in C7 or Parts 1–3). Adopting it would reduce "unresolved on the conjugation axis" to the textbook statement, which is standard and not NTE's.
