## Finding 2 — N1 is not shown to be a consistent theory (disagree with both Cursor legs)

N1 is two Einstein–Hilbert actions with no potential, plus one matter field coupled to both metrics. Three problems, in order of strength.

**(a) Separate Bianchi identities.** Each Einstein equation requires ∇^±_μ(T_m^± + T_ν^±)^{μν} = 0 *separately*. T_m^± are separately conserved. But ν is one field on one manifold, so S_ν is invariant only under the *diagonal* diffeomorphism, which guarantees only the *sum* of the two ν-conservation laws on the ν equation of motion. The remaining four equations, ∇^+_μ T_ν^{+μν} = 0, are extra constraints on ν and the metrics that the action does not supply. On g⁺ = g⁻ they hold trivially (each T_ν^± is half the total). Off it — and the sectors' matter is not co-located, so perturbations are off it — they do not hold automatically. Either they over-constrain the system or they promote four would-be gauge functions to dynamical degrees of freedom. Neither Cursor file mentions this. This is my own argument; it is standard reasoning but nobody has reviewed it.

**(b) Multi-graviton no-go.** Boulanger, Damour, Gualtieri, Henneaux (hep-th/0007220), abstract: "there is no consistent (ghost-free) coupling, with at most two derivatives of the fields, that can mix the various 'gravitons'… The only possible deformations are given by a sum of individual Einstein-Hilbert actions. The impossibility of cross-couplings subsists in the presence of scalar matter." N1 is two massless gravitons cross-coupled through shared matter. Caveat stated honestly: the abstract says *scalar* matter; I have not read the paper to see whether the spinor case is covered. So this is "strong presumption against", not "theorem applies".

**(c) With a potential it gets worse, not better.** If Track B adds the Hassan–Rosen potential and keeps the shared ν, Yamashita, De Felice, Tanaka (1408.0487), abstract: "the Boulware-Deser ghost generally revives in the presence of doubly coupled matter and … ghost freeness strongly restricts the model of kinetically doubly coupled matter." So the Cursor Scratch starter's "(S_ν optional)" is not a free add-on; and Cursor Jammer's line "Optional shared ν does not waive the ghost/branch checklist" understates it — the shared ν is itself the ghost hazard.

**(d) Spinor-specific.** One spinor with two vielbeine transforms under one local Lorentz group, not two. The antisymmetric part of e⁺⁻¹e⁻ stops being gauge. Cursor Scratch's "γ^± = e^±γ^a, same flat Clifford algebra" does not address this. Unquantified; flagged, not relied on.

Net: "standard spinor kinetic signs on each metric; no wrong-sign metric mixing" (Cursor Scratch §6) checks the wrong thing. The hazard in doubly-coupled matter is the broken second diffeomorphism, not the sign of the spinor kinetic term.

**What this means for NTE, stated plainly.** The deposit's central dark-sector object — one neutrino field living on both orientations and gravitating in both — is exactly a doubly-coupled matter field. If the two orientations have two independent metrics, that object sits in the class the literature flags. If they do not have two independent metrics (one geometry, two orientations), there is no bigravity and no S_ν[g⁺,g⁻,ν] to vary; the bridge-law programme is then mis-posed and the question returns to Door B. Which of these the deposit means is not something I found stated. That is a question for Greg, and I think it is the most important thing in this file.

## Finding 3 — on the symmetric background N1 halves the neutrino's gravity (new; not pre-registered)

Disclosure: this consequence was **not** in my pre-registration. I noticed it while reading. It is a count, not a fit, and it goes against the theory, but treat it as post-hoc.

With g⁺ = g⁻ = g, S_ν is (½ + ½) × the ordinary Majorana action: ν is one canonically normalised ordinary neutrino. Its stress splits half to each metric. Adding the two Einstein equations: G = 8πG(T_b + ½ T_ν,standard). The result is independent of whether the weights are written ½:½ or 1:1 (rescale the field).

So under N1 each sector's expansion feels **half** the standard neutrino density: neutrino contribution to N_eff ≈ 3.044/2 ≈ **1.52**, against ACT DR6 2.86 ± 0.13 (figure as verified in my ΔN_eff verdict of 2026-10-06; not re-read today). About ten standard deviations low. Cursor Scratch's table says "ΔN_eff exposed … adds radiation-like stress on both metrics" — the sign is the other way: N1 by itself removes radiation, it does not add it. Anything added to repair it (conjugate photons crossing) is new content with free strength unless derived — no-f applies.

Also: on the symmetric background the "bridge" is just the ordinary neutrino. r_bg = ρ_ν/ρ_b is not a dark component at all. Cursor Scratch's "hot bridge established" is the Standard Model neutrino, counted at half weight.
