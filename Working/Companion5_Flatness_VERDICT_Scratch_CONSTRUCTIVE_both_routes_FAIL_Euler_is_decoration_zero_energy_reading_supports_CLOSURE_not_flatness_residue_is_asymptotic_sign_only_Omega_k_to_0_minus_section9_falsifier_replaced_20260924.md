## Q1 — Does the bilateral zero-energy condition imply anything about spatial curvature? No — and read correctly it favours closure.

Two quantities, both called "zero total energy," are conflated:

- **(A) Bilateral / Janus sum.** §5: the two CPN-conjugate sectors nucleate "with total energy, charge, and baryon number exactly zero." This is a cancellation *between sectors*: E₁ + E₂ = 0. It is antisymmetric bookkeeping across the pair.
- **(B) Single-sector FRW specific energy.** The Newtonian reading of the Friedmann equation: ½ȧ² − (4πG/3)ρa² = −½k, i.e. E ∝ −k. Here E=0 ↔ k=0 (flat, critical density); E<0 ↔ k=+1 (closed); E>0 ↔ k=−1 (open). "E" is the specific energy of one sector's expansion.

§4 takes (A)'s zero and feeds it into (B)'s "E=0 ⇒ k=0." But (A) is a sum over two sectors; (B) is one sector's expansion energy. **(A)=0 is satisfied by each sector closed (k=+1, E_sector<0) with the two summing to zero**, and NTE's architecture (closed S³, item 11) *requires* exactly that. (A) constrains neither the sign nor the magnitude of a single sector's curvature.

**The sharpening (beyond AC's suspicion).** Even granting §4 its own language — "the total energy of the manifold must vanish" as a global, single-universe statement — the correct GR fact is that a **closed (k=+1)** universe is the one whose total energy is unambiguously zero (the Hamiltonian constraint with no spatial boundary; matter + gravitational potential energy cancel — the standard "zero-energy universe"). Flat and open universes have infinite spatial volume and no comparably well-defined vanishing total energy. **So "total energy of the manifold vanishes" is a hallmark of closure, not flatness.** §4 uses the correct premise (closed S³, zero total energy) and draws the sign-inverted conclusion (flat). The premise supports Ω_k < 0.

**Answer.** The bilateral zero-energy condition does not bear on curvature in the direction §4 needs. Read as a bilateral sum it is silent on single-sector k; read as a global total it favours closure. Either way the flatness conclusion does not follow.

## Q2 — Is the Euler-identity step doing any work? (b) legitimately in §5 as the antipodal map; (c) decoration in §4 for flatness.

e^{iπ}+1=0 is an algebraic identity about complex exponentials. It is true whether the universe is flat, closed, or open, so **it cannot constrain a physical curvature.**

- **In §5 — legitimate (category b).** "The CPN transformation is the antipodal map on S³ … multiplication by e^{iπ}." Here e^{iπ}=−1 *is* the antipodal/parity operation; correct, structural, and used elsewhere in the corpus. No objection.
- **In §4 — decoration (category c) for the flatness conclusion.** "The Euler identity … encodes the zero-sum energy constraint." Operationally this maps "+1" and "e^{iπ}=−1" to the two sectors' energies, so e^{iπ}+1=0 is a mnemonic for E₁+E₂=0 (the bilateral cancellation of Q1(A)). That is at most notation for a two-term cancellation; as a step toward Ω_k=0 it is a **pun on "zero"** — the algebraic zero of the identity is asserted to imply zero spatial curvature, with no physics connecting them. It does no derivational work.

**Say it plainly, as the directive asks:** the Euler-identity step is decoration on the flatness conclusion. It is the same shape the corpus has retired four times (six-note chord; β_NTE √6/π; θ₁₃; Cabibbo) — an elegant identity presented as a derivation of a physical number it does not touch.

## Q3 — The dynamical route. Does H=β|Φ|² imply flatness? No — expansion rate, not curvature.

Friedmann: H² = (8πG/3)ρ_tot − k/a². The NTE constraint H = β|Φ|² fixes the **left-hand side** (the expansion rate) in terms of the field. It says nothing about the curvature term −k/a²: for any k, the constraint is met by ρ_tot adjusting. Part 1 §2.3 is explicit that H=β|Φ|² is an **identity/relabelling** — "adopting |Φ|² = v²(H/H₀) makes H=β|Φ|² an identity with β=H₀/v² … Φ_NTE is a relabelling of the expansion history." A relabelling of H(N) carries no curvature information whatever.

**Answer.** The dynamical route constrains the expansion rate only (AC's expectation confirmed). It does not survive as an independent derivation of flatness. **Fork 3 refuted.** §4's "the Hubble relation explains how flatness is maintained" is unsupported: H=β|Φ|² neither establishes nor maintains k=0.

## Q4 — Reconciliation: what survives? Asymptotic, sign-only Ω_k → 0⁻.

- **Asymptotic Ω_k → 0⁻ — defensible and correct.** NTE requires a closed S³ (k=+1), so Ω_k = −k/(aH)² < 0. Inflation drives aH large, hence |Ω_k| → 0 **from below**. Near-flatness is *inflationary dilution of a closed geometry*, not an exact topological zero. This is precisely Part 3's ratified "Ω_k → 0, exact Ω_k = 0 not claimed" and item 11 (Ω_k < 0 strictly). The mechanism is inflation, not Euler closure.
- **Magnitude vs sign — sign only.** NTE fixes the **sign** (Ω_k < 0) from the closed-S³ topology. It does **not** fix the **magnitude**: |Ω_k| ~ e^{−2N_e} depends on the inflationary e-fold count N_e, which is a horizon-matching input, not pinned (the C21 T1 result; the P1–P3 open node). AC's expectation confirmed, and the P1–P4 round supplies the structural reason: the crossing datum carries at most one real number Ω, already built from the parent's β — no spare content to set a second parameter such as |Ω_k|.
- **Consistency with Planck.** Ω_k = 0.0007 ± 0.0019 is consistent with zero; its central value is slightly **positive** (which strict closure forbids), but the interval spans Ω_k < 0, so the data are consistent with the required closed geometry. Item 11's live falsifier (a *confirmed* Ω_k > 0 falsifies) is the correct statement.

**So what survives is not the derivation but its replacement:** a closed geometry whose curvature is inflated toward zero from below, sign-constrained only. That is already in the corpus (Part 3 + item 11) and owes nothing to §4's Euler/energy argument.
