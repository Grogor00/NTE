## The translation of Greg's idea — three versions, as given to him

1. **One field, two projections.** The same field couples to both metrics. That is the symmetric coupling: the class arXiv:1408.0487 flags. If energy is counted once, each side gets half (my N_eff ≈ 1.52).
2. **Even combination of two components, one per orientation**, tied by a non-derivative link. Probably ghost-safe and exchange-symmetric, but it brings the odd combination as extra light states — mirror-neutrino mixing — against "exactly three" and the no-fourth-neutrino falsifier.
3. **The neutrino's near-masslessness does the work.** This is the lead.

## The lead, stated so it can be killed

*Claim to be tested, not asserted:* a massless spinor's kinetic term depends only on the conformal class of the metric. So if the two orientation-metrics are related by a conformal factor, g⁻ = Ω²g⁺, then a kinetic term on each is the same term twice, and the factor can be absorbed by rescaling the field. The neutrino then does not couple kinetically to the *difference* between the metrics at all. Only the mass term sees the difference, and it does so without derivatives — the form 1408.0487's exception allows.

If that held, "symmetric and bilateral" (the deposit's neutrino) and "no second kinetic coupling" (the ghost-free condition) would be the same thing for an almost-massless field, with the departure controlled by the mass.

Deposit hooks, from reads on 2026-10-07: Companion 7 §2 — the neutrino's near-uniform fibre distribution "'wants' to be null-time, like a photon"; mass as "the cost of being almost, but not quite, entirely unresolved". These are the sentences Greg is pointing at.

## Forks

- **Cf1** — the doubly-coupled ghost does not arise for conformally invariant matter on two metrics, generally or on a stated class of metric pairs wide enough to include cosmological perturbations. Then the deposit's symmetric neutrino is in a safe class up to mass-suppressed terms. Gate 0 would move for a reason native to NTE.
- **Cf2** — it evades the ghost only for the relative conformal mode. Any relative shear between the metrics couples doubly, and the ghost or an equivalent pathology returns there. No real gain.
- **Cf3** — the theorem, or a later paper, already covers conformally coupled or fermionic matter and the ghost revives. Dead.
- **Cf4** — not determinable from the literature; would need an original constraint analysis.

**Prior: Cf2.** Reason: two generic metrics are not conformally related, and the Boulware–Deser mode is tied to the lapses, not obviously to the conformal factor. I expect the trick to be real for one mode and irrelevant for the rest. Greg's instinct points at Cf1; that is a reason to test it and a reason to distrust any lane, including me, that lands there easily.

## Kill criteria, fixed now

The lead is dead if any one holds:
1. The two orientation-metrics cannot be restricted to a conformal pair without an added assumption the deposit does not make. (Then say what the assumption is; it is new content.)
2. A relative non-conformal perturbation reintroduces a kinetic double coupling at leading order.
3. The neutrino mass term, though non-derivative, couples to both lapses in a way the 1408.0487 exception does not cover. The exception is written for a scalar with its potential on *one* metric; a mass term weighted on both measures is not obviously the same thing.
4. The argument needs the neutrino exactly massless. NTE requires three massive Majorana neutrinos.
5. It needs a tunable quantity to work. No-f.
