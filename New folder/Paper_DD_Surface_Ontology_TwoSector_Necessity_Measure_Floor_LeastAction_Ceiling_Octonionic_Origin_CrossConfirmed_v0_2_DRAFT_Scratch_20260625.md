## Abstract

NTE makes a surface fundamental and reads the Born rule as invariant area on it. We ask what constrains such an ontology and find two independent requirements, each pointing at three, that **cannot be met on a single manifold** — and that therefore force the surface ontology to split into two sectors. **Floor (measure/quantum):** the Bloch sphere's rotation group SO(3) contains a free group F₂ and is non-amenable, so the sphere is Banach–Tarski-paradoxical and the area that defines probability is unique only on measurable sets; measurability is thus a load-bearing physical constraint, non-trivial precisely because the surface is non-amenable. Gleason's theorem then forces the Born measure, but only at Hilbert dimension ≥ 3 — the single qubit (ℂP¹ = S²) is doubly deficient (first non-amenable sphere *and* where Gleason fails). **Ceiling (dynamical/least-action):** the stability of the action's bound-state extrema gives V''(r₀) ∝ (3 − d), so stable orbits and atoms require spatial dimension d ≤ 3; with propagating gravity (absent below d = 3) this is a self-contained dynamical bracket d_space = 3. **The key result:** these two requirements are *disjoint on one manifold* — stability needs manifold dim ≤ 3 (Hilbert n ≤ 2, Gleason-deficient), while Gleason-forced Born needs n ≥ 3 (manifold real-dim ≥ 4, dynamically unstable). No manifold does both. A surface ontology that wants *both* a stable space *and* a forced (not posited) Born rule is therefore compelled to separate an **external dynamical sector** (spatial S³) from an **internal Hilbert sector** (the generations). This two-sector necessity, derived here from dynamics-plus-measure, is **independently realised** by the established octonionic-origin construction, in which the external sector is the quaternionic subalgebra ℍ ⊂ 𝕆 (S³ ≅ SU(2)_L) and the internal generation sector is the exceptional Jordan algebra J₃(𝕆) — two distinct features of one octonionic root. The number 3 is *not* forced by this line; what is (conditionally) forced is the two-sector *architecture*.

## 1. The question

The surface-ontology axiom is silent on why the surface has the dimension it does, and on why space and the internal/generation structure are separate sectors. This note finds that the separation is not a modelling choice but a consequence of two requirements that cannot be jointly met on one manifold. Throughout we keep three "dimensions" distinct — spatial d_space (S³), state-space/Hilbert d_H (qubit ℂ²), and the sphere dimension in the amenability argument — and flag every crossing, since conflating them is the standard way a dimension argument fools itself.

## 2. The floor: measurability is load-bearing because the Bloch sphere is non-amenable

SO(2) (the circle) is abelian, hence amenable: invariant length exists on all subsets, no paradox. SO(3) (the 2-sphere) **contains a free group F₂** — generated by rotations through arccos(1/3) about two perpendicular axes (verified numerically: 1456 reduced non-trivial words of length ≤ 6 all sit ≥ 0.13 from the identity). A non-amenable rotation group makes the sphere paradoxical (Hausdorff–Banach–Tarski): finitely many non-measurable pieces reassemble two spheres of equal area. The switch flips once, at S²/SO(3), and stays flipped. Hence the invariant area that defines probability is unique **only on measurable sets**: measurability is a physical constraint preventing "probability from nothing," non-trivial precisely because the surface is non-amenable.

## 3. From invariant area to the Born rule: Gleason, and the deficient qubit

Gleason's theorem forces the Born measure for Hilbert dimension ≥ 3, and **fails at dimension 2** — the qubit, ℂP¹ = S². The bare qubit is thus **doubly deficient**: first non-amenable sphere *and* the dimension where Born is not forced. Forcing Born requires d_H ≥ 3, supplied by the generations, not the bare Hopf qubit. (Floor: d_H ≥ 3.)

## 4. The ceiling: least action forbids spatial dimension above three

For a central potential V ∝ −r^{2−d}, the effective potential's circular-orbit curvature satisfies **V''(r₀) ∝ (3 − d)**: a stable minimum exists only for d < 4, i.e. d_space ≤ 3 (d = 3 Kepler/stable; d = 4 marginal; d ≥ 5 unstable). With the requirement that gravity propagate (no local degrees of freedom below d = 3), this is a self-contained dynamical bracket d_space = 3 (Ehrenfest–Tangherlini–Whitrow).

## 5. The result: the two requirements are disjoint, forcing a two-sector split

For Hilbert ℂⁿ the pure-state manifold is ℂP^{n−1} (real dim 2(n−1)); Gleason forces Born ⟺ n ≥ 3; least-action stability holds ⟺ manifold dim ≤ 3:

| n | state-sphere S^{2n−1} dim | Gleason | dynamically stable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 (qubit) | 3 | FAILS | stable |
| 3 | 5 | FORCED | unstable |

**No n gives both.** Stability ⇒ dim ≤ 3 ⇒ n ≤ 2 ⇒ Gleason fails; Gleason ⇒ n ≥ 3 ⇒ dim ≥ 4 ⇒ unstable. The requirements are disjoint on one manifold. *(The earlier hope — that the Hopf map S³ ⊂ ℂ² unifies the dynamical and quantum threes into one forced integer — fails: the Hopf map lands the spatial 3 on the qubit n = 2, exactly the Gleason-deficient side. The threes are different in type — a manifold dimension dim(S³) = dim SU(2) versus a representation/algebra structure — and coincide only in value.)*

> **Two-sector necessity.** A surface ontology demanding both a dynamically stable space (dim ≤ 3) and a *forced* Born rule (d_H ≥ 3, manifold real-dim ≥ 4) cannot place both on one manifold. It must split into an **external dynamical sector** (spatial S³, stable, Gleason-deficient qubit) and an **internal Hilbert sector** (the generations, Gleason-forced, no stability burden).

**Conditionality (honest):** this necessity assumes (i) Born-forcing routed through Gleason, and (ii) the rigid QM-is-geometry reading in which the dynamical manifold is the qubit state-space sphere. Both hold as NTE is written; relax either and the necessity weakens to a strong consistency. Not banked as an unconditional theorem.
