There is a fact buried in your physics textbook that almost every physicist notices, and almost every physicist sets aside.

A photon of light — any photon, ever — experiences zero time between the moment it's created and the moment it's absorbed. Not very little time. Zero. Every physicist knows this. It follows directly from Einstein's special relativity, one of the most tested theories in all of science. And then, almost universally, physicists set it aside and get on with their calculations.

## A Note on Method

This paper is not a claim that NTE is the correct theory of fundamental physics. It's a working bet — the kind of bet Feynman described in *QED* when he asked why all theories of physics look so similar in structure. He named three possibilities. The first is that physicists are limited in their imagination, fitting new phenomena into frameworks they already have. The second is that Nature genuinely has only one way of doing things and repeats her story. The third is that apparent similarities are because we are seeing fingers of the same hand.

NTE is built on a working bet that the second and third are operative — that when multiple mathematical descriptions agree on a phenomenon, the agreement is not coincidence but evidence of a common underlying structure, and that the task of theoretical unification is to find the structure rather than to choose between the descriptions.

This is a bet, not a law. It can be wrong. The framework's success or failure will be measured the way any theoretical framework is measured: predictions that hold, derivations that survive scrutiny, and the willingness of the structure to keep simplifying as new observations come in. Beauty — the sense that a description is finding its natural form — is a useful guide to which path to try first. It's not a shortcut past empirical test.

One methodological commitment is worth stating plainly at the outset, because it governs how every number in this paper should be read. NTE does not fit numbers. Every quantity in the framework is either *structural* — fixed by the geometry or the algebra, with no dial to turn — or else *honestly scoped*: labelled as conditional, as a cross-check, or as open. No quantity is assembled to land on a measured value, and no correction is bolted on after the fact to close a gap. When a result matches observation, the match is either forced by the structure or it is flagged as a consistency rather than a derivation. When a result does not yet close, the paper says so. This discipline is what makes the working bet testable rather than decorative.

The rest of this paper is what the bet has produced so far.

## The Clue Nobody Followed Up

Here is the thing about photons and time. Photons are massless. They have zero mass. And it turns out that every single entity in the universe that is massless experiences zero time. Not approximately zero. Exactly zero.

Conversely: every single entity that has mass accumulates time as it moves through the universe. The more mass, the more deeply it's embedded in the flow of time.

This is not a coincidence. The Standard Model — the most successful theory of fundamental physics ever constructed — has a mechanism that gives particles their mass. It's called the Higgs field. And here is the remarkable fact: the mass–proper-time correlation is exact. Every particle that couples to the Higgs field experiences time; every particle that does not couple does not. NTE reads this as the surface signature of a coordinated cascade at gauge birth (see below): the Standard Model Higgs governs mass; a separate boundary expansion field, written |Φ_NTE|, governs proper-time accumulation. The question is: what does this mean?

The answer **Non-Temporal Emergence (NTE)** — the **Null-Time Extension to the Standard Model** — proposes is structural. The Standard Model Higgs continues to do its specific job of giving mass to fermions through Yukawa coupling. The two fields' roles are correlated, but they are not identical. The reason every particle with mass also experiences time, and the photon with no mass also has no time, is that both fields acquired their identities at the same moment in cosmic history — the *gauge birth* — and the assignment of couplings to particles happened in a single cascade event that coordinated both. A particle's coupling to the Standard Model Higgs (its mass) and its engagement with |Φ_NTE| (its proper-time accumulation) were set by the same place in that cascade. A particle that ended up at the zero-coupling place was assigned zero on both sides — that particle is the photon. A particle that ended up at the high-coupling place was assigned high values on both — those are the heavy quarks. The correlation we observe between mass and proper-time accumulation is the structural signature of a coordinated cascade, not the consequence of a single coupling.

This is the framework's key disclosure: NTE adds *one new scalar field* to the universe's geometric description, and that field is *not* the Standard Model Higgs. The Standard Model Higgs continues to do everything the Standard Model says it does. The framework's contribution is to recognise that there is a second, geometric scalar — |Φ_NTE| — playing a role the standard description does not register, and that the empirical correlation between mass and time is the surface signature of a structural coordination at the gauge birth.

What NTE adds to existing physics is *ontology* — a structural account of where things come from — not a replacement of the Standard Model's machinery.

## The Universe as an Expanding Surface

To understand what this means, let's build a picture of the universe from scratch.

Forget the idea that the universe began in a point and expanded into some pre-existing void. In NTE, our entire three-dimensional universe is the *surface* of a four-dimensional sphere — a closed three-dimensional space curved in a way our visual intuition cannot directly picture, but which is mathematically straightforward.

The closest everyday analogy is a balloon, but it has to be used carefully. Imagine an ant on the surface of an inflating balloon. The ant has access only to the surface. It can walk in any direction along the surface; it cannot move "up" off the surface or "down" into the surface. For the ant, the surface is the entire universe. Distant points on the balloon recede from each other as the balloon inflates, with no centre of expansion that the ant can point to from within the surface. Every point looks central; expansion is happening everywhere.

The analogy carries us most of the way. Where it fails — and this is critical — is that an actual rubber balloon has an *inside*, full of air. We can see this inside; the ant cannot. NTE makes a stronger claim than that the inside is inaccessible: it asserts that the inside *is not a place at all*. The mathematical description of our universe involves a four-dimensional geometric structure whose surface is what we live on, but the four-dimensional "interior" is not a region of space we could in principle reach with better instruments. There is no inside to point to. There is only the surface, and the history of how the surface has expanded.

To see why this is more than a denial, consider what happens at the limit where our universe's curvature becomes very gentle — locally, where the radius of the four-sphere is enormous. The surface looks flat. From within a flat region of the surface, the directions "up out of the surface" and "down into the surface" become indistinguishable: there is no preferred orientation, just two equally-available sides of the same locally-flat sheet. This is what NTE means by saying the bilateral structure of the universe is two *orientations* of one manifold rather than two regions of space. The surface has, in effect, two sides — like the two sides of a sheet of paper — and the two sides are physically real but neither is "inside" the other. We will return to this when we discuss the manifold's thickness in the next section.

The expansion of the surface does not happen *through* time. The expansion *is* the fundamental process, measured not in seconds but in a dimensionless count of how many times the universe has doubled in size. This count is called the e-fold number N, and it plays the role that time plays in standard cosmology — except that it requires no clocks, no pre-existing temporal background, nothing but geometry.

Now the boundary expansion field enters. The relationship is compact: the local expansion rate ties to the field through **H = β_NTE|Φ_NTE|²**, where β_NTE is a constant of the framework. Deeper engagement with |Φ_NTE| means deeper embedding in time — gravitational time dilation in everyday language. Clocks tick faster in deep space than on the surface of a dense star, because on the star's surface the boundary expansion field is slightly weaker (responding to the strong curvature) and corresponds to slightly less time per expansion step.

A clarifying note on how the framework states this relationship. The paper will sometimes use production-flavoured language for the central equation H = β_NTE|Φ_NTE|² — phrases like "the field that gives time," "governs the rate at which proper time accumulates," or "the universe's expansion produces time." This language is pedagogically useful, and the reader is welcome to carry it as a working picture. The framework's fundamental statement, however, is more careful: H = β_NTE|Φ_NTE|² is a *geometric constraint* relating two quantities in the manifold's structure — the local expansion rate and the local field strength — and the proper-time coordinate t is defined by integrating the inverse of this constraint along the e-fold history. The relation t(N) is a proportionality definition, not a separate dynamical law. The production-flavoured language is an effective description; the framework's underlying claim is that time and the boundary expansion field are two readings of the same geometric structure, related by the constraint. Two readings, one structure.

## Why We Are Not Travelling at the Speed of Light

This brings us to a subtle but important point that often confuses readers of cosmological models. If the universe is the surface of an expanding sphere, and the expansion is the fundamental process, what is the speed at which the surface itself is expanding?

The answer is: the speed of light. Exactly the speed of light, by definition rather than coincidence. In NTE, c is not merely a property of photons but a geometric property of the boundary itself — it's the rate at which the surface of the universe expands per unit of accumulated time. The constancy of c, which has puzzled physicists since Einstein, becomes natural in this picture: the speed of light is constant because it *is* the expansion rate of the manifold, and the manifold has one expansion rate.

So if the surface is expanding at c, and we are on the surface, why are we not moving at c?

The resolution is that we are not exactly on the wavefront of the expansion. Photons are. Photons have zero engagement with the boundary expansion field — and, by the same structural fact, zero Yukawa coupling to the Standard Model Higgs — and therefore zero embedding into the surface; they ride the wavefront, co-moving with it at c, and accumulate no time along the way. Massive particles are *embedded* in the surface to varying depths, set by their engagement with |Φ_NTE|, and the embedding holds them slightly behind the wavefront. We — made of atoms with their full complement of mass and proper-time engagement — are embedded enough that we move with the local geometry of our region rather than racing outward at c. The wavefront has gone on ahead.

What we observe in the night sky is consistent with this picture in a striking way. Distant galaxies do recede from us, with the most distant ones receding faster than the nearer ones. At a specific distance — the *Hubble radius*, currently about 14.4 billion light-years — galaxies recede from us at exactly the speed of light. Beyond that, galaxies recede *faster* than the speed of light. This is not a violation of relativity; it's the geometric fact that the surface of the universe is expanding at c, and parts of the surface that are far enough away from us are riding the wavefront in directions away from us.

We are at the centre of our own observable universe, with the wavefront racing outward in every direction. Photons that left distant sources long ago, on their own wavefront paths, are now reaching us. We see them precisely because, from their frame, the journey took no time at all — they have been on the wavefront the whole way, while the universe and we have continued to expand around them.

## One Manifold, Two Orientations

The expansion started from a boundary point — the Janus boundary, named after the two-faced Roman god. At this moment, the universe acquired its bilateral structure. The natural mental picture here would be two separate expanding surfaces — our universe and a mirror universe, expanding in opposite directions. The framework's geometric reading is sharper than that: there is one manifold with a *thickness* along the e-fold direction, and the two universes are the two orientations of that thickness.

The thickness is not a new dimension. It's the same e-fold count N that already parameterises expansion. What the framework's reading makes explicit is that this count has a definite *extent*, and the extent varies with mass-energy density: where the boundary expansion field |Φ_NTE| is locally weaker (in a gravitational potential well, for example), more N is consumed per unit observable time, and the manifold has greater N-thickness in that region. This is gravitational time dilation, viewed from a different angle: dilation is the time-side measure of how the manifold's N-thickness varies pointwise.

The two orientations occupy this thickness from opposite directions. They share the boundary at N = 0 that neither orientation occupies. Each has its own matter content. From either orientation, the universe looks complete and self-consistent. From the other, it looks CPN-conjugate (matter, parity, and e-fold direction all reversed). Neither orientation is privileged; we happen to occupy one of them, by a topological selection in the geometry.

This is not a new idea in spirit — versions of bilateral or "two-headed" universe models have been proposed by cosmologists including Barbour, Koslowski & Mercati (who identified a gravitational arrow of time emerging from a time-symmetric initial condition) and Boyle & Turok (who developed the CPT-symmetric universe proposal). ## Entanglement: the Photon's Trick on a Larger Scale

The photon's null-time was the first clue. If emission and absorption can be the same event on the expansion wavefront, the same geometry may work for entities that are not photons.

The answer the framework gives is yes, and the name for it is *quantum entanglement*.

When two particles are prepared in an entangled state, then separated — sometimes by metres in a laboratory, sometimes in principle by light-years — measurements made on one of them turn out to be correlated with measurements made on the other. The correlations are not what you would expect if each particle carried its measurement outcomes around with it as a hidden property. They follow specific patterns, mathematically described, and the patterns have been confirmed in laboratory experiments thousands of times since the 1980s. The 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded to three of the experimenters who confirmed it most carefully.

The standard interpretation of this fact has tied physicists in knots for ninety years. Either the particles are communicating across whatever distance separates them (which seems to violate relativity), or they are not really connected and the correlations are some kind of measurement artefact (which is hard to reconcile with the precision of the experimental results). Both options have generated reams of philosophical literature. Neither has produced a picture a physics student can hold in their head without disclaimers.

The framework's claim is that the standard interpretations have accepted the wrong starting assumption: that there are two particles. Once we relax that assumption, the strangeness dissolves.

The two entangled particles are not two particles connected by some mysterious link. They are two endpoints of a single geometric object — the same kind of object the photon is, scaled up to a context where it has internal structure rather than being a single line through spacetime. The object lives on the surface of the universe and connects two locations the same way the photon connects emission to absorption: by being on the wavefront the whole time, with no proper time accumulated along the way, with no need for anything to travel between the endpoints because they are aspects of one structure rather than two separate things needing to coordinate.

The "spookiness" was an artefact of treating the two particles as separated entities that needed to communicate. They are not separated in the relevant sense. They are endpoints of a single zero-proper-time feature on the surface of the universe. The correlations between them are not transmitted; they are *properties of the shape* both endpoints belong to. No information moves faster than light because no information moves at all. The shape was always there.

There is an additional structural reading here. Bell's theorem gives an upper bound on the correlations any local theory can produce: the bound is 2. Quantum mechanics violates it, but only up to a specific value, 2√2, called the *Tsirelson bound*. No experiment has ever produced correlations stronger than 2√2. In the standard treatment, this number falls out of Hilbert-space mathematics with no further explanation. In the framework's geometric reading, 2√2 is what you get from the angular overlap of states on the three-sphere, projected through the Hopf fibration. The framework reads the number as sphere geometry; that experiments saturate it in carefully prepared entangled systems is consistent with the underlying state space being what the framework claims — not, by itself, proof of it.

## The Particle That Bridges Both Orientations

The neutrino is the strangest particle in the Standard Model. It has a tiny mass — a fraction of a millionth of an electron's mass. It barely interacts with anything. It passes through the entire Earth as easily as if the Earth were not there.

In the NTE picture, the neutrino's strangeness has a geometric explanation. Every other massive particle has gone through a process that pins it firmly to one orientation of the manifold's thickness: it has a definite matter or antimatter identity, a definite electric charge, a definite relationship to everything else. The geometric operation that performs this pinning is the *Hopf-projection completion* — the topological collapse, at the gauge-birth cascade, that takes a pre-temporal mode living on the three-sphere and projects it onto a definite point of identity. For every other massive species, this projection completed; their post-locking wavefunctions are confined to one orientation of the manifold's thickness.

The neutrino's projection did not complete. The framework's reading sharpens what this means: the cause of non-completion is *topological*, not a matter of small Yukawa coupling. The neutrino's Yukawa coupling to the Standard Model Higgs is small (around 10⁻¹²), but it's large enough to satisfy the locking condition the cascade applies. What prevents the projection from finishing is something else — the unresolved Majorana character of the species, with its particle–antiparticle binary not yet topologically resolved. The geometric operation that would create the particle/antiparticle distinction has no target to act on, and the projection terminates incomplete.

What survives in the incomplete state is what makes the neutrino strange. The wavefunction remains in a *superposition* of structural configurations that the completed projection would have distinguished — specifically, a superposition of what physicists call left-handed and right-handed chirality, with both amplitudes present at once. This is the framework's structural reading of the textbook label "Majorana neutrino": neither particle nor antiparticle, because the Hopf projection that would create the binary has not completed (Companion 7); not a single particle identified with its antiparticle. The question "is this neutrino a particle or its antiparticle?" presupposes a completed projection the species does not have; the question does not apply.

The bilateral consequence follows. A species whose Hopf-projection did not complete has its wavefunction supported not on one orientation of the manifold's thickness but across both. Its stress-energy sources curvature on both orientations simultaneously. The unresolved neutrino's wavefunction lives across the thickness. It's the framework's only species in our universe with this property — its non-resolution is what gives it bilateral support, and bilateral support is what makes it the structure on which cross-thickness gravity acts.

A note about an apparent puzzle the chirality picture raises. If the neutrino's underlying configuration is a symmetric superposition of left-handed and right-handed amplitudes, why do the weak-interaction experiments performed in our universe see only left-handed neutrinos, never right-handed ones? The framework's answer is that the two orientations of the manifold sample the chirality superposition at *opposite phases* of the underlying rhythm — our orientation's weak force catches the configuration at the phase where the left-handed half is forward; the conjugate orientation's mirror weak force, if we could observe it, would catch the same configuration at the opposite phase, where the right-handed half is forward. Same underlying configuration; opposite phase samples. The strong handedness asymmetry the Standard Model sees is the asymmetry between two phase-samples of one underlying symmetric configuration, not an asymmetry in the configuration itself. This gives the framework a structural reason for parity violation in the weak sector that the Standard Model's account treats as a brute empirical fact.

This makes the neutrino a *cross-thickness bridge* — a species whose geometric structure straddles the two orientations of the manifold and lets the gravitational field of one orientation register in the other.

Here is the prediction that gives NTE its sharpest experimental edge.

This is dark matter.

Dark matter is not a new particle hiding in the darkness of our universe, waiting to be detected in an underground laboratory. ## The Arrow of Time

Physicists have asked for over a century why the past is fixed and the future open, while the fundamental equations treat past and future alike.

The fundamental equations of physics — quantum mechanics, general relativity, the Standard Model — do not distinguish between past and future. They are time-symmetric: a movie of any process that obeys these equations could be played backwards and would still describe a physically valid process. And yet our experience of time is asymmetric in the strongest possible way. The future is open and undetermined; the past is fixed and unalterable. We remember backwards, not forwards. Eggs break, they do not unbreak. The arrow of time points one way, decisively.

The standard explanation for this asymmetry, going back to Boltzmann in the nineteenth century, is statistical: entropy increases because there are vastly more disordered states than ordered ones, so any closed system tends statistically toward disorder over time. This is a real explanation as far as it goes, but it explains the *direction* of time only by assuming that the universe started in an extraordinarily low-entropy state. The deep question — why the initial state was so special — is then pushed back to cosmology, where it remains unresolved.

NTE offers a different account. Time does not have a direction because of statistics. Time has a direction because of geometry, and the geometric mechanism is the locking transition we have been describing.

When the early universe crossed N_lock — the e-fold at which gauge symmetry switched on, the boundary expansion field |Φ_NTE| stabilised, and the Standard Model Higgs acquired its identity as the SU(2) gauge doublet — every massive particle in the universe committed itself to one orientation of the manifold's N-thickness. The commitment was geometric and irreversible: each species, at the moment its embedding depth crossed the locking threshold, anchored itself to one side of the thickness, and became thereafter bound to accumulate proper time only in that orientation's +N direction. This commitment is what produces the arrow of time.

The arrow points the way it does, in our orientation, because every particle around us — every atom we are made of, every photon emitted in our region, every interaction we observe — is locked to the same orientation we are. Their accumulated proper time accumulates only in our +N direction. The CPN-conjugate orientation has its own arrow of time, pointing in its own +N direction, which from our perspective looks like our −N direction; but our orientation cannot cross to it without traversing the Janus boundary, which it will never do.

This matches the book's **was / now / next** framing rather than the textbook entropic story alone. **Was** is written in the geometry — the e-folds the universe has expanded through are part of the manifold's structure, not erased by subsequent expansion. **Next** is what the manifold has not yet expanded into — genuinely open, not a hidden script already laid down. Time has a direction not because disorder is favoured but because the matter-content of our universe was anchored, at the moment of the gauge birth, to one orientation of the geometric thickness, and that anchoring has never been undone.

This account is consistent with the entropic story — the universe still does start in a low-entropy state, and entropy still does increase with N — but it locates the deeper origin of the arrow in geometry rather than statistics. The low-entropy initial state was the consequence of the locking transition, not its cause. And the irreversibility of the arrow is the irreversibility of geometric commitment, not of statistical evolution.

## Why Quantum Mechanics Is Geometry

The picture gets stranger, and then it gets cleaner.

The state of a quantum particle — the full description of its physical possibilities — lives on a geometric object called a three-sphere. The framework's reading of the universe places the same kind of three-sphere structure at the heart of cosmology: the universe's boundary is a three-sphere with a U(1) phase to be quotiented through the Hopf fibration, just as the quantum state space is. The two contexts share the same Hopf-fibration apparatus, even if the framework does not claim they are *literally identified* as one geometric object playing both roles. The Born rule — the quantum mechanical formula that tells you the probability of a measurement outcome, first written down in 1926 and treated as an axiom ever since — turns out to follow from the geometry of this sphere, in either context.

The argument is almost embarrassingly simple in shape, once you see it (although the rigorous version, developed in Companion 2 of the NTE corpus, requires more mathematical detail than this paper can carry). The state of a quantum particle is a point on a three-sphere. Two points on that sphere that differ only by an overall phase factor (a rotation by an undetectable angle) represent the same physical state — this is the unobservability of global phase, built into quantum mechanics from the beginning. The geometric structure that implements this identification is the Hopf fibration: a specific way of projecting the three-sphere onto a two-sphere, discovered by the mathematician Heinz Hopf in 1931.

The probability formula **P = |⟨ψ|φ⟩|²** — the Born rule, the thing every quantum mechanics student learns to compute — is the only smooth, symmetric, properly normalised function you can write on the Hopf-projected sphere. At the level of mathematical detail beyond the scope of this paper, it can be shown to be not a postulate but a consequence of the geometry of the state space.

Why is the exponent 2 and not 3 or 4? Because the defining equation of a sphere in complex space is quadratic (|z₁|² + |z₂|² = 1), and the probability rule must respect this constraint. The square comes from the sphere. Recent technical work sharpens the scope: once gauge birth activates the U(1) fibre and the Hopf quotient is in play, admissible probabilities must be linear in |z₁|², not in |z₁| — the square is forced by the level-set geometry, not imported as a separate "amplitude squared" axiom. What remains open — and the framework says so plainly — is deriving complex structure itself from real, pre-gauge-birth data without assuming ℂ upfront. The physical mechanism is in place; a derivation from less is not.

## The Moment Physics Came Into Being

The framework's most dramatic point is the origin of time itself.

When the universe had not yet expanded beyond a tiny fraction of an e-fold — when the expansion count N was near zero — there was no time, in the sense that no entity had yet accumulated any. This is not a metaphysical claim but a mathematical one: temporal duration accumulates at a rate set by the boundary expansion field |Φ_NTE|, and in the earliest expansion, |Φ_NTE| had not yet stabilised into a form that gives a well-defined dt/dN. The Standard Model Higgs as we know it — the SU(2) gauge doublet that gives mass through Yukawa coupling — did not yet exist either, because the gauge structure within which it is defined had not yet emerged. Both fields' identities are products of the locking transition that makes Standard Model physics operate.

Without photons, there is no electromagnetism. Without electromagnetism, there are no atoms, no stars, no galaxies, no us.

## The Algebraic Structure of Creation

If you have been paying attention, you may have noticed that the framework keeps encountering the three-sphere and its properties. The quantum state space is a three-sphere. The universe's boundary is a three-sphere. The dark-matter calculation uses the Hopf fibration of the three-sphere. The Born rule comes from the topology of the three-sphere.

This convergence has a deep explanation. There is a result in pure mathematics — proved by Hurwitz in 1898 and completed in full generality in the 1950s and 1960s — that says there are exactly four number systems with the properties required for quantum-mechanical amplitudes: the real numbers, the complex numbers, the quaternions, and the octonions. Only four. Not an infinite family, not even a large finite family. Four.

The octonions are the strangest of these. Unlike the others, multiplication in the octonions is not associative: you can get a different answer depending on which multiplication you perform first. (Multiplication of ordinary numbers is associative — (2 × 3) × 4 gives the same result as 2 × (3 × 4) — and most readers have never met a multiplication that wasn't.) This seems like a defect, but in NTE it's a feature. In the pre-temporal epoch — where there is no time, and therefore no temporal ordering of operations — there is no reason for multiplication to be associative. The lack of temporal ordering means you literally cannot say whether operation A came before operation B, so the question of whether (A times B) times C equals A times (B times C) has no physical content.

The pre-temporal universe is octonionic. Its symmetry group is called G₂ — the smallest of the exceptional Lie groups that mathematicians discovered in the nineteenth century. The technical name does not matter for the present purpose; what matters is that this group is fully classified by mathematics, and its properties are completely known.

At the gauge birth, causality appears. Time gives ordering to operations. The algebra is forced to become associative — there now *is* a fact of the matter about which operation happened first. With associativity restored, the octonions can no longer be the algebra of physics. The next-richest associative algebra in the Hurwitz hierarchy is the quaternions, and the framework's claim is that the gauge birth is precisely the algebraic reduction from octonions to quaternions, mediated by the emergence of temporal order.

The remarkable thing — and this is where the framework's structural ambition is most visible — is what falls out of this reduction. When the octonionic G₂ symmetry breaks under the constraint that operations must now be ordered, the residual symmetry that survives is exactly SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1). This is the Standard Model gauge group: the symmetry structure of the strong force, the weak force, and electromagnetism. Every gauge boson, every quark, every charged lepton — the entire interacting machinery of the Standard Model — operates under this specific symmetry group, and it's what the framework reads as emerging when octonions reduce to quaternions under the appearance of time.

This is not obvious, and it is not solely NTE's discovery. That the division algebras encode the Standard Model gauge group and its representations is an established line of work (see the related-work section below); NTE's specific contribution is the *reduction mechanism* — the claim that the octonion-to-quaternion step is driven by the emergence of temporal ordering at gauge birth. ## Hubble Tension, and Dark Energy Naked

There are two recent observational puzzles in cosmology that earlier popular accounts of this framework treated as one story. They are not. The void mechanism helps with one; it does not rescue the other.

The first is the Hubble tension. The expansion rate of the universe today, the Hubble parameter H₀, can be measured by two methods. The first uses observations of the cosmic microwave background — the relic radiation from when the universe was 380,000 years old — and standard cosmology to back-calculate the present-day expansion rate. The second uses observations of supernovae and other "standard candles" in the relatively local universe to measure the expansion rate directly. The two methods should give the same answer. They do not. The CMB-based method gives about 67 km/s/Mpc; the local supernova method gives about 73 km/s/Mpc. The discrepancy is several standard deviations and has resisted explanation for nearly a decade.

In the framework's picture, the local rate of cosmic expansion at any point is set by the local strength of |Φ_NTE|, through H = β_NTE|Φ_NTE|². Most of the universe by volume is in cosmic voids — vast regions essentially empty of matter. In voids, |Φ_NTE| is at its asymptotic value. In matter-rich regions like the surroundings of galaxies, |Φ_NTE| is slightly suppressed by the gravitational potential. (This is one of the structural differences between |Φ_NTE| and the Standard Model Higgs the reader knows from CERN: the Standard Model Higgs is locked at 246 GeV everywhere by the steepness of its potential and does not vary with local mass-energy density. The boundary expansion field, by contrast, *does* vary spatially and cosmologically, and that variation is what the framework reads as the source of gravitational time dilation.) Because most of the universe is voids, supernovae we observe at low redshift are mostly seen *through* voids — through regions where |Φ_NTE|² is at its higher asymptotic value, which means the locally observed expansion rate is slightly higher than the cosmic mean. This produces the elevated H₀ measured by local supernova surveys. The CMB-derived value, which integrates over the whole expansion history rather than being biased toward void lines of sight, gives the true cosmic mean. That part of the story still stands: the void bias is correctly signed for the Hubble tension, and it remains testable (directional scatter in local H₀; brighter supernovae along void-dominated lines of sight).

The framework's sharpest current disagreement with observation is dark energy. It predicts that dark energy is exactly constant — an equation of state of precisely minus one, with no freedom to adjust. Through 2025–2026 the DESI survey, combined with the microwave background, began to prefer a dark energy that changes over time — roughly three standard deviations away from constant in the supernova-independent combination. There is no internal dial the framework can turn to absorb this, and it does not pretend otherwise. The framework has its own mechanism involving cosmic voids, and one might hope to recruit it to explain the DESI signal; but when that mechanism is actually run forward it pushes the wrong way, and it cannot touch the part of the data that carries the signal. So the framework says so, and lets the prediction stand naked. The verdict will come between 2027 and 2029, from DESI's full survey and from Euclid and Rubin. If the evolving-dark-energy signal firms up, this prediction fails — cleanly, and on the record. (The void mechanism does still make a testable mark of its own: supernovae seen along void-dominated lines of sight should look slightly brighter. Finding that would test the mechanism; it would not rescue the constant-dark-energy prediction.)
