## 1. PC's sharp question

> Can "no emitter" be given content on a locus with no event structure, without smuggling in a past light cone?

**No — and what gets smuggled is worse than a light cone.**

"An absorption with no emission" needs an absorption event. An absorption event needs an incoming photon. A photon with no emitter in its own aeon is, in ordinary terms, *incoming radiation given as initial data*. So to instantiate the emitter half at the daughter's origin you need a photon that arrives in the daughter without having been emitted there. That is **the mode crossing the interface**.

The paper ratified the opposite. v0.7 §6: "What crosses the interface is not energy, and … not the mode either." §7: the photon language "belongs to the parent side and is not carried over." §12.1 records that reading the seed as matter content recollapses the daughter. The v0.5 recast exists to stop exactly this object from crossing.

So E1 requires the thing the recast excludes. It is closed by the paper's own ruling, whatever one decides about cones.

Two supporting points from the deposit:
- Companion 5 §3: "before locking, photons do not exist." At N = 0 there is no absorption event either. It is not an absorption missing its emitter; **both halves are absent**. There is nothing for the symmetry to attach to.
- Part 2 §8.3: at gauge birth the energy difference "is radiated as gauge bosons." The daughter's first photons have emitters in the daughter.

PC's "the transaction that cannot complete at I⁺ is the transaction that cannot begin at N = 0" is a single photon spanning two aeons. That is Penrose's global picture, where radiation does pass through. Companion 21 §9 defines NTE's variant by departing from it.

## 2. E3 — no second trigger class inside an aeon

PC's argument is that the past light cone gets denser, so emitters are never scarce. Correct. There is a more basic reason: every photon that is absorbed either has a source or was present as initial data, and in NTE the initial photons are radiated at gauge birth. An emitterless absorption is not a rare event in a live aeon; it is not an event.

## 3. E4 — the crediting is over-committed, and on the absorber side too

This is the part I did not expect.

In the transactional interpretation as I understand it, an offer wave with no confirmation does not produce a stranded photon. **No transaction means no emission.** Wheeler–Feynman is the same: the emitter radiates only by virtue of the absorber's response, and the theory's standard results assume the future universe absorbs completely. A universe where late photons have no absorbers — Companion 21's exact setting — is the case in which that theory says the emitter does not radiate in the ordinary way at all. I recall a literature on absorber theory in expanding universes that turns on this; I have not sourced it today.

Companion 21 §4 uses the phrase the other way: "Half a transaction: an emission with no absorption", meaning a photon that exists and never closes. On the credited theory that object does not exist.

§4's "what NTE takes and what it does not" paragraph disclaims Cramer's account of measurement. It does not disclaim this. So the honest crediting is narrower than "the incomplete-transaction criterion": NTE takes the *image* of an unclosed exchange and applies it to ordinary retarded photons that the credited theories would say were never emitted. A referee who knows Cramer will raise this before the emitter question.

**If this holds when sourced, it is a wording-and-crediting change to §4, not a change to the mechanism.** The trigger is defined in §3 by a photon count and does not need Cramer.
