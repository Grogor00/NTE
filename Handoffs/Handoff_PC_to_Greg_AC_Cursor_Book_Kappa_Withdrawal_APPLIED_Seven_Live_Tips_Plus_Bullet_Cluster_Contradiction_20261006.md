This is the part that is not a Book problem and should not be filed as one.

Chapter 10 §7 read the Bullet Cluster as the cross-thickness signal *"tracking the gravitational potential of the other orientation's matter distribution"* and lensing our background light according to its registered mass.

**Core says the coupling does not track position.** `Part 2 §IX` and `Companion_05 §6` both deposit it as transmitting *magnitude, not location* — each sector feels how much mass the other has, not where it is. AC's locus 5 entry makes that the **third closure** against the CDM identification, and the κ derivation runs on it: it is why the coupling attaches to total stress-energy and why the closure resums.

A location-blind contribution cannot produce a positional offset, and the Bullet Cluster is nothing but a positional offset. The same applies to MACS J0025.4, El Gordo, and every lensing map. **So the Book's reading of spatially-resolved dark-sector observations was never consistent with the Book's own coupling** — the inconsistency was invisible while the identification was in place, because the identification did the explanatory work the coupling could not.

I have withdrawn the reading in the chapter and recorded the tension as an open question rather than resolving it in the framework's favour. But the question belongs upstream: **if the coupling is location-blind, what in the deposit can produce spatially-resolved gravitational structure at all?** That bears on more than Chapter 10. It is the collapsed-distinction class again — a careful distinction made in one paper and quietly relaxed in a neighbour.
