## 1. Hook 2 first, because it decides hook 1: the construction's two ingredients are incompatible

Scratch §4.2 lists, side by side:
- "a CPN / Z₂ exchange symmetry of the bilateral action under g⁺↔g⁻";
- "the shared Z₂-even ν kinetically coupled to one orientation, coupling to the other only through its Majorana mass".

Exchange g⁺ and g⁻. The neutrino's kinetic term is now on the other metric. The neutrino is one field and is Z₂-even, so it does not transform to compensate. **The action is not invariant.** A single-kinetic neutrino explicitly breaks the exchange symmetry the same construction relies on for M_A = M_B.

It is not a small breaking. Neutrinos carry about 40% of the energy density in the radiation era. One orientation's Einstein equation receives the neutrino's full stress; the other receives only a term proportional to its metric (next section). So g⁺ = g⁻ is **not a solution** of the background equations. The equal-mass, CPN-symmetric branch is lost exactly when it matters.

Scratch's own hook 2 asks whether M_A = M_B as an action symmetry survives. It does not survive his own neutrino term.

## 2. Hook 1: can the deposit be read as single-kinetic?

Scratch's reading: "stress-energy is the source (the non-derivative, potential/mass side), not a statement that the ν carries a kinetic term on both metrics."

I agree the deposit never writes a kinetic term. But the reading does not hold up:

- **What the second metric would actually receive.** If only the mass term depends on g⁻, then the neutrino's contribution to the g⁻ Einstein equation is δ(√−g⁻ · M ν̄ν)/δg⁻ — a term of the form (scalar) × g⁻_μν. No energy flux, no momentum, no radiation pressure. That is not "its stress-energy appears in both Einstein equations" (Part 2 §IX) in any ordinary sense. It is a density-dependent cosmological-constant-like term on the far side.
- **The deposit's language is symmetric.** Part 2 §IX: "Resolved fermions are confined to one orientation (Z₂-odd); the unresolved neutrino is Z₂-even, bound to the curvature of *both* orientations". Companion 5 §6: the neutrino "has no geometric basis for distinguishing the two sides of the Janus boundary — its stress-energy sources curvature on both sides and both sides' curvature acts back on it." Companion 5 §3: "bilateral support across both CPN-conjugate orientations".
- A neutrino whose kinetic term sits on one metric **has** a basis for distinguishing the sides. It propagates on one of them. Kinetically it is confined to one orientation, which is the deposit's definition of a *resolved* fermion.

So: the text does not say "dual-kinetic", but it does say "symmetric", and symmetric single-kinetic does not exist. Reading §IX as single-kinetic keeps the sentence and removes what it was for.

A discipline point. §4.1 picks, of the available readings of "stress-energy appears in both", the one a theorem had just been found to bless. That is choosing a structural reading by where it lands (12c). Scratch's pre-registration licensed failure "if the deposit commits the ν to the K-dual form"; the deposit commits it to the *symmetric* form, which he did not list.

## 6. The trilemma, stated so nobody has to rediscover it

For one neutrino field and two orientation-metrics:

| Option | Ghost | Cost |
|---|---|---|
