## 0. Why rebuild rather than edit

**Because a rebuild constructs the dependency graph instead of discovering it.**

Every failure in the closure record was found by *reading* — someone opening a file and noticing a claim resting on something withdrawn. That is slow and unreliable, and it kept catching things months late: the Door A action live in Companion 15; κ₀ < 1 propagated into two papers; *"rest mass is Yukawa coupling"* false for seesaw neutrinos and seeding a confusion that ran from June to October.

**Under the rule in §2.1 below, none of those could have been introduced.** That is the whole argument for the method, and it is also why the dependency graph proposed on 2026-09-30 and never built should be a *product* of the rebuild rather than a tool applied to it.

## 2. **The three construction rules**

### **2.1 — Every claim enters with its ground named, and the ground must already be in the corpus.**

**This is the mechanism, not a guideline.** It makes a circular or orphaned dependency *impossible to introduce*, rather than detectable afterwards.

**Test case from the closure record:** *"the neutrino is unresolved, therefore Majorana"* could not have been admitted, because the conjugation axis is a **Majorana-mass fact** — the ground would have had to be the seesaw, and the circularity would have been visible at entry.

### **2.2 — Register at point of entry.**

**Derived / inherited-and-credited / adopted / conjecture.** Decided as each claim is admitted, never retrofitted.

**Most of this month's work was retrofitting registers to claims that had drifted** — the amplifier stayed "derived" after the action that would generate it was withdrawn.

### **2.3 — "This does not rebuild" stays a live outcome at every step.**

**The characteristic risk of a rebuild is re-justification:** you know where you are going, so you accept a step you would have questioned cold.

**If the Born-rule reformulation needs something that is not there, the rebuild must report it rather than route around it.**
