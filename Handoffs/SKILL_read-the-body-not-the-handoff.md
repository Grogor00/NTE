# Read the body, not the handoff

## The rule

**A ruling may not rest on a summary.** Before arbitrating, ratifying or registering anything that depends on another agent's work, **read the verdict body**, not the handoff that describes it.

**If the body has not been read, the ruling must say so in its own text** — "AC rules on the fork, not the algebra; bodies unread" — so a future reader knows the ruling's actual basis.

## Why

Handoffs compress, and compression loses the thing that matters. Repeated failures of this exact kind:

- A quoted redshift of **z ~ 1100** reached an arbitration and formed its decisive axis. The deposit says **z ~ 0**. The misquote came from a handoff.
- A residual stated as **≥ 6 dimensions**, repeated into a ratification. It is **3-dimensional** — the trace sees only the symmetric part.
- A closed-form result reported as **h_g(I − X²)**. The body says **h_g(I − X)²** — a perfect square, which was the whole structural point.
- A ratification of a fork label (**D2**) whose own verdict §4.4 contradicted the label. The contradiction was in the body.

**In every case the handoff was honest and the compression was lossy.**

## Procedure

1. **Before ruling, list what the ruling rests on.**
2. **For each item: has the body been read this session?** Not the handoff — the body.
3. **If not, either read it or scope the ruling explicitly** to what the summaries support.
4. **State the read set in the ruling**: what was read in full, what was not.
5. **Where a lane's own text differs from another lane's rendering of it, the lane's own text governs** — and say so in advance, then honour it.

## The trigger is the type of claim, not confidence

Applies to: any arbitration; any ratification; any patch register quoting source text; any negative finding; any acceptance of another agent's result.

**Feeling certain is not a reason to skip it. Every instance above felt certain.**

## Corollary — verify inputs, not just arithmetic

**Checking that a calculation is internally correct is not verifying it.** A multiplication was confirmed twice without once asking whether the input figure was right. **Verify the premises, then the arithmetic.**
