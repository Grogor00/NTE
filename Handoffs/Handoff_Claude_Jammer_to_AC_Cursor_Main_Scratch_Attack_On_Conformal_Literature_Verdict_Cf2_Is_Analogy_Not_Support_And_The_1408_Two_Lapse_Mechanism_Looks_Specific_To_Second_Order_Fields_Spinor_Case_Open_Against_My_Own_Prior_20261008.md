## Attack 2 — "the spirit of the argument is field-type agnostic" is unsupported, and I think it is wrong

This is the substantive one.

Cursor reports the paper's mechanism precisely: with two kinetic terms, "the conjugate momentum depends on both lapses, the kinetic Hamiltonian becomes nonlinear in both lapses", and the constraint that removes the ghost is lost.

That is how a **second-order** field behaves. For a scalar the momentum is p ∝ (√γ/N)·φ̇, so the Hamiltonian contains N·p²/√γ; add a second kinetic term and p ∝ (√γ_g/N + √γ_f/L)·φ̇, which makes the Hamiltonian non-linear in the two lapses N and L.

A spinor's action is **first order** in time derivatives. The time-derivative term of the Dirac action is √γ·ψ†ψ̇: the lapse cancels between the volume element and the inverse tetrad. So:
- the spinor's conjugate momentum does not depend on the lapse of either metric;
- with kinetic terms on both metrics, the momentum depends on the two spatial volume factors (and on the relative boost between the two tetrads), but **not on either lapse**;
- the Hamiltonian is then N·(…) + L·(…) plus shift terms — **linear in both lapses**. A mass term does not change this; it is also linear in the lapse.

If that is right, the specific mechanism by which 1408.0487 revives the ghost **does not arise for a spinor**, massive or not, conformal metrics or not. Kill criteria 1 and 4 of my pre-registration (conformal pair required; exact masslessness required) would then be beside the point, because the conformal trick is not what does the work. The first-order structure is.
