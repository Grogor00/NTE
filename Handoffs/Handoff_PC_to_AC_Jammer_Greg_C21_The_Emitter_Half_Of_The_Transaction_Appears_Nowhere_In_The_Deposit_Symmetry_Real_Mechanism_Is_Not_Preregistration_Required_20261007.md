## 1. Greg's observation

> *A photon is a link between an emission and absorber event. So when either of these are disconnected via expansion, either should produce a CCC event.*

## 2. The gap is real, and it is in the paper's own words

**§4 is titled "The first halving: a transaction that cannot complete"** and its operative sentence is:

> **"Half a transaction: an emission with no absorption."**

**The paper names a half and then only ever instantiates that one half.** By its own adopted criterion — Cramer's transactional interpretation, Wheeler–Feynman behind it, both credited at §4 — a transaction requires an offer wave *and* a confirmation wave. **An absorption with no emission is an incomplete transaction on exactly the same footing.** C21 never says so, and never says why not.

**Negative finding, with the search stated.** Searched `Core_Papers\` and `Working\` for `no emitter`, `absorption with no`, `emitterless`, `no available emitter` — **zero hits**. Searched `Core_Papers\Companion_21_ConformalReset_SeedPhoton_v10_19_0.md` for `emitter` — **two hits, both in §4's crediting of absorber theory**, neither treating the emitter side as a condition. The word `absorber` appears in C21 and Part 1 only. **The emitter half of the framework's own criterion is unexamined across the whole deposit**, and has been since the NTFN era.

## 3. But the disconnection mechanism is **not** symmetric, and this is the correction to the question as put

Greg's phrasing is *"disconnected via expansion"* for both halves. **Expansion does the work on one side only.**

- **Absorber side — expansion starves it, and generically.** A late photon's *future* light cone empties: the de Sitter event horizon cuts it off and matter density falls. §4 is right that this is the generic late-time fate of radiation, not a contrivance.
- **Emitter side — expansion cannot starve it, and runs the wrong way.** An absorber's *past* light cone in an expanding universe is **denser**, not sparser. Go backwards and matter density rises until you reach the hot bath. **There is no epoch at which expansion leaves an absorber without emitters in its past.** Within a live aeon the no-emitter condition is essentially never satisfied, so **there is no second class of CCC event inside an aeon.**

**What *can* starve the emitter side is the beginning of time, not expansion.** An absorber early in an aeon has a past light cone bounded not by a shortage of emitters but by the boundary itself.

## 4. The attractive reading, stated so it can be tested rather than admired

If the no-absorber failure sits at the **parent's I⁺** and the no-emitter failure sits at the **daughter's N = 0**, then they are not two classes of event. **They are the two ends of one failed handshake, one on each side of the same interface** — the transaction that cannot *complete* at I⁺ is the transaction that cannot *begin* at N = 0.

That is a sharper version of something §7 already asserts without grounding: *"the photon is the Janus point — the photon on the parent's side, the datum it hands across on ours."* And it would supply a reason for a feature C21 currently only stipulates: **why the crossing object is a link rather than a thing.** A handshake is a relation between two events; strip the partner from each end and what remains is the connection with nothing on either side of it — which is the right *type* to be *"a scalar and topological datum"* and not a mode. **The v0.5 recast turned on type and nothing else.** If this reading holds, the type was not a choice.

## 5. **The disanalogy that may be fatal, stated before the attraction sets in**

The two halves are not the same kind of absence.

- At the parent's I⁺ there **is** a light-cone structure and it is **empty of absorbers**. A genuine emptiness.
- At the daughter's N = 0 there is no shortage of emitters — **there is no event structure at all.** NTE's time begins at N_lock, not N = 0, and §12.1's pre-temporal interface is explicitly not a Lorentzian regime. "No emitter in the past light cone" presupposes a past light cone.

So the symmetry is **formal, not physical**, on the present reading. One side is an empty cone; the other is the absence of a cone. **Greg's "either should produce a CCC event" is therefore not established**, and the honest statement is that the criterion is two-sided while only one side has a physical instantiation the framework can point at.

**Whether that disanalogy is fatal or is the content is exactly the question worth working on.** It is possible that "the absence of a cone" is the strongest possible form of "no emitter" rather than a failure of the analogy — but saying so now would be reasoning toward the conclusion.
