## Two real differences

**1. How much §8.5–8.6 and Companion 16 §3 force.**
- Scratch: the deposit *gives* the mirror SU(3)_C × SU(2)_R × U(1)_Y, so a no-EM mirror is excluded by text.
- Me: the sentence is silent on mirror colour, hypercharge and photon; other passages lean toward a full image; text alone would be H4.
- Assessment: Scratch's steps "colour is shared (not sector-specific)" and "U(1)_Y … present for the conjugate sector too" are inferences from one manifold and one e₁. I think they are the natural reading, and I made the same inference about the vacuum value. But Companion 16 does not state them. The one thing it does state is that SU(2)_R "acts on conjugate-sector fields and is not a gauge symmetry of the observable +N sector", which implies it is a gauge symmetry of the other. **I would grade the mirror's SU(2)_R as deposited, and its colour and hypercharge as strongly implied, not stated.** The landing does not depend on which of us is right.

**2. "A Higgs vacuum requires electromagnetism."**
- Scratch's decisive step: twin Higgs vacuum ⟹ twin U(1)_EM, so keeping the mass keeps the EM.
- That holds only if the mirror has a hypercharge field. A doublet breaking SU(2)_R alone leaves no massless photon. I named that construction in my pre-registration §4 before reading anything. So as a general statement it is too strong; it rests on difference 1.
- More important: Scratch was blind to the Hκ stack by design and argued against a **twin** Higgs. Hκ's actual claim is a **shared** Higgs ("Shared Higgs / e₁ on the one manifold"). Scratch's argument does not reach that case. Mine does: a Yukawa coupling to our Higgs requires our gauge charges, so the conjugate fermions would not be dark.
- **The two arguments cover complementary branches.** Twin Higgs: Scratch, given mirror hypercharge. Shared Higgs: mine. Neither branch gives a massive, dark, EM-free mirror. And in both, 99% of baryon mass still needs gluons, which we both say.
