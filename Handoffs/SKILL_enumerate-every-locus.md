# Enumerate every locus

## The rule

**A withdrawal register that does not enumerate every section asserting the claim will leave one standing.**

Before issuing any register: **grep the whole corpus for the claim**, not for the file you happen to be reading. **Include abstracts, conclusions, headings and sibling papers.**

## Why — the record

Nine propagation failures, every one the same shape: a register fixed the loci someone thought of, and the claim survived elsewhere.

- A withdrawn prediction stayed in **Part 1's abstract** for ten days after the body was corrected.
- A flatness clause corrected in one paper, left live in another.
- **κ₀ < 1** survived in two papers after the series it belonged to was withdrawn.
- A convergence condition still grounded a headline claim four days after its basis fell.
- **Companion 6 §6** still stated a withdrawn number as a result weeks later — in a paper on the publication list.
- A **"recovers standard BBN"** assertion survived in one section while the same paper disclaimed it in another.

**Abstracts were missed twice.** The abstract is what a reader meets first; a paper whose abstract contradicts its body is worse than one not yet updated.

## Procedure

1. **Name the claim**, not the file.
2. **Grep the corpus** — all papers, plus abstracts, conclusions, headings, falsifier lists, status tables, and any register or index.
3. **Report every hit** before drafting blocks. Enumerate; do not assume the first three are all of them.
4. **Check sibling claims**: if a quantity is withdrawn, find every place it is *used*, not only where it is *stated*.
5. **Check the conclusion section specifically.** It is where a reader lands last and where claims are restated in compressed form.

## Two standing requirements

**Abstracts are a default line item in every register.** Not an afterthought.

**One claim, several papers, corrected in some** is the signature failure. **If a correction is made in one document, ask immediately which other documents assert the same thing** — and treat "I don't think any do" as a hypothesis to test, not an answer.

## Corollary — a status header is not a fix

A header saying "X is open" on a paper whose body still asserts X is **worse than no header**: it signals the problem is handled when it is not. **Bodies first, headers second.**
