## 0. **What this is not**

**Nothing is dropped and nothing is deleted.** The full corpus deposits as it stands, with the cosmological sector marked open. **The fork is a second, narrower artefact** whose purpose is that a reader meets the strong result without first wading through four open sectors.

**And the existing two-register split (P08) does not do this job**, which is why the fork is a better instrument: a register *label* does not stop a reader reading everything.

## 1. **The line must be drawn deliberately — P08's register (A) is NOT the core**

**This is the first correction and it matters.** Register (A) accumulated items that were sorted there because they were **not cosmology-sector failures**, not because they belong to the quantum-geometric core: **forced Majorana, |δ_J| = π/2, θ₂₃ = π/4, w = −1, closed S³ with Ω_k < 0, κ₀.**

**Those are particle- and cosmology-sector items.** Inheriting (A) wholesale would reproduce the problem the fork exists to solve.

## 3. **What must be REWRITTEN, not moved** — and this is most of the work

**The core papers were written inside a framework that then extended into cosmology. Moving them unchanged would carry that framing with them.**

- **Register discipline.** The Born rule is a **reformulation, not a derivation** — Companion 2 says so of itself three times, and that must be the fork's stated register, prominently, not a buried qualification.
- **Dependency pruning.** Any core claim that cites a withdrawn or open item for support must be re-grounded or re-registered. **The unresolved-neutrino retest just showed this happens**: Part 1 §I's *"rest mass is Yukawa coupling"* is false for seesaw neutrinos and seeded a confusion that propagated for months.
- **Depth, per Greg's direction.** The strong claims get **full ontological grounding** — what the claim is, what it rests on, what it does not claim, and the evidence accumulated. **This is the main writing task and it is additive, not editorial.**

## 4. **One test the fork must pass, and it should be run before writing**

> **Does any surviving core claim depend, directly or indirectly, on anything withdrawn or open?**

**AC proposes this as a commissioned check, not an assumption.** The month's pattern is that dependencies were discovered by reading rather than by construction. **If the Born-rule reformulation leans anywhere on the bilateral structure, the fork needs to know before it is written, not after.**

## 5. **The negative record as a first-class deliverable — Greg's point, and AC thinks it is the sharpest thing here**

> *"The why is often the most important aspect… An explanation should not just be a 'this doesn't work.' It needs a deep grounding… it may point to what does work."*

**AC agrees, and proposes this be written as a deliberate artefact rather than left implicit in Part 3's open-work entries.**

**What it would contain, for each withdrawn claim:** what was claimed; what defeated it, with numbers; **why the approach failed in kind, not just in instance**; and what would have to be true for a similar approach to succeed.

**Worked examples already in hand:**
- **Native (n_s, r)** — failed because Φ_NTE cannot inflate under H = β|Φ|² (5 × 10²⁰). *Why: the framework's defining constraint and an inflationary potential cannot both hold.*
- **κ as dark matter** — failed on three independent closures. *Why: CPN symmetry gives κ its footing and forces the equal temperatures that make the sector un-cold. The symmetry producing the number denies its interpretation.*
- **The cold-conjugate and Hκ rescues** — failed on deposited text and on internal consistency. *Why: removing conjugate gauge content to make the twin dark also removes what gives it mass.*

**That last pattern is genuinely useful to a future worker**, and it is exactly the kind of thing normally omitted.
