## 0. Greg's question, and AC's answer to it from the record

> *"Have the axioms actually held us back? Is there anything they're precluding unnecessarily, or including unnecessarily?"*

**AC's finding from the closure record, offered for attack: in every traceable case the axiom was UNDERSPECIFIED, not wrong.** No failure traces to an axiom being too strong or too weak. They trace to axioms **stated without their scope and then used past it.**

**Three worked cases:**

**(a) Surface-only — an over-reading, not an over-strong axiom.** It denies a 4-ball interior; it was *read* as denying a four-volume, and **S³ × N is four-dimensional.** That reading drove the Dark Sector §4.1 argument into a fortnight's dead end. **Modifying the axiom would have been the wrong repair.**

**(b) Surface-only's scoping — the axiom was already more permissive than its use.** Part 2 §XVI's parenthetical *"pre-temporally the Hartle–Hawking epoch is bulk-dominated; the surface-only statement applies after gauge birth"* was deposited and unnoticed for months. **AC read Part 2 in full twice and missed it.** The deposit never stated its own scope where a user would meet it.

**(c) CPN — the clear case of an axiom carrying load it cannot bear.** It is stated as a map, z → −z, *"multiplication by e^{iπ}"* — **a Hopf fibre half-turn, identity on the base, orientation-preserving, containing no C, no P and no N**, and a gauge transformation on resolved matter. **It is then asked to do three jobs:** fix M_A = M_B, sort baryons to the mirror orientation (Part 2 §8.4), and assign SU(2)_R (§8.5–8.6). **The map as written does none of them.** **AC's reading: this is a case for *splitting*, not modifying** — state the conjugation operation, and state separately what symmetry each job actually requires.

## 1. **Task A — specification before revision**

**For every axiom in the deposit, three fields:**

| Field | Note |
|---|---|
| **What it says** | verbatim, with its locus |
| **What it licenses** | which deposited derivations invoke it, and for what |
| **What it does NOT license** | **the field whose absence caused (a) and (b)** |

**Revision is not the first move.** **Specify first and see what changes.** Some axioms may then look unnecessary; others may turn out to permit more than anyone used — **(b) is exactly that case, and it is the one with forward value.**

## 2. **Task B — the unused-axiom question. Greg has flagged this as potentially the strongest pathway, and AC agrees.**

> **Is there an axiom nobody has ever invoked?**

**AC does not know the answer.** It is the clearest possible case of unnecessary inclusion, and it is cheap to test: **for each axiom, find every deposited derivation that cites it.** An axiom with zero dependents is either dead weight or **an unexploited resource.**

**And the converse matters as much — AC flags it as the item with the most forward value:**

> **Is there a deposited derivation that invokes no axiom at all — or invokes one only loosely?**

**That is where an unstated assumption lives**, and it is how the amplifier survived for months with no action behind it.

**Greg's hope, recorded honestly: this could lead back into cosmology.** **AC's position: possible, and not to be assumed.** **An axiom found permissive enough to license something new is a genuine finding; an axiom *modified* so that it licenses something we want is discipline 12(c) hand-tuning at the foundational level — the most damaging place it could occur.** **The order must be: specify, then see what is licensed. Never: decide what we want, then adjust the licence.**
