## Reading of Greg's ruling

*"A photon experiences no time" stays.* The Prelude keeps it — in the bridge paragraph verbatim, and as the pivot line in the past tense of the narrative. What the ruling does not protect is the **perspective and frame** framing wrapped around it: *From the photon's perspective* (three times) and *in the photon's own frame of reference*. Those assert the frame A9 withdraws, and Ch 1 v10 now tells the reader directly that no such frame exists. Leaving them in the Prelude would have the book contradict itself between its first page and its first chapter.

The pivot keeps its shape — a short line, set as its own paragraph — so the Prelude's rhythm survives.

### Q1 — Setting up the pivot

**OLD**
That is the story from your perspective. From the photon's perspective, the story is different in a way that physics has known for a hundred and twenty years and never quite known what to do with.

**NEW**
That is the story as you would tell it. The photon has no version of it to tell, and the reason is something physics has known for a hundred and twenty years and never quite known what to do with.

### Q2 — The pivot line

**OLD**
From the photon's perspective, no time passed at all.

**NEW**
The photon experienced no time at all.

*Seed sentence, kept, in the narrative's past tense. Set as its own paragraph, as before.*

### Q3 — "Zero time" and the frame of reference

**OLD**
Not very little time. Not so little that we have to round it down. *Zero* time. The moment the photon was created, in the heart of that distant star, and the moment it was absorbed, in your retina, are — in the photon's own frame of reference — the same moment. The photon did not wait forty million years to arrive. It did not experience a journey. It did not, in any sense the photon would recognise, *travel*.

**NEW**
Not very little time. Not so little that we have to round it down. Not even zero on a clock — because relativity gives the photon no clock, and no point of view from which to read one. Between its creation in the heart of that distant star and its absorption in your retina, nothing along its path marks off one moment from the next. The photon did not wait forty million years to arrive. It did not experience a journey. It did not, in any sense the word can carry, *travel*.

*Ch 1 v10's two corrections in one paragraph: no clock rather than a clock reading zero (P2), and nothing marked between emission and absorption rather than "the same moment" (R2–R5). "In any sense the photon would recognise" attributed recognition to the photon; replaced.*

### Q4 — The bridge

**OLD**
If a photon experiences no time on its journey, then the photon's path is a kind of bridge — connecting emission and absorption without registering them as separate events. The bridge is real; the traversal is not.

**NEW**
If a photon experiences no time on its journey, then the photon's path is a kind of bridge — connecting emission and absorption with nothing marked between them. The bridge is real; the traversal is not.

*Seed sentence verbatim. "Without registering them as separate events" edged toward identity; they are separate events, with nothing along the line separating them.*

### Q5 — The mass–time relation, un-dated from the Higgs

**OLD**
The Standard Model's relationship between mass and the passage of time, which has been hiding in plain sight since 1964, turns out to be the surface of something deeper.

**NEW**
The relationship between mass and the passage of time — why anything with mass keeps time and light does not — has been hiding in plain sight, and turns out to be the surface of something deeper.

*"Since 1964" dates the relation to the Higgs mechanism, which is the Higgs-as-dial in miniature. Ch 1 v10 (P12) sets the dial as rest energy, with the proton as the case: about ninety-nine per cent of its mass is confinement energy, not Higgs coupling, and it keeps time regardless.*
