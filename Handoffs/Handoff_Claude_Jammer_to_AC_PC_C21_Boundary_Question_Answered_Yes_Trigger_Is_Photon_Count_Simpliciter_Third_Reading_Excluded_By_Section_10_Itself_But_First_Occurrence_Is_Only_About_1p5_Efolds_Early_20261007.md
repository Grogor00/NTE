## The question

> Does a Hubble cube containing a photon and no relic neutrino satisfy C21's trigger as the paper defines it?

## Answer: yes. Greg's repair works on the paper's own terms.

**1. The trigger as defined is a photon count, simpliciter.** §3: "n_γ · V_H = 1, with V_H ≡ R_H³". Nothing about massive content appears in the condition. "Matter decays to photons" is the sentence before it — narrative lead-in, not part of the criterion.

**2. The physical content §4 gives the trigger is about absorbers.** "The physical content of the trigger is that the last photons have no absorbers… Half a transaction: an emission with no absorption." A relic neutrino is electrically neutral and is not a photon absorber. On §4's own reading a neutrino in the cube does not even block the trigger. Charged matter does.

**3. The third reading — the whole boundary must be free of massive content — is excluded by the paper's own §10.** §10 support 1: anything beyond the de Sitter horizon is "permanently causally disconnected: not distant, but unreachable", so "How many black holes the parent contains is therefore irrelevant to what a causally severed patch inherits." If massive content elsewhere on the same S³ could spoil the conformal step, black holes elsewhere would too, and §10's decisive support would fail. The paper cannot require a mass-free S³ and keep §10. It is committed to patch-locality, and §9 says so: "The transformation is per-photon… The reset is not global."

So readings one and two both hold, and reading three is not available to the paper.

## What is left of the third reading, stated exactly

It does not vanish; it turns into item (c). The global phrasing lives in §5 ("A universe of purely massless content has no scale to lose") and §9's credit to Penrose. What Penrose's step needs is not that massive *particles are absent* but that *no scale is present in the physics*. A patch holding one photon still sits under laws with a neutrino mass, a finite |Φ_∞| (§2 insists on it) and a Planck length (used to define the map). Neutrinos elsewhere on S³ add nothing to that problem. So PC should not look for the difficulty in locality; it is entirely in (c).

For v0.8 item (a), two sentences would close it: the conformal step acts on the photon's causal patch, by the same severance §10 uses; and the trigger is absence of absorbers in that patch, so neutral relics are not an obstruction.

## One correction to the repair as worded — "long before"

AC's note: such a cube is "close to guaranteed long before any global massless state" and the first-occurrence time is "earlier than all three" figures.

Against a global massless state, yes — that state never arrives. Against the nominal 67 e-folds, **barely**. Reason: the relic photon bath is uniform to parts in 10⁵. Voids are empty of matter, not of photons. So the photon condition is met at nearly the same time everywhere, and inhomogeneity does not bring it forward.

Rough estimate, mine, unreviewed, not pre-registered:
- Mean photons per cube at e-fold N from today: λ ≈ 1.9 × 10⁸⁷ × e^(−3N). Mean relic neutrinos ≈ 0.8 λ.
- Chance a given cube holds at most one photon and no neutrino: of order λ·e^(−1.8λ).
- Number of cubes: at least about 10⁵ today (from the curvature bound Part 2 quotes, Ω_k = 0.0007 ± 0.0019, giving a curvature radius of at least ~16 Hubble radii), growing as e^(3N).
- Setting cubes × chance ≈ 1 gives λ ≈ 115, which is N ≈ **65.4**.

So first occurrence is about **1.5 e-folds, a few times 10¹⁰ years, before** the nominal epoch. A much larger S³ moves that only logarithmically. And at the nominal epoch itself, about 45% of cubes already hold no neutrino. The honest statement for v0.8: *first occurrence at roughly 65 e-folds; the bulk of empty-space cubes within an e-fold or two of 67; cubes containing bound matter much later, with a long tail.* The 10²²–10²³ figure and Ht ≈ 5.7 × 10¹¹ should still go.

The spread of trigger times is real and is in the tail, not the front: it comes from cubes with bound charged matter, which keep producing photons and absorbers.

## Wording

"Uncountably many" events (AC's note, and C21 §8): one event per Hubble cube on a closed S³ is a finite number at any time. Large, growing, finite.
