## Scope

NTE makes a surface fundamental and reads the Born rule as invariant area on it. This companion asks what constrains such an ontology. **Result (conditional):** two independent requirements — a Gleason/measure floor and a least-action dynamical ceiling — **cannot be met on a single manifold**, forcing a split into an external dynamical sector and an internal Hilbert/generation sector. The number 3 is *not* forced by this line; the two-sector *architecture* is.

**Retracted from the source draft's v0.1:** any claim that Hopf geometry forces $d=3$ as a single integer unifying both threes. The brackets are disjoint; the threes share a root, not an identity.

**Not claimed:** novelty of Banach–Tarski / Gleason / Ehrenfest–Tangherlini; novelty of the octonionic SM programme (Companion 16). The increment is the two-sector necessity under NTE's rigid QM-is-geometry reading.

## 1. Floor — measurability is load-bearing

SO(2) is amenable; SO(3) contains a free group $F_2$ and is non-amenable, so $S^2$ is Banach–Tarski-paradoxical. The invariant area that defines probability is unique **only on measurable sets**. Measurability is therefore a physical constraint, non-trivial precisely because the surface is non-amenable.

Gleason's theorem forces the Born measure for Hilbert dimension $n\geq3$ and **fails at $n=2$** (the qubit, $\mathbb{C}P^1=S^2$). The bare qubit is **doubly deficient**: first non-amenable sphere *and* Gleason-deficient. Forcing Born requires $d_H\geq3$, supplied by the generations, not the bare Hopf qubit.

---

## 2. Ceiling — least action forbids $d>3$

For a central potential $V\propto -r^{2-d}$, circular-orbit curvature satisfies $V''(r_0)\propto(3-d)$: stable orbits require $d_{\mathrm{space}}\leq3$. With propagating gravity (absent below $d=3$), this is a self-contained dynamical bracket $d_{\mathrm{space}}=3$ (Ehrenfest–Tangherlini–Whitrow lineage).

---

## 3. Disjointness → two-sector necessity

For Hilbert $\mathbb{C}^n$, the pure-state manifold is $\mathbb{C}P^{n-1}$ (real dim $2(n-1)$):

| $n$ | Gleason | Dynamically stable? |
|---|---|---|
| 2 (qubit) | fails | stable |
| 3 | forced | unstable |

**No $n$ gives both.** Stability $\Rightarrow$ manifold dim $\leq3$ $\Rightarrow$ $n\leq2$ $\Rightarrow$ Gleason fails; Gleason $\Rightarrow$ $n\geq3$ $\Rightarrow$ dim $\geq4$ $\Rightarrow$ unstable.

> **Two-sector necessity (conditional).** A surface ontology demanding both a dynamically stable space and a *forced* (not posited) Born rule must separate an **external dynamical sector** (spatial $S^3$, stable, Gleason-deficient qubit) from an **internal Hilbert sector** (generations, Gleason-forced, no stability burden).

**Conditionality:** assumes (i) Born-forcing routed through Gleason, and (ii) the rigid QM-is-geometry reading in which the dynamical manifold is the qubit state-space sphere. Relax either and the necessity weakens to a strong consistency. Not an unconditional theorem.

## 4. Octonionic cross-check (Companion 16 — recorded, not re-derived)

The same split is realised algebraically by the adopted octonionic programme:

- **External / spatial** = quaternionic subalgebra $\mathbb{H}\subset\mathbb{O}$: $S^3\cong SU(2)_L$.
- **Internal / generation** = $J_3(\mathbb{O})$ diagonal slots (three generations).

Dynamics-plus-measure (§§1–3) and division-algebra structure (Companion 16) converge on one architecture. A tempting shortcut — that spatial 3 and generation 3 are the *same* object $\mathrm{Im}(\mathbb{H})$ — is **not** the corpus reading: they share a root, not an identity.

## 5. Open — $\sigma$-triple $\leftrightarrow J_3(\mathbb{O})$

NTE also describes generations as the $\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3$ Cartan-orthogonal triple (Companions 7, 10). Whether that triple is the quaternionic shadow of the $J_3(\mathbb{O})$ slots is **open** — and is the same family of questions as Session Item 1 (Scratch/Jammer $\sigma\leftrightarrow$generation dictionary). Closing it would complete the common-root picture; leaving it open does not affect §3.
