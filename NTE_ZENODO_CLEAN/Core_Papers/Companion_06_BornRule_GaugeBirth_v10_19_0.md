## Abstract

The Born rule P = |⟨φ|ψ⟩|² acquires its physical activation conditions from the geometry of the cosmological S³ boundary at the moment of temporal locking. Companion 2 establishes that, given a state space with complex Hilbert-space structure and unobservable global U(1) phase, the squared-modulus rule is the geometrically natural map on the state space, with the quadratic exponent selected by the Hopf projection. That derivation takes S³ and the U(1) phase symmetry as inputs. This paper provides the physical mechanism for *when* those inputs become operative: the U(1) fibre activates at the gauge birth, not before. (The complex Hilbert-space input itself is sharpened, not fully closed, by the gauge-birth story.)

Before temporal locking (N < N_lock), the S³ boundary supports undifferentiated spinor modes classified by the Dirac operator. There is no gauge symmetry, no U(1) phase, and no Hopf projection — the Born rule does not apply, because there is nothing for it to apply to. At temporal locking, gauge symmetry activates (the gauge birth). The U(1) factor in SU(3)_C × SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y makes global phase physically unobservable, and that unobservability *is* the Hopf fibre. The Born rule switches on with gauge symmetry — it is not an eternal law applied retroactively, but a geometric consequence of the gauge birth.

The specific cos²(θ/2) correlation for spin-½ measurements follows from the Wigner d-matrix on S³ — the angular overlap between spinor harmonics at different orientations on the 3-sphere. For entangled systems, the single-object picture (Companion 1) combined with this overlap produces the Bell-violating correlations as a geometric identity. No postulate beyond the S³ boundary geometry, the gauge birth, and Companion 2's inputs is required.

The activation question is thereby closed at the level of physical mechanism. The Born rule's quadratic *form* remains as Companion 2 derives it — contingent on the complex Hilbert space and bilinearity-in-amplitude inputs — and whether that form reduces to less than those inputs is a separate, open question.

## 1. The question

The question is the physical mechanism behind the Born rule: starting from the undifferentiated S³ boundary geometry of the pre-temporal epoch, why does the outcome probability P(opposite | θ) = cos²(θ/2) for spin-½ particles emerge, and when? Companion 2 supplies the mathematical derivation — on S³, given a complex Hilbert space and the unobservability of global U(1) phase, the unique probability function satisfying U(1) invariance, SU(2) equivariance, normalisation, additivity, continuity, and consistency under SU(2)-rotated bases is P = |⟨φ|ψ⟩|². But that derivation treats S³ as an abstract state space and the U(1) invariance as a given. What remains is the physical mechanism: why the S³ state space is the cosmological S³ boundary, and why the U(1) invariance activates at temporal locking rather than being eternal. This paper provides both, at the level of physical mechanism. The complementary question — whether the rule's form derives from less than the complex-Hilbert-space and bilinearity inputs — is left open, as Companion 2 leaves it.

## 2. The pre-temporal S³ boundary

**What exists before locking.** The pre-temporal S³ boundary (N < N_lock ∼ 0.1) supports topological spinor modes classified by the eigenvalues of the Dirac operator on S³: λ_n = ±(n + 3/2)/r, n = 0, 1, 2, …, each with degeneracy (n + 1)(n + 2). These modes carry spin and Fermi statistics and occupy levels up to the Planck-scale cutoff; their total number is set by the S³ topology at the cutoff.

**What does not exist before locking.** The pre-temporal modes are neither massive nor massless — both categories presuppose temporal and gauge structures not yet emerged. They have no gauge quantum numbers (no colour, weak isospin, or hypercharge); they are not quarks, leptons, or gauge bosons, but undifferentiated spinor degrees of freedom on S³. Critically, **there is no U(1) gauge symmetry before locking.** The gauge connection A_μ(x) is defined on a fibre bundle over a manifold with a timelike direction; without the emergent t, the bundle lacks its base and the gauge principle cannot be formulated. Without U(1) gauge symmetry, global phase is *not* unobservable: |ψ⟩ → e^{iξ}|ψ⟩ is not a gauge redundancy but a physical operation that changes the state. There is no reason to quotient by U(1), no Hopf fibration, and no Born rule.

**The epistemic status of pre-temporal "states."** The language of states, measurements, and probabilities is post-temporal. Before locking, the S³ boundary has a geometric configuration — a distribution of spinor mode amplitudes across the Dirac spectrum — but this is not a quantum state in the Born-rule sense, and "measuring" a pre-temporal mode is undefined (measurement requires a detector with temporal extension and gauge-charged interactions, neither of which exists pre-temporally). The pre-temporal S³ boundary is a geometric object, not a quantum system; it *becomes* a quantum system at the gauge birth.

## 3. The gauge birth activates the Hopf projection

**At temporal locking.** At N_lock ∼ 0.1 the emergent coordinate t stabilises, and simultaneously gauge symmetry switches on. The undifferentiated spinor modes are assigned to representations of SU(3)_C × SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y, acquiring colour, weak isospin, and hypercharge — they become the Standard Model spectrum. The energy released by this reorganisation is the thermal bath of the hot Big Bang.

**U(1) becomes a gauge symmetry.** The activation of U(1)_Y has a specific geometric consequence: global phase becomes unobservable. Before locking, |ψ⟩ and e^{iξ}|ψ⟩ are distinct geometric configurations on S³; after locking they are gauge-equivalent — physically identical states connected by a gauge transformation. This is the moment the Hopf fibre appears: the S¹ subgroup acting by (z₁, z₂) → (e^{iξ}z₁, e^{iξ}z₂) becomes a gauge redundancy, points along each fibre are identified, and the quotient S³/U(1) = S² becomes the physical state space — the Bloch sphere. **The Hopf fibration does not pre-exist the gauge birth; it is created by it.**

**The Born rule switches on.** Companion 2 proves that, given a complex Hilbert space with global U(1) phase invariance, the unique smooth probability function on S³ that is U(1)-invariant, SU(2)-equivariant, and normalised — with normalisation, additivity, continuity, and consistency under SU(2)-rotated bases jointly enforcing linearity in |z|² — is P = |z₁|² = |⟨φ|ψ⟩|². This applies to any S³ with a U(1) fibre under those inputs. Before locking there is no U(1) fibre, so the premise fails; after locking the fibre exists, so the proof applies.

The restriction to P = f(|z₁|²) follows from two independent layers of gauge symmetry that activate together at the gauge birth. (1) Global U(1) invariance restricts P to U(1)-invariant combinations. (2) The stabiliser U(1) of the measurement axis within SU(2), acting as (z₁, z₂) → (e^{iθ}z₁, e^{−iθ}z₂), eliminates the cross-terms Re(z₁z̄₂) and Im(z₁z̄₂) — these rotate non-trivially under the second action while |z₁|² and |z₂|² do not — and normalisation, additivity, and continuity then force f(r) = r uniquely. Both layers are supplied by the gauge birth: the global U(1) is U(1)_Y, and the stabiliser U(1) is the residual symmetry of the measurement-axis frame within SU(2)_L. The Born rule activates at temporal locking because both prerequisites — the unobservability of global phase and the stabiliser-induced elimination of cross-terms — are consequences of gauge symmetry that activates then.

This answers the activation question: the moment at which the U(1) fibre exists, the Hopf projection becomes operative, and the squared-modulus rule applies is the gauge birth, because gauge symmetry is part of that crystallisation. The rule's specific quadratic form remains as Companion 2 derives it (contingent on the complex Hilbert space and U(1) phase invariance), so the Born rule is not a timeless axiom in its activation conditions; whether its quadratic form is *also* derivable from less than those inputs is a separate, open question.

## 4. The cos²(θ/2) correlation function

**Spin-½ on S³.** Once the Born rule is active, the angular dependence of outcomes follows from the representation theory of SU(2) ≅ S³. A spin-½ state |ψ⟩ = z₁|↑⟩ + z₂|↓⟩ is a point on S³; a measurement along an axis tilted by θ corresponds to the rotated basis |↑_θ⟩ = cos(θ/2)|↑⟩ + sin(θ/2)|↓⟩, |↓_θ⟩ = −sin(θ/2)|↑⟩ + cos(θ/2)|↓⟩. The rotation is an SU(2) element acting on S³ by left multiplication — the Wigner d-matrix d^{1/2}(θ).

**The probability.** For a state prepared as |↑⟩ and measured along the θ-axis, the Born rule gives P(↑_θ) = |⟨↑_θ|↑⟩|² = cos²(θ/2) and P(↓_θ) = sin²(θ/2). This is a geometric statement about S³ — the squared overlap between two points related by an SU(2) rotation of angle θ. The cos²(θ/2) function is not a separate input; it is the Hopf projection of the SU(2) rotation matrix.

**Why the half-angle.** The half-angle θ/2 arises because SU(2) is the double cover of SO(3): a physical rotation by θ in three-dimensional space corresponds to a rotation by θ/2 on S³, and the Hopf projection maps the S³ rotation to a probability on S². This double-cover structure is intrinsic to S³ — the same reason spin-½ particles require 4π rotation to return to their original state. The half-angle is a topological property of S³, not a physical assumption.

**The entanglement correlation.** For the singlet |ψ⟩ = (|↑↓⟩ − |↓↑⟩)/√2, the probability that detectors at relative angle θ obtain opposite outcomes is cos²(θ/2). In the single-object picture (Companion 1), the singlet is one geometric object at zero e-fold depth projected onto two detection events, and cos²(θ/2) is the angular overlap on S³ between the single object's spinor harmonic and the two detector orientations, constrained by conservation of angular momentum at the projection. The Bell-violating correlations are geometric — they exceed the classical limit (which assumes two independent local objects) because the single object's S³ geometry enforces tighter constraints than any local model can reproduce. The single-object mechanism is explicitly non-local.

## 5. The angular overlap as an S³ inner product

The pre-temporal spinor modes are eigenstates of the Dirac operator on S³; at locking they are partitioned into coupling regimes and assigned gauge quantum numbers, and a mode now identified as a spin-½ fermion occupies a spinor harmonic on S³. The spinor harmonics are classified by (l, m) with l = 2j; for spin-½ the two harmonics are Ψ_{1/2,+1/2} and Ψ_{1/2,−1/2}. The inner product between two such harmonics at relative orientation θ is the Wigner d-matrix element d^{1/2}_{m'm}(θ), and its squared modulus is the Born-rule probability: cos²(θ/2) for m = m' = +½, sin²(θ/2) for m = +½, m' = −½. The cos²(θ/2) correlation is thus the S³ inner product between spinor harmonics at relative angle θ, squared by the Hopf projection — and every element (the S³ geometry, the spinor harmonics, the Hopf projection) is a property of the cosmological boundary, not an abstract postulate. This is not metaphorical: the inner product is computed on the physical S³ boundary, the Hopf projection is activated by the gauge symmetry of the physical Standard Model, and cos²(θ/2) is the angular structure of the physical 3-sphere we inhabit.

## 7. Experimental survival tests

**Sorkin parameter.** The Born rule predicts zero third-order interference (Sorkin parameter ε = 0); any deviation in multi-slit experiments would falsify the Hopf derivation, which produces exactly P = |⟨φ|ψ⟩|² with no higher-order corrections. The measured |ε| < 0.01 (three-slit) is consistent with ε = 0.

**Bell correlations.** The cos²(θ/2) function is tested in every Bell experiment; the CHSH violation S = 2√2 (versus the classical S ≤ 2) is a direct consequence of it, and every confirmation of S = 2√2 confirms the Hopf-derived correlation.

**Universality.** The Born rule applies to photons, electrons, atoms, molecules — all quantum systems. The Hopf derivation explains this: the U(1) fibre structure is the same in every dimension of Hilbert space, and the gauge symmetry that makes phase unobservable acts identically on all representations. There is no known exception, and the derivation predicts none.

## 8. What this establishes, and what remains

The activation question is answered: starting from undifferentiated spinor modes on the pre-temporal S³ (classified by the Dirac operator), the gauge birth activates U(1) gauge symmetry, global phase becomes unobservable, the Hopf fibre appears, and the squared-modulus rule becomes the unique probability map under Companion 2's inputs (with the two-layer gauge-symmetry argument closing the first step); the spinor modes are assigned to Standard Model representations at the same moment; and cos²(θ/2) follows as the Hopf-projected Wigner d-matrix overlap on S³.

The presence of a U(1) factor in the surviving gauge group — the prerequisite for the Hopf projection and hence for the Born rule's activation — is itself a consequence of the octonionic structure of the pre-temporal boundary algebra. The unique non-associative normed division algebra is 𝕆 (Hurwitz), with automorphism group G₂ = Aut(𝕆); at gauge birth the Higgs VEV selects a complex direction in Im(𝕆), whose stabiliser within G₂ is SU(3) (identified with SU(3)_C); SU(2)_L arises from S³ ≅ SU(2) ⊂ S⁷ with CPN selecting left-handed chirality (parity violation by construction of the Janus boundary); and U(1)_Y arises from the complex direction the VEV selects. The Standard Model gauge group is therefore forced by 𝕆 plus the Janus boundary plus temporal emergence, and the U(1) factor is a topological consequence of that structure, not an independent assumption. A universe with a different pre-temporal algebra would generate a different residual gauge group and might lack a U(1) factor; in that case the Hopf projection would not activate and the Born rule would not apply. The Born rule's universality in our universe is a consequence of the universality of the gauge-birth mechanism that produced our specific gauge group.

Two things this does not do. It does not address the measurement problem — why individual outcomes are definite rather than superposed; it gives the correct probabilities but not the transition from superposition to a definite result (consistent with Companion 2's scope). And it does not settle whether the rule's specific quadratic form derives from less than the complex Hilbert space and bilinearity-in-amplitude inputs Companion 2 assumes — that is the framework's open research target. This paper answers *when and why the rule activates*; the form-from-less question is the complementary one, and it stays open.
