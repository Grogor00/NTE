## 2. The boundary-only ontology

**There is no interior.** The 4-ball B⁴ has boundary ∂B⁴ = S³; this topological fact identifies the boundary, it does not create a physical interior. There is no accessible bulk: the S³ surface is closed, and from the perspective of anything on it, it extends without an "inward" direction. The "radial coordinate" of the 4-ball is not spatial — it is the e-fold accumulation N, the logarithmic count of how many times S³ has scaled since nucleation. The past is not a place; it is the accumulated e-fold count. The future does not yet exist; no expansion beyond the current N has occurred. The 4D action integral ∫d⁴x = ∫dN ∫d³x_{S³}: the "fourth dimension" is the e-fold history of boundary expansion, not a direction into a bulk. All physics lives on the S³ surface, parameterised by N.

**Reconciliation with standard physics.** This does not contradict general relativity or the Standard Model. Both are field theories on a 4D manifold — but the manifold is (N, x_{S³}), not (t, x_{ℝ³}). The substitution is d/dt → β_NTE|Φ_NTE|² d/dN; all equations of motion, conservation laws, and symmetries are preserved. The ontology changes what the coordinates mean, not what the equations say.

## 4. Expansion as geometric scaling

The expansion of the universe is not the growth of a ball into a void; there is no void and no bulk to grow into. It is a transformation of the scale factor a(N) = e^N. As N increases the scale factor grows, the embedding thickness ΔN thins relative to the scale, and the ratio a/ΔN increases — what coupled observers perceive as the passage of time and the expansion of space. The speed of light c is the expansion velocity of the S³ boundary, constant because it is a property of the wavefront, not of photons; a photon sits at zero depth, co-moving at c. Exceeding c is not prohibited but geometrically undefined — there is no manifold ahead of the boundary.

**Near-flatness.** The boundary ontology requires a closed S³, so spatial curvature is negative: Ω_k < 0 strictly, and exact flatness is excluded by the geometry. What the framework accounts for is the *observed* near-flatness — the e-fold run drives |Ω_k| toward zero from below, so the observable patch is flat to any achievable precision (Planck 2018: Ω_k = 0.0007 ± 0.0019). Observed flatness is thus a consequence of the expansion history rather than a separate boundary condition. The framework fixes the *sign* and not the magnitude. Earlier versions of this paper derived exact Ω_k = 0 from the Janus zero-energy condition; that derivation is **withdrawn** (2026-09-24). It conflated the bilateral sum over the CPN pair with the single-sector Friedmann energy, and the bilateral sum vanishes with each sector closed. Read as a statement about the manifold's total energy it points the other way: a spatially compact universe has identically zero total energy, so the premise supports closure, not flatness.

**Dark energy as geometric unloading.** In the early universe matter was uniform and coupling density maximal — maximum boundary deformation, maximum impedance to expansion. As N increases, matter disperses, coupling weakens, and curvature smooths; the boundary becomes progressively less deformed. Expansion accelerates not because of a dark-energy fluid but because the resistance to expansion is decreasing — mechanical unloading, the boundary's geometric impedance thinning as the standing-wave density (matter) disperses.

We cannot see conjugate-sector matter electromagnetically (photons are confined to one direction) or through the weak force; we detect only its gravitational footprint. That is dark matter.

**The bare coupling.** The neutrino's unresolved configuration sets the bridge's geometric cross-section. The Hopf fibration projects resolved fermions onto the S² base; the unresolved neutrino does not complete this projection, and the S² cross-section of the bridge is the residual base geometry.
