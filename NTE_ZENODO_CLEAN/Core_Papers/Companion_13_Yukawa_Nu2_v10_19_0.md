## Abstract

NTE inherits most Yukawa couplings from observation, and is honest that it does so. The exception developed here is the second-generation neutrino Dirac Yukawa, whose flux-line structure the framework fixes — though, as set out below, its absolute normalisation is not derived. With the heavy scale M_R from the σ₃ Cartan mode, the seesaw gives the solar-scale mass

  m_{ν₂} = (8π)^{-2/3} v_EW² / M_R = 0.0086 eV,

which is 0.993 × √(Δm²_solar) — but only on the 8π branch. That sub-percent agreement is contingent on selecting (8π)^{-1/3} for the Dirac normalisation; the representation-consistent value is (4π)^{-1/3}, giving ≈ 1.57× instead, and the finite-Dirac computation that would settle the normalisation derives no Yukawa magnitude at all (Part 3, open item: finite-Dirac Yukawa normalisation). The branch-independent content is the (flux)^{2/3} shape of the mass ratio and the trade-off it forces: 8π fits the solar level and overshoots the ratio by ≈ 1.5×; 4π fits the ratio and overshoots the level by ≈ 1.57×; no single M_R fits both legs. The atmospheric leg, with the same single M_R, also overshoots by ≈ 1.5, and these limitations are stated rather than hidden. A critical point of seesaw bookkeeping is made explicit: the denominator for the light ν₂ is the Cartan-scale M_R, not the Pathway-α M₂; the M₂-only reading is wrong by orders of magnitude and is ruled out. The same finite-Dirac openness that leaves the Dirac normalisation unfixed also leaves the sign of the Janus CP-phase δ_J free — a within-sector CP-parity, gauge-redundant across the twin pair. §6 grounds the citation Parts 2–3 make to this companion.

## 1. Why the Yukawas were the last open note

NTE derives a great deal of its gravitational and cosmological sector from a single heat-kernel computation on its canonical spectral triple, but the Yukawa couplings sit outside that gravitational spine: they enter through the finite algebra and the finite Dirac operator, not through the curvature heat-kernel coefficients. So the corpus has, honestly, *inherited* the Yukawas from the Standard Model and left the neutrino Yukawas that set the solar and atmospheric splittings open. This paper closes the second-generation neutrino entry — and only that entry — at the same structural register as the Majorana prefactor it mirrors. "Partial" here does not mean approximate: it means the ν₂ Dirac Yukawa is fixed, while the first-generation and charged-lepton Yukawas, and the full finite-Dirac mass matrix, remain genuinely open.

## 2. The mode assignment

The eigenmode ontology (Companion 7) gives three neutrino mass eigenmodes along the su(2) directions relative to the Higgs VEV: the σ₃ (Cartan) mode aligned with the VEV, and the σ₁/σ₂ pair orthogonal to it. The heavy right-handed scales attach as σ₃ → M_R (heaviest, M₃) and σ₁/σ₂ → M₁, M₂ (the leptogenesis-scale pair of Companion 10, split by M₂ = M₁(1 + sin²θ₁₃) under the residual σ₁↔σ₂ symmetry — the same symmetry that fixes maximal atmospheric mixing). The light ν₂ is the σ₁/σ₂-sector light state; its Dirac Yukawa lives on that sector's coupling line, and — the point made precisely in §4 — its seesaw denominator is the Cartan-scale M_R, not the light Majorana M₂.

## 3. The duality: Majorana (8π)^{1/3}, Dirac (8π)^{-1/3}

Companion 10 fixes the Majorana mass on the σ₁/σ₂ sector by flux quantisation in the S³ Hubble 3-volume, giving the BPS-saturating cube-root normalisation

  M_BPS = (8π)^{1/3} (M_Pl² H_GB)^{1/3},

A *Dirac* coupling on the same flux line carries one fewer volumetric power: where a Majorana mass scales as (flux/volume)^{1/3}, a Dirac Yukawa scales as (flux/volume)^{-1/3}. Hence

  Y_{ν₂} = (8π)^{-1/3} ≈ 0.341.

A Dirac coupling, however, connects ν_L to ν_R as a complex bilinear; the ×2 in 8π is the *reality* of the Majorana Spin(4) representation and has no analogue in a complex Dirac coupling. Only the 4π Hopf base flux is a geometric property of the shared line, so the representation-consistent Dirac value is Y_{ν₂} = (4π)^{-1/3} ≈ 0.430. Selecting (8π)^{-1/3} instead — carrying the Majorana doubling across — is what brings the solar leg onto its measured value; it is not forced by the flux geometry. ## 4. The seesaw, and a bookkeeping that must be right

The light ν₂ mass is the seesaw of this Dirac Yukawa against the heavy Cartan-scale Majorana propagator:

  **m_{ν₂} = (8π)^{-2/3} v_EW² / M_R**,  with M_R = 8.2 × 10¹⁴ GeV, v_EW = 246 GeV,

giving m_{ν₂} = 0.0086 eV, against √(Δm²₂₁) ≈ 0.0087 eV — a ratio of 0.993 **on the 8π branch**. This sub-percent agreement is contingent on the branch choice, not a parameter-free consequence of it: the 4π branch (the representation-consistent Dirac value) gives ≈ 1.57× instead. The genuine, branch-independent content is therefore not the 0.993 coincidence but the **(flux)^{2/3} shape** of the mass ratio and the trade-off it forces — 8π fits the solar level and overshoots the ratio by ≈ 1.5×; 4π fits the ratio and overshoots the level by ≈ 1.57×; no single M_R fits both legs.

The bookkeeping point is essential and easy to get wrong. The denominator is the **Cartan-scale M_R**, not the light σ₁/σ₂ Majorana M₂. Using Y_{ν₂} = (8π)^{-1/3} against M₂ alone gives a mass of order a keV — wrong by five orders of magnitude and decisively excluded. (An equivalent, algebraically tied form writes m_{ν₂} = Y_{ν₂}² v_EW²/M₂ with Y_{ν₂} = (8π)^{-1/3}√(M₁/M_R), landing at 0.972 × solar; the small difference from the canonical route is the sin²θ₁₃ split, not a free parameter.) That the correct denominator is M_R, derived ab initio from the σ₃-mixing structure, is itself an open programme item; here it is the established seesaw reading.

**The atmospheric cross-check, stated honestly.** With Y₃ = 1 on the σ₃ Cartan mode and the same M_R, the heavy leg gives m_{ν₃} = v_EW²/M_R = 0.074 eV against √(Δm²₃₁) ≈ 0.050 eV — a factor ≈ 1.49, an overshoot, not a sub-percent match. The predicted mass ratio m_{ν₃}/m_{ν₂} = (8π)^{2/3} ≈ 8.57 against the observed √(Δm²₃₁/Δm²₂₁) ≈ 5.8 differs by the same factor ≈ 1.5: a single M_R cannot fit both legs at the percent level. The genuine, falsifiable content is the (flux)^{2/3} shape of the ratio; the sub-percent solar-leg agreement is not parameter-free but branch-contingent (8π), and the atmospheric leg is an open tension. (Any earlier "0.997× atmospheric" reading used an incorrect comparand and does not stand.)

## 6. The sign of δ_J: a within-sector CP-parity, and free spectral data

Part 2 §X fixes the Janus Bogoliubov phase to its maximal magnitude, $|\delta_J| = \pi/2$, from the geometry of the Majorana boundary transformation: the exponential $(1 - e^{-M/\hbar H_{\mathrm{dS}}}) \to 1$ for any leptogenesis-scale mass, so the magnitude is forced with no free parameter. The *sign* of $\delta_J$ is not fixed by that geometry, and the reason is the same finite-Dirac openness that leaves the Dirac normalisation of §3 unfixed.

The magnitude of $\delta_J$ is geometric — it is the maximal Bogoliubov mixing angle of a Majorana mode at the boundary, and geometry supplies an angle, not an oriented one. The sign is a *branch*: it is set by whether the finite Dirac operator carries the neutrino mass matrix $M_\nu$ or its complex conjugate $M_\nu^*$. Under CP the mass matrix maps to its conjugate, so the two branches are CP-images and choosing between them is a within-sector CP-parity. NTE's finite geometry fixes the flux structure of the magnitudes (this paper) but does not select this parity: the operator phase is free spectral data in exactly the sense the absolute Yukawa normalisation is (§3, §6-now-§7). It is, in fact, the *phase*-analogue of the open magnitude — the finite-Dirac computation that would ground any Yukawa magnitude (Part 3, open item: finite-Dirac Yukawa normalisation) is the same computation that would fix the mass-matrix phase, and since that computation derives no magnitude, a fortiori it fixes no CP-parity. The sign of $\delta_J$ is not an independent unknown; it is the open finite-Dirac normalisation, read on the phase.

A distinction must be drawn between this within-sector CP-parity and the cross-sector symmetry relating the Janus twins. The pairing symmetry is bilateral CPN ($= N\circ CP$; Part 2 §IX–X), which is cross-sector and, because $\mathrm{CPN} \supsetneq \mathrm{CP}$, is *not* the within-sector CP whose parity is at issue. Bilateral CPN correlates the two twins to *opposite* branches: whichever mass-matrix orientation our sector realises, the conjugate twin realises the other. Two consequences follow, different in kind:

- The **relative** sign is fixed. The twins are opposite — matter sorted to $+N$, antimatter to $-N$ (Part 2 §8.4) — which is precisely the sorting-not-annihilation that lets a maximal asymmetry survive. This is physical, and it is what baryogenesis requires.
- The **absolute** sign is a gauge-redundant label. Which twin is "ours," the one with $B>0$, is a relabelling of the pair — a symmetry, not an observable. There is no fact internal to the framework about which side is matter; a matter-dominated observer is guaranteed to be able to call their side "ours."

The honest scope for the asymmetry is therefore layered. Its **existence and maximality** are zero-parameter: $|\delta_J| = \pi/2 \neq 0$ is forced, so a maximal CP asymmetry is guaranteed to exist. Its **relative** sign across the pair is fixed by bilateral CPN (the sorting mechanism). Its **absolute** sign — the direction of $B$ in our sector — is a free within-sector CP-parity, gauge-redundant across the pair, hence neither predicted nor physical. Its **magnitude** $\eta_B$ is a structural consistency at order unity, not a zero-parameter prediction, inheriting the $O(1)$ residue in $M_1$, the atmospheric-ratio overshoot, and an underived washout efficiency (Part 2 §X; Companion 10). This layered reading is what Parts 2 (§X) and 3 (§XVII) cite here: the sign of $\delta_J$ is a free within-sector CP-parity, not locked to $B$.

The result is falsifiable in one direction only, and correctly so. No measurement of "which side is matter" can bear on NTE, because that label is gauge-redundant — a would-be prediction of the absolute sign would be a category error. What *would* change the status is the finite-Dirac normalisation computation: were it to fix the mass-matrix phase, the $\delta_J$ sign would be upgraded from free to derived — by the very same computation that would fix the Yukawa magnitude. The sign of the Janus phase and the neutrino Yukawa normalisation share one open grounding, and they will close together or not at all.

- **Solar splitting precision (8π branch).** On the 8π branch the prediction implies Δm²₂₁ ≈ 7.4 × 10⁻⁵ eV², about 0.5σ below the current central value. A future shift of the measured central value well outside this (by more than ~0.5 × 10⁻⁵ eV² in the relevant direction, at >3σ, with M_R held fixed) falsifies the D5-inverse assignment.
- **Mass ordering.** The σ₃-Cartan denominator assignment requires normal ordering; confirmed inverted ordering breaks it.
- **The denominator structure is non-negotiable.** Any reading that seesaws (8π)^{-1/3} against M₂ alone (without the √(M₁/M_R) factor, i.e. without the Cartan scale) is empirically dead (~keV); the M_R denominator, or its equivalent form, is required.
- **Ratio coherence.** A precision improvement that widens the m_{ν₃}/m_{ν₂} gap further from (8π)^{2/3} at >3σ, while the solar leg holds, falsifies the σ₃-Cartan assignment collectively — the solar and atmospheric legs stand or fall together with the duality.
- **Direct coupling.** A direct determination of Y_{ν₂} away from (8π)^{-1/3} ≈ 0.341, were seesaw-scale physics ever accessible, would falsify the assignment.

The collective vulnerability is deliberate: the Dirac and Majorana normalisations are one duality, and if the (flux)^{2/3} ratio shape fails, the D5 closure fails with it.
