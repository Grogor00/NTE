## The sister prediction — what the same symmetry says about charged leptons

The σ₁ ↔ σ₂ Z₂ symmetry that locks θ₂₃ to 45° has a sister implication for charged-lepton physics. Because the symmetry exchanges two Cartan directions of SU(2)ₗ, it implies a degeneracy: the σ₁ and σ₂ Cartan modes share a common mass (a symmetry that swaps two modes can only do so if the modes are at the same energy). That degeneracy freezes a usually-free parameter in the broader Majorana neutrino mass matrix — specifically, the rotation in the Casas-Ibarra R-matrix that mixes the σ₁ and σ₂ modes — and the freezing forces a structural relation between two channels of charged-lepton flavour violation.

Charged-lepton flavour violation (CLFV) is the phenomenon where a charged lepton decays into another charged lepton plus a photon — μ → eγ or τ → eγ. These processes are vanishingly small in the Standard Model but can be larger in extensions with right-handed Majorana neutrinos. The framework's structural prediction is for the *ratio* between two CLFV amplitudes squared:

|A(μ → eγ)|² / |A(τ → eγ)|² = tan²θ₂₃

For θ₂₃ = 45° this ratio is exactly 1. The framework's odds versus *anarchic* seesaw (Monte Carlo over random Casas-Ibarra matrices) are about 9 to 1 in favour of the structured prediction — a substantial preference, though not overwhelming.

Six qualifiers travel with this prediction, and naming them honestly is what makes it a gate rather than an advertisement.

It is a *conditional* discriminator — the prediction is only useful if CLFV is observed at all. The framework's absolute rate for μ → eγ at its preferred right-handed Majorana mass scale (M_R ≈ 8.2 × 10¹⁴ GeV, predicted by the P5 / S6 companion derivations) sits at a branching ratio of about 10⁻⁵⁹, far below the current experimental bound of 10⁻¹³ from MEG II. The framework does not predict CLFV will be observed at near-future experiments; it predicts what the ratio *would be* if a signal is detected.

It is at the *amplitude level*, not the full branching ratio. The full branching ratio includes powers of lepton mass that distort the simple ratio; the structural prediction is cleanest at the underlying amplitude before the mass-power dressing.

The width on the ratio prediction is about ±2–3%, reflecting subleading corrections to the σ₁/σ₂ degeneracy that have been quantified but not eliminated.

The mechanism producing the prediction — the σ₁/σ₂ Cartan degeneracy freezing the Casas-Ibarra rotation — is the same Z₂ symmetry that the present pivot's main result depends on. Two predictions, one symmetry.

This means θ₂₃ = 45° and the CLFV ratio are not two independent claims but two expressions of the same Z₂ symmetry on the chirality-superposed Majorana sector. A successful future detection of CLFV at a sensitivity that resolves the μ/τ ratio would test the framework's symmetry through a second, structurally independent channel — independent from the cosmological-mixing-angle test but tied to the same underlying commitment. The Clean Gates companion paper develops the CLFV lab-physics test in full; the present paper develops the cosmological-mixing-angle test, and the structural connection between the two is what the framework's case rests on.
