## Abstract

The hierarchy problem — why the Higgs mass (125 GeV) is sixteen orders of magnitude below the Planck mass (10¹⁹ GeV) — has motivated decades of theoretical work, including supersymmetry, extra dimensions, and compositeness. None of these extensions have been confirmed experimentally. This paper argues that no new physics is required. Any scalar field with a symmetry-breaking potential on a compact 3-manifold generates a Schrödinger-type operator with two competing scale contributions: a geometric term from the manifold's Laplacian (scale ∼ 1/r², r the manifold radius) and a field-theoretic term from the potential (scale ∼ λv², v the vacuum expectation value, λ the self-coupling). These contributions balance when the dimensionless parameter β = λv²r² equals unity. For the Higgs field on S³, the condition β = 1 yields r = 1/m_H — the electroweak scale is the unique geometric resonance where the manifold's curvature and the field's self-interaction become equal. The hierarchy between the electroweak and Planck scales is then the hierarchy between the current effective S³ radius and the Planck length — simply the statement that the universe is large and old. No fine-tuning is required, because the resonance condition β = 1 is approached dynamically as the effective radius grows with cosmological expansion.

## 1. The problem

The Standard Model contains one fundamental scalar: the Higgs, with mass m_H = 125.25 GeV and vacuum expectation value v = 246 GeV. The Planck mass, the natural scale of quantum gravity, is M_P = 1.22 × 10¹⁹ GeV. The ratio m_H/M_P ≈ 10⁻¹⁷ is the hierarchy problem: radiative corrections to the Higgs mass scale as the square of the ultraviolet cutoff, so if the cutoff is M_P the "natural" value of m_H is ∼M_P, and obtaining 125 GeV requires cancellations to one part in 10³⁴. Every proposed solution introduces new physics at or below the TeV scale — supersymmetric partners, Kaluza–Klein excitations, composite substructure, relaxion fields — and after decades of LHC searches none has been observed. This paper proposes that the problem is not one of missing physics but of missing geometry: the electroweak scale is a geometric resonance, the natural balance point between two contributions to a scalar field operator on a compact manifold.

## 2. A scalar field on a compact manifold

**The operator.** For a real scalar Φ with a symmetry-breaking potential V(Φ) = −μ²Φ² + λΦ⁴ on a compact Riemannian 3-manifold M, the field has VEV v = μ/√(2λ), and fluctuations h around it (Φ = v + h) satisfy [−Δ_M + V''(v)] h_n = E_n h_n, with V''(v) = 4λv² the scalar mass-squared. This Schrödinger-type operator has a *geometric term* −Δ_M (eigenvalues ∼1/r² for a manifold of radius r) and a *potential term* 4λv² (constant, independent of geometry).

**The dimensionless parameter.** Their relative importance is set by β = 4λv²r², the ratio of potential to geometric energy scale. For β ≪ 1 geometry dominates (eigenvalues those of the free Laplacian); for β ≫ 1 the potential dominates (eigenvalues those of the potential alone); at β = 1 the two are equal — maximal mixing between geometric and field-theoretic modes, neither treatable as a perturbation of the other.

**The resonance condition.** At β = 1, 4λv²r² = 1 ⇒ r = 1/(2√λ v) = 1/m_scalar, with m_scalar = 2√λ v. For the Higgs, m_scalar = m_H = 125.25 GeV, giving r = 1/m_H ≈ 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁸ m — the electroweak scale, the characteristic length at which the Higgs mechanism operates.

## 3. The case of S³

**Why S³.** Among compact 3-manifolds, S³ is distinguished for the Higgs field: S³ ≅ SU(2), the weak gauge group, so an SU(2) scalar lives on it naturally (the manifold *is* the gauge group); it is simply connected, so the VEV can be defined globally without obstruction; it has constant positive curvature R = 6/r², giving a spatially homogeneous geometric contribution consistent with the observed homogeneity of the VEV; and it is parallelisable, supporting global spinors.

**The spectrum.** On S³ of radius r the eigenvalues of −Δ_{S³} are λ_l = l(l + 2)/r² with degeneracy (l + 1)², so the full operator eigenvalues are E_l = l(l + 2)/r² + 4λv². At β = 1 (where 1/r² = 4λv² = m_H²), E_l = m_H²[l(l + 2) + 1]: the ground state l = 0 gives E₀ = m_H², the first excitation l = 1 gives E₁ = 4m_H², and the spectrum grows as ∼l²m_H². The l = 0 eigenvalue at the resonance scale *is* the Higgs mass-squared — a consistency check (the operator was built from the Higgs potential), but with content: the Higgs is the lowest-energy excitation of a scalar field on S³ at the specific radius where geometry and field theory balance.

## 4. The hierarchy as cosmological evolution

**Two distinct scalar fields.** The framework carries two scalar fields on the cosmological S³ boundary, with distinct roles. The *geometric scalar* |Φ_NTE(N)| is the boundary expansion field whose square sources the Hubble rate, H(N) = β_NTE|Φ_NTE(N)|², and it governs the cosmological evolution of the effective S³ radius r(N). The *Standard Model Higgs* Φ_SM is a structurally separate field with its own inherited potential V_SM = −μ²|Φ_SM|² + λ|Φ_SM|⁴, parameters (μ², λ), and v_EW = μ/√(2λ); it supplies the resonance condition β = 1 of this paper. The two identities crystallise together at gauge birth but follow distinct dynamics thereafter, coinciding in value only at the present epoch (H = H₀); at gauge birth |Φ_NTE| is enormously larger than v_EW, while V_SM and v_EW are fixed by Standard Model parameters independent of |Φ_NTE|. The hierarchy between m_H and M_P is therefore: m_H = 2√λ v_EW is set by Standard-Model-inherited parameters at the resonance; the present-epoch scale r is set by the |Φ_NTE|-driven expansion since gauge birth; these are independent inputs, aligning at β = 1 because the universe has expanded by ∼10¹⁷ Planck lengths since gauge birth. No fine-tuning is invoked — only the input parameters and the universe's age.

**The dynamic β.** As the effective radius evolves, β(N) = 4λv²r²(N). At early times (small r, near gauge birth) β ≪ 1: geometry dominates and the Higgs potential is a small perturbation on the very small S³ — the unbroken phase. As the universe expands and r grows (driven by H = β_NTE|Φ_NTE|²), β rises; when it reaches unity the contributions balance and the Higgs settles into its symmetry-breaking vacuum — electroweak symmetry breaking, not as a purely thermal trigger but as the geometry growing into resonance with the Higgs self-coupling. (In the conventional thermal picture EWSB occurs at T ∼ v_EW; this is the same temperature, because there r ∼ 1/m_H by the radiation-era Friedmann relation — the two pictures agree on *when*, but the resonance picture supplies a structural reason rather than only a thermal trigger.) At late times (large r) β ≫ 1: the potential dominates and standard particle physics applies.

**The hierarchy is the age of the universe.** The ratio m_H/M_P = l_P/r_resonance = l_P × m_H ≈ 10⁻¹⁷ is the ratio of the Planck length to the resonance radius r = 1/m_H. The hierarchy exists because the universe has expanded from the Planck scale to a scale far larger than the resonance radius — the Higgs mass is small compared to the Planck mass because the universe is old compared to the Planck time. This is not fine-tuning: any universe that has expanded past ∼10¹⁷ Planck lengths will have undergone EWSB with a Higgs mass hierarchically below M_P.

**Why 125 GeV specifically?** The resonance condition fixes r = 1/m_H but not m_H itself, which depends on the coupling: m_H = 2√λ v, with v = 246 GeV (from the Fermi constant) and λ ≈ 0.129 (from the measured Higgs mass), giving 125.25 GeV. Both v and λ are Standard-Model-inherited, structurally independent of |Φ_NTE|. This paper does not derive λ or v from geometry; they remain measured inputs. What it establishes is that *given* λ and v, the electroweak scale is not a fine-tuned accident but the geometric resonance of the Higgs potential on S³, and the hierarchy to the Planck scale is a consequence of cosmological expansion, not of cancellation. The deeper question — why λ ≈ 0.129 — is left open; if λ were fixed by the geometry of S³ (e.g. by a self-consistency condition on the spectral zeta function of the Higgs-Laplacian), the Higgs mass would be fully geometric. That is a programme for future work.

## 5. Comparison with existing approaches

**Supersymmetry** cancels the quadratic divergences via a partner for every particle, predicting TeV-scale superpartners — none observed, with natural parameter space largely excluded. **Extra dimensions** (large or warped) dilute gravity over additional dimensions, predicting short-distance gravity modifications and Kaluza–Klein excitations — none observed. **The relaxion** scans the Higgs mass with a slowly rolling axion-like field, requiring a new scalar. The geometric resonance shares the relaxion's dynamical structure — β evolving from small to large, the Higgs activating at β = 1 — but requires no new field: the "scanning" is the cosmological expansion of the effective S³ radius, governed by |Φ_NTE|.

## 6. Predictions and tests

**No new particles below the Planck scale.** If the hierarchy is a resonance rather than a cancellation, there is no need for TeV-scale new physics; colliders should find the Standard Model and nothing else at accessible energies. This is a negative prediction, hard to test directly, but every LHC null result (no SUSY, no extra dimensions, no compositeness) is consistent with it and inconsistent with the alternatives.

**The Higgs self-coupling.** The l = 0 and l = 1 eigenvalues at β = 1 give E₁/E₀ = 4, a specific consequence of S³ geometry. The di-Higgs production cross-section is sensitive to the self-coupling λ; a deviation from the Standard Model value consistent with the l = 1/l = 0 ratio on S³ would be evidence for the resonance interpretation. Measuring the self-coupling is a primary target of the HL-LHC and future colliders.

**Vacuum stability.** The Standard Model Higgs potential, extrapolated to high energies, lies near the boundary between absolute stability and metastability (current estimates: metastable, with a lifetime far exceeding the age of the universe). In the resonance picture, stability is tied to the sign of the Laplacian correction at large l; if the S³ curvature stabilises the potential at high energies, the metastability may be an artifact of neglecting the geometric term, and the true vacuum may be absolutely stable.

## 7. What this paper does and does not accomplish

**Does.** It reframes the hierarchy problem from "why is the Higgs mass fine-tuned?" to "why is the universe large?" — which has a straightforward answer: the universe is large because it is old. It identifies the electroweak scale as the geometric resonance β = 1, a specific mathematical identification rather than an analogy. It eliminates the need for TeV-scale new physics, and the absence of BSM discoveries at the LHC is *predicted* rather than merely accommodated.

**Does not.** It does not derive λ or v from geometry; these remain measured inputs, and the resonance fixes the electroweak *scale* given them, not their values. It does not derive the particle mass spectrum; the Higgs-Laplacian eigenvalues on S³ at β = 1 do not obviously correspond to other known masses. It does not derive the cosmological scalar |Φ_NTE| or its dynamics; the evolution of r(N) under H(N) = β_NTE|Φ_NTE(N)|² is a separate component of the framework.

## 8. Conclusion

The hierarchy problem has been treated as evidence for new physics at the TeV scale; after decades of null results, an alternative deserves consideration. The electroweak scale is not fine-tuned — it is a geometric resonance, the unique scale at which a scalar field's self-interaction and a compact manifold's curvature contribute equally to the eigenvalue spectrum. For the Higgs on S³ this occurs at r = 1/m_H, and the hierarchy to the Planck scale is the ratio of the S³ radius to the Planck length — the statement that the universe has expanded by ∼10¹⁷ since the Planck epoch. Any universe old enough to have expanded past the resonance point has a Higgs mass hierarchically below the Planck mass: no cancellations, no new particles, no fine-tuning. The hierarchy is cosmological, not microphysical.

The question left open is *why S³* — why a compact 3-sphere underlies the state space. The Born rule, quantisation, and now the hierarchy all follow from S³ geometry; a framework in which S³ arises as the boundary topology of a 4D spacetime, giving a unified geometric origin for all three (with the gauge-group derivation a specific recent advance), is developed in the corpus.
