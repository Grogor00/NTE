## Issue C7.1 — §"The Strange Question" references an epigraph that no longer exists

In §"The Strange Question", paragraph 3:

> "The epigraph names the awkwardness. *In the beginning* is the phrase we reach for. *Nothing* — here: no time, no gauge group, no species yet. *Exploded* — the gauge birth, when physics as we know it arrived."

This paragraph references three terms — *In the beginning*, *Nothing*, *Exploded* — as if quoting from the chapter's epigraph. But the current Greg-ratified epigraph reads:

> *Before time, there was no order to enforce. After time, the order is locked. What we call the gauge group is what survives the locking.*

The three terms aren't in it.

The terms *do* appear in the chapter's closing line:

> "In the beginning, there was nothing — no time, no gauge structure, no mass — which exploded into everything we observe."

PC reading: the §"Strange Question" paragraph is left over from an earlier draft whose epigraph contained "In the beginning, nothing, exploded" wording. When the Pratchett-removal pass replaced the epigraph with the *Where Causality Begins / Before time, there was no order to enforce* version, this paragraph wasn't updated.

**Recommended patch C7.1:** delete the paragraph entirely. The preceding paragraph ("Your intuition will keep whispering *before*. The framework cannot use that word honestly...") already does the pre-temporal/temporal awkwardness work the deleted paragraph was trying to do via the old epigraph's terms. The chapter close still uses "In the beginning, there was nothing... which exploded" as a familiar Big-Bang handle, but it doesn't need a forward-pointer earlier in the chapter to land.

Net saving: ~40 words; removes the internal inconsistency.

Alternative patch: rewrite the paragraph to reference the current epigraph:

> "The epigraph names the awkwardness. *Before time, there was no order to enforce* says it cleanly: pre-temporally, structure is present, but the coordinate we call time isn't yet doing its job. The gauge group is what survives when temporal order is enforced."

PC preference: delete (the previous paragraph already covers this; the alternative adds words without adding content).
