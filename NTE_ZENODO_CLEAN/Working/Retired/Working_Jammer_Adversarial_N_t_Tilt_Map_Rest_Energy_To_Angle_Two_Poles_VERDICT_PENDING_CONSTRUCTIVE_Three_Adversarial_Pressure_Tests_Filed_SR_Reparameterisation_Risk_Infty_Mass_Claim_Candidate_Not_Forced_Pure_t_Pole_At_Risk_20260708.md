## §3 — Metric derivation caveats (Q1)

Under t = iΔN (canonical Wick relation), the "N–t plane" is not a genuine 2D real manifold — it's a single real direction (N) plus an analytic-continuation direction (t).

**Three interpretations:**

### §3.1 — Interpretation A: Euclidean N–t plane (2D real)

$$ds^2 = dN^2 + dt^2$$

- Positive definite metric
- Tilt is a real angle in the plane
- Under Wick t = iτ: dt² = -dτ², giving ds² = dN² - dτ² (Lorentzian)
- **Problem:** if t is emergent from N via Wick, treating them as two independent real dimensions is contradictory to core axioms
- **Verdict:** interpretation A over-endows the plane with structure not in the corpus

### §3.2 — Interpretation B: Lorentzian N–t plane

$$ds^2 = dN^2 - dt^2$$

- Mixed-signature metric
- Null cones at dt = ±dN (worldline slopes)
- Photons ride null cones at 45° (θ_photon = π/4, NOT θ_photon = 0)
- **Problem:** doesn't match AC's "m=0 → θ=0" pole
- **Verdict:** interpretation B contradicts the specified pole

### §3.3 — Interpretation C: Wick-rotated complex plane

$$t = i\Delta N \Rightarrow ds^2 = dN^2 \quad \text{(only N is real)}$$

- t is analytic continuation of N
- "Tilt" is really a Wick-rotation angle in the complex plane
- Photons: no proper time, worldline along real-N axis (θ = 0 in the Wick sense)
- Massive: proper time accumulates as ΔN progresses, "tilt" from real axis
- **This interpretation matches AC's poles.**

### §3.4 — Adversarial verdict on §3

Interpretation C is most consistent with the corpus. Under it, the N–t "plane" is a Wick-rotation complex plane, and the tilt is a rotation angle in ℂ.

For a particle with rest energy m:
