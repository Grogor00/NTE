## Issue C6.1 — §"What the Framework Adds" unification-claim paragraph restated three times in close succession (density, minor)

Three paragraphs in §6 deliver the same content in slightly different phrasings:

(¶~7) "The weaker claim is still doing structural work... What the shared Hopf-fibration structure says is that the Born rule's specific form follows from the same kind of geometric setup the framework attributes to the universe at large..."

(¶~8) "So the Born rule, in the framework, is a geometric consequence of the kind of state space quantum mechanics has — and the framework's reading of the universe says the universe has the same kind of state space, locally."

(¶~9) "This is the unification claim the chapter is making, in its honest form. The reason quantum mechanics has the specific probability rule it has is that the state space of quantum particles is a three-sphere with a U(1) phase, and the framework's reading of the universe places the same kind of structure at the heart of physics. The Born rule is what falls out when you do the geometry on a spin-1/2 system. The unification is structural at the level of *shared Hopf-fibration apparatus*, not literal identification of one S³ with another."

Three passes saying "the geometry is shared, not the literal object." The third paragraph's "unification is structural at the level of *shared Hopf-fibration apparatus*, not literal identification of one S³ with another" is the cleanest formulation and could replace ¶7 and ¶8.

**Recommended patch C6.1:** keep ¶7 (the "weaker claim is still doing structural work" framing) and ¶9 (the cleanest formulation). Delete ¶8 (the bridging restatement). Net saving: ~70 words.

## Issue C6.2 — Cross-chapter pattern: "framework's signature move" phrase

The "framework's signature move once again, applied here to [the X-and-Y pairing]" phrase pattern now appears in three consecutive chapters:

- Ch 4 §6: "The framework's signature move once again, applied here to the curvature-and-field pairing."
- Ch 5 §4: "The framework's signature move once again, applied here to the Higgs-and-|Φ_NTE| pairing"
- Ch 6 §6: "The framework's signature move once again, applied here to the quantum-mechanics-and-cosmology pairing: one geometric apparatus appearing in two domains, with both real."

Same template, three different X-and-Y pairings. Each chapter uses it once.

This is a tracking note, not a hard patch recommendation. The phrase pattern is doing legitimate callback work to Ch 1+2's "one object appearing as two" framing. But three consecutive uses in three consecutive chapters starts to read as a corpus tic rather than fresh pedagogy.

**Recommended patch C6.2 (lower priority):** consider replacing the Ch 6 §6 instance with a chapter-specific phrasing that delivers the same content without the recurring template. Possible alternative:

> Quantum mechanics's state space and the framework's universe-S³ share the same geometric apparatus. The Born rule emerges in one domain; the framework's geometric programme operates in the other; both are doing what the shared structure makes possible.

The Ch 4 §6 instance is the natural primary home for the "framework's signature move once again" callback (curvature-and-field is the most original of the three pairings, post-photon/entanglement); Ch 5 and Ch 6 instances can be reworded.

PC defers to Greg — the template might be intentional cross-chapter scaffolding. Flagging for awareness.

## Issue C6.3 — §"What This Means For The Rest of the Book" closing minor restatement (density, very minor)

The closing paragraph:
> "The rule that Max Born could not derive in 1926 is, on the framework's reading, the simplest possible probability function on the state space, given the constraints. The state space is a three-sphere. The Born rule is what falls out when you project through the Hopf fibration. The exponent of 2 is selected by the geometry quantum mechanics has — the same geometric structure the framework's reading attributes to the universe's boundary as well."

The chapter has just spent §"Why the Square Comes Out" and §"What the Framework Adds" making exactly these points. This closing paragraph functions as a chapter-end anchor — acceptable as pedagogy — but adds nothing new.

**Recommended patch C6.3 (optional):** trim to:

> The rule Max Born could not derive in 1926 is, on the framework's reading, the simplest possible probability function on a three-sphere with a U(1) phase. The exponent of 2 emerges from the geometry, not from a postulate. This is the shape of probability. It has been the shape all along.

Net saving: ~40 words.
