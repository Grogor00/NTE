## 1 — Prof. Mikhail Shaposhnikov (EPFL) — νMSM, scale invariance

**Why:** νMSM is the most economical "hidden RH-neutrino" model; Shaposhnikov's scale-invariance work bears directly on whether mass scales like M_R are fixed or free.
**NTE angle surfaced:** DM/baryon as a structural fixed ratio; derivability of M_R.

> **Subject:** A question on the dark-matter/baryon ratio and the origin of M_R
>
> Dear Professor Shaposhnikov,
>
> I'm an independent researcher developing a geometric extension to the Standard Model, and I've spent a lot of time with the νMSM — the economy of addressing dark matter, neutrino oscillations, and the baryon asymmetry with three right-handed neutrinos is remarkable.
>
> Two genuine questions, if you can spare a moment:
>
> 1. In the νMSM the dark-matter abundance is set by the production mechanism (Dodelson–Widrow mixing, or resonant production with a lepton asymmetry). Is there, in your view, any *production-independent* reason the dark-matter-to-baryon energy-density ratio lands as an O(5) number — or is that ratio necessarily a contingent outcome of the production history? I ask because in the framework I'm working on the ratio appears as a quantity fixed by structure rather than by abundance-matching, and I'm trying to understand whether the νMSM admits any such reading or is fundamentally production-determined.
>
> 2. The right-handed Majorana scale M_R enters as an input. Given your work on scale invariance and the absence of fundamental mass scales, do you regard M_R as irreducibly free, or are there constructions in which it is fixed relative to the Planck and electroweak scales?
>
> I'm happy to share what I'm doing if it's of interest, but mostly I'd value your view on the two questions above. With thanks and respect for your work,
>
> [Greg White]

## 2 — Prof. Alexander Kusenko (UCLA) — keV sterile neutrinos, PBH dark matter

**Why:** Leading proponent of keV sterile DM and of PBH/horizon-era dark-sector formation.
**NTE angle surfaced:** production-independence of the dark abundance; horizon/e-fold relations to the mass spectrum.

> **Subject:** Production-independence of the dark abundance, and horizon-scale relations
>
> Dear Professor Kusenko,
>
> I'm an independent researcher working on a geometric extension to the Standard Model, and I've followed your keV sterile-neutrino program — and the primordial-black-hole dark-matter work — with great interest.
>
> Two questions I'd genuinely value your view on:
>
> 1. Across the sterile-neutrino dark-matter scenarios, the relic abundance is set by the production channel. Is there any production-independent reason the dark-to-baryon ratio should come out near the observed O(5), or is it essentially tuned by the production history in each case? In the framework I'm developing this ratio appears to be fixed structurally, and I'm trying to understand how unavoidable the production-dependence is.
>
> 2. In the PBH / horizon-era formation scenarios, does the mechanism impose any relation between the dark-sector mass spectrum and the horizon scale or the number of e-folds — or is the spectrum essentially free? The e-fold count and a horizon/projection structure play a central role in what I'm doing, so I'm trying to learn whether horizon-era formation predicts a characteristic scale or stays agnostic.
>
> With thanks for your time and admiration for the work,
>
> [Greg White]

## 3 — Prof. Zackaria Chacko (Maryland) / Prof. Kathryn Zurek (Caltech) — Twin Higgs, mirror dark matter

**Why:** Twin Higgs solves the little hierarchy with a Z₂ mirror sector; closest "imposed discrete symmetry between sectors" analogue to NTE's orbifold-derived bilateral structure.
**NTE angle surfaced:** whether the mirror Z₂ can *descend from* a geometric/orbifold quotient rather than being postulated.

> **Subject:** The origin of the mirror Z₂ — imposed symmetry vs geometric descent
>
> Dear Professor Chacko,
>
> I'm an independent researcher developing a geometric extension to the Standard Model, and the Twin Higgs is, to my mind, one of the most elegant resolutions of the little hierarchy problem — a real economy of assumptions.
>
> My question concerns the discrete Z₂ relating our sector to the twin. In the standard construction it's imposed as a symmetry of the Lagrangian. Is there, in your view, any motivation — or any obstruction — to that mirror Z₂ *descending from* a geometric or orbifold construction (a fixed-point / quotient structure) rather than being postulated at the Lagrangian level? I ask because the framework I'm working on generates a bilateral two-sector structure from an orbifold quotient, and I'm trying to understand whether a geometric origin for the twin Z₂ would be natural, redundant, or in tension with the pseudo-Goldstone mechanism that does the protective work.
>
> Grateful for any thoughts, and for the work itself,
>
> [Greg White]
