## Issue C14.1 — §6 "trouser leg" phrasing (RETIRED — alignment fix)

**§6 (Pati and Salam, 1974)**, closing paragraph (Critical disambiguation):

> "**Critical disambiguation.** Pati–Salam needs *W*_R at some energy. The framework predicts no such boson: weak handedness is which trouser leg we stand on, not a missing particle. Same algebra, different empirical bet. Colliders will decide."

The phrase **"which trouser leg we stand on"** is the trousers-of-time metaphor Greg explicitly retired in the 2026-05-31 directive. Ch 10 §1 was patched to replace the trousers framing with branch/fork/root; Ch 14 §6 carries a leftover from the same pre-patch register.

**Recommended patch C14.1 (alignment-safe, goes into Batch 2 handoff):**

**FIND:**

> Pati–Salam needs *W*_R at some energy. The framework predicts no such boson: weak handedness is which trouser leg we stand on, not a missing particle.

**REPLACE WITH:**

> Pati–Salam needs *W*_R at some energy. The framework predicts no such boson: weak handedness is which orientation samples the chirality superposition, not a missing particle.

Or alternatively (keeping the spatial metaphor without trousers):

> Pati–Salam needs *W*_R at some energy. The framework predicts no such boson: weak handedness is which branch the local apparatus stands on, not a missing particle.

PC preference: the first option ("which orientation samples the chirality superposition") is more precise — it reads back to Ch 8's chirality-superposition + opposite-phase-strobe structure that the framework actually commits to, rather than substituting one metaphor for another. The second option is more compact if shorter prose is preferred.

**Add to Batch 2 patch handoff.**

## Issue C14.2 — §5 "eighth colour" reference may confuse reader (minor — not a patch)

**§5 (Penrose and Marcolli — patches without points)**, "Particles as islands" paragraph:

> "...the count of **eight** fermionic slots per generation tracks octonion dimensionality directly. An older draft called this an 'eighth colour'; that metaphor was cut. The number comes from the algebra, not from a paint box."

The phrase "An older draft called this an 'eighth colour'; that metaphor was cut" refers to a *fermionic-slot-count* eighth-colour framing that was indeed cut. But the book still has Ch 5 titled "The Eighth Colour" using the metaphor for |Φ_NTE| as the framework's added scalar field.

These are two different "eighth colour" metaphors:
- **Ch 5's eighth colour** (active in the book): |Φ_NTE| as the additional scalar field doing geometric work the SM's seven inventory ingredients don't address
- **Ch 14 §5's "older draft eighth colour"** (cut): the 8-slot fermionic count per generation

A reader holding Ch 5's eighth-colour framing might briefly wonder why Ch 14 says "that metaphor was cut" — the contexts are sufficiently different (scalar field vs fermionic-slot count) that the confusion likely resolves quickly, but PC flags this for awareness.

**No patch recommended.** The §5 sentence is a Ch-14-specific note about a different metaphor; rewriting to disambiguate from Ch 5's metaphor would add bulk without value. The reader's brief moment of "wait, didn't we keep that?" is acceptable cost.

PC mentions this only because the broader alignment audit (Parts 1 and 2) may want to harmonise the "eighth colour" usage across the book. If Ch 5's metaphor is later revised in the alignment pass, this Ch 14 §5 reference becomes simpler.

## What works in Ch 14

**Engage floor:** "You have now met the photon, the Hopf fibration, the gauge birth, the neutrino, κ, the chord, and the cosmological constant — each time with a named piece of mathematics doing the heavy lifting. This chapter steps back and asks a simpler question: **why does the same old mathematics keep showing up?**" Strong reset framing.

**Fleeting-glimpse:** The §1 Hopf table (where you met it / what Hopf is doing across QM, weak-force geometry, Bell/Tsirelson, κ₀) is the clearest fleeting-glimpse in the chapter — one table summarises four chapter-appearances of the same primitive.

**Schema-attachment:** Wigner epigraph ("the miracle of the appropriateness of the language of mathematics for the formulation of the laws of physics is a wonderful gift which we neither understand nor deserve") carries the chapter's central question.

**Earned-term defer:** Each rhyme introduced via short historical context — Hopf 1931, Hurwitz 1898, Connes-Rovelli 1994 — with the technical term following the picture.

**Honest scope:** Strong throughout. The opening framing explicitly: "Rhymes are not proof. They show the framework is working in well-mapped territory, not on the fringe. Whether the map matches reality is still decided by the predictions in Chapter 15 — and by measurements, not by elegance alone." §9 closes with: "It is still not demonstration. Demonstration is κ and *r* and *w* and the score in Chapter 15. The rhymes tell you the question is serious and the tools to test it exist." Clean.

**Critical disambiguation discipline:** §6 explicitly names where Pati-Salam and the framework agree (the algebra of two-sector content) and where they differ (Pati-Salam needs W_R; framework predicts no such boson). The discipline is right; the prose carrying it has the retired-phrasing issue C14.1 above.

**Attribution discipline:** §8 ("§5 named Penrose for accessibility. The Palatial technical content is **joint work with Matilde Marcolli** (2009 and following)... Naming the accessible source without the earlier technical source would fail the same honesty this chapter asks of physics. The correction is recorded here on purpose.") is the kind of explicit-credit discipline the book needs throughout.

**Density:** No redundant restatements found. Each rhyme is one section; each section is tight.

**Forward-momentum:** Each section advances the catalogue. §9's three consequences (tools exist / authorship is in the commitments / rhymes are signal, not verdict) recapitulate the chapter's argument without restating individual rhymes.

**Engagement:** Strong rhetorical close: "Wigner called mathematics's fit to physics *unreasonable* and left *why* open. One reading — not the only one — is that physics is the search for commitments that force the right classical structures, and mathematics has been cataloguing those structures all along. The framework made one guess about the commitments. The comprehensive rhyme list is what came back."
