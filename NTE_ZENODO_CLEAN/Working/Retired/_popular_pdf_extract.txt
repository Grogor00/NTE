The Null-Time Extension to the Standard Model, Explained From First Principles
There is a fact buried in your physics textbook that nobody has fully followed to its conclusion.
A photon of light — any photon, ever — experiences zero time between the moment it’s created and the
moment it’s absorbed. Not very little time. Zero. From the photon’s perspective (to the extent that word
means anything for an object with no clock), emission and absorption are the same instant. The entire
journey from a star four hundred light-years away to your eye happens, for the photon, in no time at all.
Every physicist knows this. It follows directly from Einstein’s special relativity, one of the most tested
theories in all of science. And then, almost universally, physicists set it aside and get on with their
calculations.
This paper is what happens when you don’t set it aside.

A Note on Method
This paper is not a claim that NTE is the correct theory of fundamental physics. It’s a working bet —
the kind of bet Feynman described in QED when he asked why all theories of physics look so similar in
structure. He named three possibilities. The first is that physicists are limited in their imagination, fitting
new phenomena into frameworks they already have. The second is that Nature genuinely has only one
way of doing things and repeats her story. The third is that apparent similarities are because we are seeing
fingers of the same hand.
NTE is built on a working bet that the second and third are operative — that when multiple mathematical
descriptions agree on a phenomenon, the agreement is not coincidence but evidence of a common
underlying structure, and that the task of theoretical unification is to find the structure rather than to
choose between the descriptions.
This is a bet, not a law. It can be wrong. The framework’s success or failure will be measured the way
any theoretical framework is measured: predictions that hold, derivations that survive scrutiny, and the
willingness of the structure to keep simplifying as new observations come in. Beauty — the sense that a
description is finding its natural form — is a useful guide to which path to try first. It’s not a shortcut past
empirical test.
The rest of this paper is what the bet has produced so far.

The Universe as an Expanding Surface
To understand what this means, let’s build a picture of the universe from scratch.
Forget the idea that the universe began in a point and expanded into some pre-existing void. In
NTE, our entire three-dimensional universe is the surface of a four-dimensional sphere — a closed
three-dimensional space curved in a way our visual intuition cannot directly picture, but which is
mathematically straightforward.
The closest everyday analogy is a balloon, but it has to be used carefully. Imagine an ant on the surface
of an inflating balloon. The ant has access only to the surface. It can walk in any direction along the
surface; it cannot move “up” off the surface or “down” into the surface. For the ant, the surface is the
entire universe. Distant points on the balloon recede from each other as the balloon inflates, with no
centre of expansion that the ant can point to from within the surface. Every point looks central; expansion
is happening everywhere.
The analogy carries us most of the way. Where it fails — and this is critical — is that an actual rubber
balloon has an inside, full of air. We can see this inside; the ant cannot. NTE makes a stronger claim than
that the inside is inaccessible: it asserts that the inside is not a place at all. The mathematical description
of our universe involves a four-dimensional geometric structure whose surface is what we live on, but the
four-dimensional “interior” is not a region of space we could in principle reach with better instruments.
There is no inside to point to. There is only the surface, and the history of how the surface has expanded.
To see why this is more than a denial, consider what happens at the limit where our universe’s curvature
becomes very gentle — locally, where the radius of the four-sphere is enormous. The surface looks
flat. From within a flat region of the surface, the directions “up out of the surface” and “down into the
surface” become indistinguishable: there is no preferred orientation, just two equally-available sides of
the same locally-flat sheet. This is what NTE means by saying the bilateral structure of the universe is two
orientations of one manifold rather than two regions of space. The surface has, in effect, two sides — like
the two sides of a sheet of paper — and the two sides are physically real but neither is “inside” the other.
We will return to this when we discuss the manifold’s thickness in the next section.
The expansion of the surface does not happen through time. The expansion is the fundamental process,
measured not in seconds but in a dimensionless count of how many times the universe has doubled in size.
This count is called the e-fold number N, and it plays the role that time plays in standard cosmology —
except that it requires no clocks, no pre-existing temporal background, nothing but geometry.
Now the boundary expansion field enters. The relationship is beautifully simple: the rate of expansion
equals a constant (β_NTE, which describes how efficiently the boundary expansion field translates
expansion into time) multiplied by the square of the field strength. For matter on the boundary, each
expansion step corresponds to a measurable amount of time elapsed. The deeper a particle’s engagement
with |Φ_NTE|, the deeper it sits in the fabric of time, and the slower it experiences expansion from its own
frame of reference — which is exactly what we call gravitational time dilation. Clocks tick faster in deep
space than on the surface of a dense star, because on the star’s surface the boundary expansion field is
slightly weaker (responding to the strong curvature) and parameterises slightly less time per expansion
step.

Entanglement: the Photon’s Trick on a Larger Scale
The photon’s null-time was the first clue. If emission and absorption can be the same event on the
expansion wavefront, the same geometry may work for entities that are not photons.
The answer the framework gives is yes, and the name for it is quantum entanglement.
When two particles are prepared in an entangled state, then separated — sometimes by metres in a
laboratory, sometimes in principle by light-years — measurements made on one of them turn out to be
correlated with measurements made on the other. The correlations are not what you would expect if
each particle carried its measurement outcomes around with it as a hidden property. They follow specific
patterns, mathematically described, and the patterns have been confirmed in laboratory experiments
thousands of times since the 1980s. The 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded to three of the
experimenters who confirmed it most carefully.
The standard interpretation of this fact has tied physicists in knots for ninety years. Either the particles are
communicating across whatever distance separates them (which seems to violate relativity), or they are not
really connected and the correlations are some kind of measurement artefact (which is hard to reconcile
with the precision of the experimental results). Both options have generated reams of philosophical
literature. Neither has produced a picture a physics student can hold in their head without disclaimers.
The framework’s claim is that the standard interpretations have accepted the wrong starting assumption:
that there are two particles. Once we relax that assumption, the strangeness dissolves.
The two entangled particles are not two particles connected by some mysterious link. They are two
endpoints of a single geometric object — the same kind of object the photon is, scaled up to a context

being on the wavefront the whole time, with no proper time accumulated along the way, with no need

The Particle That Bridges Both Orientations

The Arrow of Time
Physicists have asked for over a century why the past is fixed and the future open, while the fundamental
equations treat past and future alike.
The fundamental equations of physics — quantum mechanics, general relativity, the Standard Model — do
not distinguish between past and future. They are time-symmetric: a movie of any process that obeys
these equations could be played backwards and would still describe a physically valid process. And yet
our experience of time is asymmetric in the strongest possible way. The future is open and undetermined;
the past is fixed and unalterable. We remember backwards, not forwards. Eggs break, they do not unbreak.
The arrow of time points one way, decisively.
The standard explanation for this asymmetry, going back to Boltzmann in the nineteenth century, is
statistical: entropy increases because there are vastly more disordered states than ordered ones, so any
closed system tends statistically toward disorder over time. This is a real explanation as far as it goes, but it
explains the direction of time only by assuming that the universe started in an extraordinarily low-entropy
state. The deep question — why the initial state was so special — is then pushed back to cosmology, where
it remains unresolved.
NTE offers a different account. Time does not have a direction because of statistics. Time has a direction
because of geometry, and the geometric mechanism is the locking transition we have been describing.
When the early universe crossed N_lock — the e-fold at which gauge symmetry switched on, the boundary
expansion field |Φ_NTE| stabilised, and the Standard Model Higgs acquired its identity as the SU(2) gauge

Why Quantum Mechanics Is Geometry
The picture gets stranger, and then it gets cleaner.
The state of a quantum particle — the full description of its physical possibilities — lives on a geometric
object called a three-sphere. The framework’s reading of the universe places the same kind of three-sphere
structure at the heart of cosmology — the universe’s boundary is a three-sphere with a U(1) phase to be
quotiented through the Hopf fibration, just as the quantum state space is. The two contexts share the
same Hopf-fibration apparatus, even if the framework does not claim they are literally identified as one
geometric object playing both roles. The Born rule — the quantum mechanical formula that tells you the
probability of a measurement outcome, first written down in 1926 and treated as an axiom ever since —
turns out to follow necessarily from the geometry of this sphere, in either context.
The argument is almost embarrassingly simple in shape, once you see it (although the rigorous version,
developed in companion paper 2 of the NTE corpus, requires more mathematical detail than this paper
can carry). The state of a quantum particle is a point on a three-sphere. Two points on that sphere that
differ only by an overall phase factor (a rotation by an undetectable angle) represent the same physical
state — this is the unobservability of global phase, built into quantum mechanics from the beginning.
The geometric structure that implements this identification is called the Hopf fibration: a specific way of
projecting the three-sphere onto a two-sphere, discovered by the mathematician Heinz Hopf in 1931.
The probability formula P = |⟨ψ|φ⟩|² — the Born rule, the thing every quantum mechanics student
learns to compute — is the only smooth, symmetric, properly normalised function you can write on the
Hopf-projected sphere. At the level of mathematical detail beyond the scope of this paper, it can be shown

The Moment Physics Came Into Being
The framework’s most dramatic point is the origin of time itself.
When the universe had not yet expanded beyond a tiny fraction of an e-fold — when the expansion count
N was near zero — there was no time, in the sense that no entity had yet accumulated any. This is not a
metaphysical claim but a mathematical one: temporal duration accumulates at a rate set by the boundary
expansion field |Φ_NTE|, and in the earliest expansion, |Φ_NTE| had not yet stabilised into a form that
gives a well-defined dt/dN. The Standard Model Higgs as we know it — the SU(2) gauge doublet that gives
mass through Yukawa coupling — did not yet exist either, because the gauge structure within which it
is defined had not yet emerged. Both fields’ identities are products of the locking transition that makes
Standard Model physics operate.
In this pre-temporal epoch, something remarkable was absent: the entire physical content of the Standard
Model. Gauge symmetry — the deep principle underlying electromagnetism, the weak force, and the strong
force — requires a timelike dimension to be defined. The photon, which is the carrier of electromagnetic
force, travels along null geodesics — zero-time paths — that only have meaning once time exists from
which to be null. The gluon, carrier of the strong force, lives on a geometric bundle that requires a full
spacetime with a temporal direction. Without time, there are no photons. Without photons, there is no
electromagnetism. Without electromagnetism, there are no atoms, no stars, no galaxies, no us.

The Algebraic Structure of Creation
If you have been paying attention, you may have noticed that the framework keeps encountering the

This convergence has a deep explanation. There is a result in pure mathematics — proved independently
by Hurwitz in 1898 and in full generality in the 1950s and 1960s — that says there are exactly four number

complex numbers, the quaternions, and the octonions. Only four. Not an infinite family, not even a large
finite family. Four.
The octonions are the strangest of these. Unlike the others, multiplication in the octonions is not
associative: you can get a different answer depending on which multiplication you perform first.
(Multiplication of ordinary numbers is associative — (2 × 3) × 4 gives the same result as 2 × (3 × 4) —
and most readers have never met a multiplication that wasn’t.) This seems like a defect, but in NTE it’s
a feature. In the pre-temporal epoch — where there is no time, and therefore no temporal ordering of
operations — there is no reason for multiplication to be associative. The lack of temporal ordering means
you literally cannot say whether operation A came before operation B, so the question of whether (A
times B) times C equals A times (B times C) has no physical content.
The pre-temporal universe is octonionic. Its symmetry group is called G₂ — the smallest of the exceptional
Lie groups that mathematicians discovered in the nineteenth century. The technical name does not matter
for the present purpose; what matters is that this group is fully classified by mathematics, and its properties
are completely known.
At the gauge birth, causality appears. Time gives ordering to operations. The algebra is forced to become
associative — there now is a fact of the matter about which operation happened first. With associativity
restored, the octonions can no longer be the algebra of physics. The next-richest associative algebra in the
Hurwitz hierarchy is the quaternions, and the framework’s claim is that the gauge birth is precisely the
algebraic reduction from octonions to quaternions, mediated by the emergence of temporal order.

out of this reduction. When the octonionic G₂ symmetry breaks under the constraint that operations must

Model gauge group: the symmetry structure of the strong force, the weak force, and electromagnetism.
Every gauge boson, every quark, every charged lepton — the entire interacting machinery of the Standard

Hubble Tension and Dark Energy as One Phenomenon
There are two recent observational puzzles in cosmology that current theories treat as separate problems.
NTE treats them as one phenomenon, viewed two ways.
The first is the Hubble tension. The expansion rate of the universe today, called the Hubble parameter H₀,
can be measured by two methods. The first uses observations of the cosmic microwave background — the
relic radiation from when the universe was 380,000 years old — and standard cosmology to back-calculate
the present-day expansion rate. The second uses observations of supernovae and other “standard candles”
in the relatively local universe to measure the expansion rate directly. The two methods should give the
same answer. They do not. The CMB-based method gives about 67 km/s/Mpc; the local supernova method
gives about 73 km/s/Mpc. The discrepancy is several standard deviations and has resisted explanation for
nearly a decade.
The second is the dark energy puzzle. The simplest cosmological model has dark energy as a perfect
cosmological constant — a fixed energy density associated with empty space, with an “equation of state”
parameter w equal to exactly minus one. Recent results from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument
(DESI) suggest that w may not be exactly minus one at low redshift; it appears slightly above. If this signal
persists, it would suggest dark energy is not a constant but is evolving — which would require new physics
beyond the standard model of cosmology.
NTE’s claim is that both puzzles arise from the same mechanism, and the mechanism is already in the
framework: the spatial variation of the boundary expansion field |Φ_NTE| in regions of low matter density.
In the framework’s picture, the local rate of cosmic expansion at any point is set by the local strength of
|Φ_NTE|, through H = β_NTE|Φ_NTE|². Most of the universe by volume is in cosmic voids — vast regions
essentially empty of matter. In voids, |Φ_NTE| is at its asymptotic value. In matter-rich regions like the
surroundings of galaxies, |Φ_NTE| is slightly suppressed by the gravitational potential. (This is one of the
structural differences between |Φ_NTE| and the Standard Model Higgs the reader knows from CERN: the
SM Higgs is locked at 246 GeV everywhere by the steepness of its potential and does not vary with local
mass-energy density. The boundary expansion field, by contrast, does vary spatially and cosmologically,
and that variation is what the framework reads as the source of gravitational time dilation.) Because
most of the universe is voids, supernovae we observe at low redshift are mostly seen through voids —
and through regions where |Φ_NTE|² is at its higher asymptotic value, which means the locally observed
expansion rate is slightly higher than the cosmic mean. This produces the elevated H₀ measured by local
supernova surveys. The CMB-derived value, which integrates over the whole expansion history rather

The dark energy story is the same mechanism viewed differently. Dark energy is, in the framework, the
