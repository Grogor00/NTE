## §1 — Susskind 2026 ("Spontaneous Breaking of T in de Sitter") — DETAILED REFRAMING

### §1.1 Susskind's core claims (one paragraph each)

**Claim S1 (T-symmetric vacuum unstable):** The Hartle-Hawking de Sitter vacuum (maximally mixed thermofield-double) is T-symmetric but violates cluster decomposition: ⟨gs|ZpZh|gs⟩ ≠ 0 for arbitrarily distant p, h. The symmetric "cat state" |U⟩+|D⟩ is unstable to slight perturbation.

**Claim S2 (Feature/clock breaks T):** Any forward-going clock (FGC) or backward-going clock (BGC) — i.e., any time-reversal-asymmetric structure — at the pode (or anywhere) selects one branch and restores cluster decomposition. The probability for a feature ∼ exp(−2πℓM) is exponentially small for macroscopic mass M but **order unity** for features at Gibbons-Hawking temperature.

**Claim S3 (Combined T includes clock):** In a closed universe, the time-reversal operator T must act on the whole system + clock. Ideal Newtonian time t is replaced by clock time τ; T τ T = τ (clock position invariant); T π T = −π (clock momentum reversed).

**Claim S4 (T plausibly gauge):** Following Harlow-Numasawa, T is best understood as a gauge symmetry. Observables must be dressed: C̄ = C·Π₋, where Π₋ = Π_f − Π_b is the T-odd projection difference. Then ⟨C̄⟩ ≠ 0 in T-invariant states.

**Claim S5 (Feature anywhere; horizon dual):** Generalisation (§6): the laboratory can be at the pode and the feature at (the stretched) horizon. As long as the horizon carries a T-asymmetric fluctuation, the pode-laboratory sees semiclassical correlations.
