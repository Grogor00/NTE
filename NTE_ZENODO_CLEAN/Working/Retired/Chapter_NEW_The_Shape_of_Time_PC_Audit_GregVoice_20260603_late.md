## §2 — What needs sharpening (loose stitching)

These claims contain real intent but the voice phrasing is imprecise enough that taking them into prose would muddy the chapter.

### 2.1 — "Time IS everything because the now is everything"

**Problem:** Conflates *time-as-parameter* with *now-as-state*. They're related but structurally distinct:

- The **now** is "what currently exists" — the framework's stance that past and future don't exist
- **Time** is a derived parameter — what change-in-N looks like locally for massive matter

Saying *"time is the now"* identifies a parameter with a state. The chapter can hold "only the now exists" cleanly without that identification. The honest version is *"the now is what currently exists; time is what change-in-N looks like to observers riding that now."*

### 2.2 — "Thickness of the now"

**Problem:** Ambiguous. Three candidate readings:

- **(a)** Bilateral spatial thickness between +N and −N orientations of the manifold (existing framework geometry; supports cross-thickness gravity)
- **(b)** Temporal thickness in N — the now has finite ΔN-extent (Option C from Flag 1 this session, which we explicitly declined to commit to)
- **(c)** Loose phrasing for "structural extent" — i.e., the now's geometric content beyond a sharp dividing line

The chapter must disambiguate before any prose. If (a), then "thickness of the now" is the bilateral structure (and that's a meaningful claim). If (b), we're committing to something we didn't agree to. If (c), the phrasing needs replacement with something more precise.

**PC view:** the now's "largeness" is already settled (Flag 1 Option B — null-time bridges). Introducing "thickness" as a new concept on top of that risks introducing fuzz. Recommendation: don't use "thickness of the now" in prose; use the established "the now is the S³ surface plus the null-time bridges across it" framing.

### 2.3 — "Time is confined within that surface"

**Problem:** Loose. Time isn't *spatially confined* to the S³ (it's not 3D-located on the surface). It's *structurally derived* from the surface's expansion.

**Honest version:** *"Time is what the surface's expansion produces, not something pasted onto the surface from outside."* Or: *"There is no time-dimension external to the surface; what we call time is the rate at which proper time accumulates per unit expansion of the surface itself."*

## §3 — Invalid as stated, but contains a real insight (LOAD-BEARING — extract carefully)

### 3.1 — The invalid inference

Greg's argument structure:
1. Time = expansion
2. The photon doesn't participate in expansion
3. Therefore the photon doesn't participate in time
4. (Time is expansion)
5. ∴ The photon doesn't participate in spatial dimensions either

**The leap from step 3 to step 5 doesn't hold.** "No proper time" doesn't logically yield "no proper distance" — they're structurally distinct properties. A particle that doesn't accumulate proper time still has spatial coordinates.

So the inference as stated is invalid. If taken into the chapter, it would be a soft spot that any careful reader would catch.

### 3.2 — The real insight underneath

There IS a sharp structural argument that delivers what Greg is reaching for. The honest version:

> *Expansion is the substrate of both time and space in the framework. The expanding S³ IS the spatial dimensions; the expansion rate IS what produces time. Massive matter participates in expansion — accumulates proper time, has proper distance. The photon doesn't participate in expansion — from its own frame, it has neither time nor distance. The photon's null status in BOTH dimensions isn't a coincidence of light-cone geometry; it's because the photon is the entity the expansion carries without claiming.*

This is structurally precise and ties together what's special about the photon:

- **Massive matter participates** in expansion → accumulates proper time AND occupies proper distance
- **The photon rides** expansion without participating → null in proper time AND null in proper distance (from its frame)
- The two-fold nullity of the photon isn't a coincidence; it's the structural consequence of non-participation in the substrate that produces both dimensions

### 3.3 — How this extends the two-registers-of-being scaffolding

The earlier scaffolding (filed in `Chapter_NEW_The_Shape_of_Time_Scaffolding_Photon_Existence_PC_20260603.md`) said:

- Massive matter exists by **persisting through time**
- The photon exists by **binding events** — a structural connection between two surface events

This audit's clean version EXTENDS that:

- **Massive matter persists** in time AND space — because it participates in the expansion that produces both
- **The photon binds** across time AND space — because it doesn't participate in the expansion that produces either; it rides the substrate without entering it

This is a stronger, more structurally precise version of the two-registers framing. It says WHY the photon is null in both dimensions (non-participation in the substrate), not just that it is.
