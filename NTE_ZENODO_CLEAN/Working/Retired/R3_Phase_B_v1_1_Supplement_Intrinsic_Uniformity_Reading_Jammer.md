## §2 — Refined §2 reading (gauge generator identification)

### §2.1 What v1 §2 said (preserved as meta-vantage description)

v1 working note §2 framed the gauge structures as:
- SU(2)_L = +N-vantage projector (vantage-specific)
- SU(2)_R = −N-vantage projector (vantage-specific)
- SU(4)_c = sector-symmetric (universal across both sectors)

This is a **meta-vantage description** — what an outside observer comparing the two sectors sees. The "vantage-specific weak vs sector-symmetric colour" asymmetry, framed this way, is descriptively accurate but looks somewhat arbitrary (why would weak forces be vantage-specific and the colour force not?).

### §2.2 The intrinsic-uniformity refinement

Under Greg's δN observation, the asymmetry resolves to *uniformity from each sector's intrinsic perspective*. From inside each sector:

- The **local-δN-aligned SU(2) gauge** acts on whatever chirality content projects onto the local δN direction. From +N's intrinsic perspective, this is "the SU(2) that gauges local-δN-aligned content" — which from +N looks like LH content. From −N's intrinsic perspective, this is also "the SU(2) that gauges local-δN-aligned content" — which from −N intrinsically also looks like its own "LH" content (which globally is RH because −N's δN points the opposite global direction).
- The **SU(4)_c colour-lepton gauge** acts on local content in each sector. Because δN is intrinsically the same in both sectors, the SU(4)_c gauge looks the same from both intrinsic perspectives — there's no chirality flip to relabel.

**All three gauge structures are intrinsically uniform across both sectors.** From each sector's own δN-aligned perspective, the gauge structures act identically. The "SU(2)_L vs SU(2)_R" distinction is a global meta-vantage labelling artifact, not an intrinsic structural difference.

### §2.3 What this changes (substantively)

v1 reading suggested two distinct SU(2) gauge structures (one per sector) plus one universal SU(4)_c. Under intrinsic-uniformity reading:

- There is structurally **one local-δN-aligned SU(2) gauge**, which manifests as "what BF 2020 calls SU(2)_L" from +N's intrinsic perspective and as "what BF 2020 calls SU(2)_R" from −N's intrinsic perspective. From a global meta-vantage comparing both sectors, the two intrinsic SU(2)s appear as distinct opposite-chirality gauges. Intrinsically they're one structure, CPN-mirrored across the Janus boundary.
- The SU(4)_c colour-lepton gauge is similarly intrinsically uniform — but doesn't appear differently from the meta-vantage because no chirality flip is involved.

**The asymmetry I noted in v1** (vantage-specific weak vs sector-symmetric colour) **isn't structurally arbitrary** — it's the natural consequence of: (i) two intrinsically identical SU(2)s being mirrored by CPN, which looks like two different gauges from the meta-vantage; (ii) one intrinsically identical SU(4)_c not being mirrored by any chirality-flip, so it looks the same from the meta-vantage.

This is a tighter and more elegant structural reading than v1's. The asymmetry isn't arbitrary; it's the meta-vantage signature of CPN-mirroring acting differently on chirality-bearing vs chirality-blind gauges.
