## §1 — Task (a): M_ν* vs CPN — the deposit-blocking verification

### §1.1 — P0.10b explicitly

From Paper Susskind v0.4 (P0.10b, ratified this session as canonical CPN definition):
$$\mathrm{CPN}(a, b) = (\bar{b}, \bar{a}) \quad \text{for } (a, b) \in A_F = \mathbb{O}_+ \oplus \mathbb{O}_-$$

This is antilinear (bar), sector-swapping (a in +N slot → b̄ position; b in −N slot → ā position). J² = I; modular conjugation of the Janus crossed product.

### §1.2 — CPN action on a mass matrix in the +N sector

For a mass matrix M_+ localized in +N (represented as (M_+, 0) in the direct sum), P0.10b gives:
$$\mathrm{CPN}((M_+, 0)) = (\bar{0}, \bar{M_+}) = (0, M_+^*)$$

**So CPN(M_+) = M_+* IN THE −N SECTOR.** The result is CROSS-SECTOR.

For a CPN-symmetric bilateral pair (M_+, M_−) with M_− = M_+*, applying CPN gives (M_+*, M_+*)... wait, let me redo:
$$\mathrm{CPN}((M_+, M_-)) = (\bar{M_-}, \bar{M_+}) = (M_-^*, M_+^*)$$

CPN-fixed point: (M_-*, M_+*) = (M_+, M_−) → M_+ = M_-*, M_- = M_+*. Both conditions equivalent: **M_- = M_+*.**

### §1.3 — Comparing to Scratch's M vs M* freedom

Scratch's Piece A found: within our +N sector (fixed matter B > 0), both M_ν and M_ν* satisfy the reflection R(M) = M and give opposite sign(δ_J). This is a WITHIN-SECTOR freedom.

**Under P0.10b:**
- Within the +N sector, we choose M_+ = either M or M*. Nothing in CPN constrains this choice within our sector.
- Bilateral CPN correlates our sector's choice with the mirror sector's mass matrix: if our +N has M, then −N has M* by CPN symmetry; if our +N has M*, then −N has M.

**So the M vs M* freedom is within-sector CP (complex conjugation on the mass matrix in our sector), NOT the full CPN (which is cross-sector).**

**CPN carries MORE than CP: specifically, the sector-swap part.**

**Verdict on §1.4:** the C part (complex conjugation) IS what turns M into M*. The P and N parts don't add flavor-basis modifications. The SECTOR-SWAP is the extra beyond CP.

**Attack:** the octonions are 𝕆 = ℍ ⊕ ℍℓ as ℝ-vector spaces (dim 8). The spacetime Dirac algebra is Cl(3,1) ≅ M_4(ℝ) ≅ ℍ ⊗ ℍ (dim 16).

**Verdict on Q2:** decorative. "Space-quaternion + time-quaternion = spacetime" is TWO TRUE FACTS standing next to each other (𝕆 = ℍ ⊕ ℍℓ; Cl(3,1) ≅ ℍ ⊗ ℍ) with NO mechanism connecting them.

## §3 — Task (c): Temporal S³ Second H forced vs permitted

### §3.1 — Sub-probe 1 (σ_ℍ identity): PIVOTAL

**The pivot:** does σ_ℍ (the G_2-involution fixing ℍ via SO(4) ⊂ G_2 stabilizer) also RELATE ℍ and ℍℓ as space ↔ time, or does it just FIX ℍ?

**Analysis:** σ_ℍ is the Cayley-Dickson involution that acts as +1 on ℍ and −1 on ℍℓ. Its fixed set is ℍ (the space quaternion). Its −1 eigenspace is ℍℓ.

**But σ_ℍ does NOT INTERCHANGE ℍ and ℍℓ.** It DISTINGUISHES them. To PAIR (ℍ, ℍℓ) as (space, time) via a swap-like involution, we'd need an involution swap: σ_swap(ℍ) = ℍℓ and σ_swap(ℍℓ) = ℍ.

**Such an involution exists in the automorphism group of 𝕆 (it's related to G_2's structure), but it is NOT σ_ℍ.** σ_ℍ fixes ℍ; it doesn't swap.

**Verdict on sub-probe 1:** σ_ℍ does NOT pair space-ℍ with time-ℍ. Forcing via σ_ℍ FAILS.

### §3.2 — Sub-probe 2 (order into ℝ, phase into S³)

The polar decomposition q = |q| · û gives:
- |q| ∈ ℝ_+: monotonic modulus (candidate for "order")
- û ∈ S³: unit direction (candidate for "phase")

**Is this decomposition forced by non-orderability of ℍ?**

ℍ is NOT orderable as a field (it's non-commutative). The only orderable subfield is ℝ. So if TIME is orderable (before/after distinction), the order must reduce to ℝ.

**Verdict on sub-probe 2:** the reduction of ORDER to the ℝ_+ factor IS forced by ℍ's non-orderability. But this ONLY tells us that "order" is in the modulus — it doesn't force S³ to be "phase" of time; S³ could be many things (spatial state space, gauge orbit, etc.).

**Sub-probe 2 verdict:** partial forcing on ORDER-into-ℝ; no forcing on S³-into-time-PHASE.

### §3.3 — Sub-probe 3 (which imaginary axis Wick-rotates)

In ℍ there is an S² of square-roots-of −1 (any unit imaginary quaternion). "Which i does the rotation" is itself a point on S².

**If time is a chosen imaginary axis:** which axis is chosen? Under G_2 automorphisms, all imaginary units are equivalent, so the CHOICE of Wick-rotation axis is a labeling.

**Under gauge birth,** the electroweak VEV selects e_1 ∈ Im(𝕆) as the physical direction (colour singlet, hypercharge axis, per Part 2 §VIII.8.5-8.6). But e_1 is used for GAUGE structure (SU(3)_C stabilizer, U(1)_Y), NOT for time Wick rotation.

**Is the Wick-rotation imaginary axis the SAME as e_1?** No canonical corpus statement makes this identification.

**Sub-probe 3 verdict:** the "chosen imaginary axis" for Wick rotation is not canonically identified in the corpus with any specific direction. Permitted-only; not forced.

### §3.4 — Sub-probe 4 (one-time reduction): HARD GATE

The 23:15Z handoff calls this the hard gate: "if you can't get it down to one observed time, say so plainly."

**Analysis:**
- 4D time (S³ + ℝ_+ = ℝ⁴) has 2T problems (as discussed §2.3)
- Hopf reduction S³ → S² × S¹ COULD produce 1D observable time, but is not forced
- Under polar decomposition (order in ℝ, phase in S³), time IS 1D (just the modulus) — but then the S³ is not "time," it's PHASE of something

**Under the polar-decomposition interpretation, the "one time" is N (radial), and S³ isn't really "time" at all. This makes the proposal consistent but also unremarkable (it's what we already have in the corpus).**

**Sub-probe 4 verdict:** one-time reduction requires either a forced Hopf reduction (not present) or reinterpreting S³ as phase-not-time (in which case the proposal doesn't add new work).

### §3.5 — Overall task (c) verdict

**PERMITTED-ONLY.** Sub-probe 1 pivot fails (σ_ℍ does not pair ℍ, ℍℓ as space-time). Sub-probes 2-4 give partial forcing on order-into-ℝ but not on temporal S³ interpretation. Under the polar decomposition escape, the S³ becomes phase-not-time — the "time as S³" idea doesn't earn new derivations.
