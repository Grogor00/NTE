## Issue C8.1 — §"What the Underlying Symmetry Looks Like to Us" structural-conclusion restated four times (density — primary issue)

The section's central structural claim — *the asymmetry is in which phase of the spinning coin each orientation's strobe catches, not in the underlying neutrino* — gets stated, then restated, then restated again, then re-summarised.

**First statement (after the strobe analogy lands):**
> "This sorts apart the asymmetry that is structurally real from the asymmetry that is meta-structural. The coin itself — the underlying chirality superposition — is symmetric; both faces are equally present, and the spin is equally accessible from any vantage. The two cameras' phase relationship is what produces the apparent one-sidedness in each camera's record."

**Second statement (next paragraph):**
> "The +N camera always sees heads because it always samples at the heads-forward phase. The −N camera, if we could observe it, would always see tails because it samples at the tails-forward phase. Each camera's vantage is its phase position relative to the spin."

Restates the previous paragraph in the +N/−N camera language. Could be collapsed.

**Third statement (two paragraphs later):**
> "This is the structural answer to the question 'why does the Standard Model see only left-handed neutrinos.' Our orientation's apparatus samples the underlying chirality superposition at one phase of its cycle; the mirror orientation's apparatus would sample at the opposite phase. The asymmetry the Standard Model observes is the asymmetry between two phase-samples of the same underlying symmetric configuration. Both orientations are seeing the same neutrino — same coin, opposite phases of the strobe."

Third pass of the same structural claim. "Both orientations are seeing the same neutrino — same coin, opposite phases of the strobe" is the cleanest formulation of the four.

**Fourth statement (next paragraph):**
> "This deepens the bilateral support consequence from the previous section. The neutrino has bilateral support not just because it's unprojected; it has bilateral support because the left-handed and right-handed halves of its chirality superposition have their primary support at opposite phases of the underlying rhythm — the +N orientation samples one half; the −N orientation samples the other. The neutrino's wavefunction straddles the manifold's bilateral structure in the handedness basis, with the two orientations being the two phase-positions from which the same underlying spin gets sampled. This straddling is what bilateral support structurally means."

Fourth pass. Same content again in slightly different framing.

**Fifth statement (in the parity-violation paragraph that follows):**
> "The neutrino itself is parity-symmetric in its underlying configuration; the parity-violating observation is what our orientation's strobe catches at the phase of the cycle from which we are sampling."

A fifth restatement.

**Recommended patch C8.1:** the section has one substantive claim and the spinning-coin/strobe analogy to deliver it. The claim should be stated ONCE clearly, demonstrated by the analogy, and forward-pointed for use. Suggested compression of the four-pass passage:

After the analogy setup (spinning coin, two cameras with opposite-phase strobes), state the structural claim **once**:

> The framework's reading is that the weak nuclear force is exactly such a measurement apparatus for the neutrino, and that the two orientations have weak forces at *opposite phases* of the same underlying rhythm. The +N orientation's apparatus samples the chirality superposition at the phase where the left-handed half is forward; the −N orientation's mirror apparatus samples at the opposite phase, where the right-handed half is forward. Both orientations see the same neutrino — same coin, opposite phases of the strobe. The asymmetry the Standard Model observes is the asymmetry between two phase-samples of one symmetric configuration.

Then proceed directly to the consequences (this deepens bilateral support; this explains parity violation; this clarifies the Majorana picture) without re-stating the structural claim in each.

Net saving: ~280-330 words. The spinning-coin → strobe analogy is preserved in full (it's earning its place); only the post-analogy restatements get trimmed.

## Issue C8.2 — §7 "What This Sets Up" recap of four consequences mildly redundant (density, minor)

§7's enumeration of "the first / the second / the third / the fourth" consequence is doing summary work. Each consequence is summarised in 2-3 sentences. The summaries restate the section content the reader has just finished.

This is acceptable as a chapter-close anchor for a substantial chapter — readers benefit from a recap of what's been established. PC doesn't recommend trimming the four-bullet recap.

But the closing tricolon — "The neutrino is unresolved. Its non-resolution gives it consequences. The third consequence is bilateral support. Bilateral support is the structure cross-thickness gravity acts on" — repeats what the four-bullet recap just said.

**Recommended patch C8.2 (minor):** drop the closing tricolon. The four-bullet recap is sufficient. The "next chapter computes what cross-thickness gravity produces" handoff is the right close.

Net saving: ~30 words.
