## §2 — Why antipodal correlation matters (the specific observational signature)

For an *isotropic* chiral background:
- ⟨EB⟩, ⟨TB⟩ integrate parity-odd component over all multipoles
- Antipodal points share the same chirality sign (statistical isotropy → no preferred direction)
- Adding antipodal signals coherently gives 2 × small at the suppressed Δχ ≲ 10⁻⁶

For a *dipolar* chiral background aligned with axis n̂:
- Chirality sign varies as cos(θ_n̂) — positive on one hemisphere, negative on the other
- Antipodal points have **opposite chirality sign**
- The directional cross-correlation ⟨B(n̂) · B(−n̂)⟩ projected onto the Wagenveld axis carries the dipolar amplitude
- This is a *new* observable — not captured by isotropic ⟨EB⟩, ⟨TB⟩ amplitudes

**The discrimination structure:**
- Standard physics: isotropic + parity-conserving → ⟨B(n̂) · B(−n̂)⟩ has no dipolar component
- NTE if dipolar chirality survives: ⟨B(n̂) · B(−n̂)⟩ has a dipolar component aligned with Wagenveld direction at amplitude TBD by derivation
- The signal is detectable in directional B-mode analysis tied to the Wagenveld axis — even at small amplitudes — because the *direction* is independently known

**Plausibility floor:** if the dipolar source is purely geometric, it could survive N_e e-folds at amplitude comparable to or *larger* than the isotropic 10⁻⁶ — because the suppression mechanism (amplitude dissipation) doesn't apply the same way.

**Plausibility ceiling:** the dipolar amplitude cannot exceed the *total* parity-violating budget set by the integrated parity-odd action. This must be derived, not assumed.

**Honest assessment:** any amplitude estimate here is illustrative only. The derivation needs to determine (i) whether the orientation-source even exists, (ii) what its initial amplitude is, (iii) how it propagates across e-folds, (iv) what fraction projects onto observable CMB modes.
