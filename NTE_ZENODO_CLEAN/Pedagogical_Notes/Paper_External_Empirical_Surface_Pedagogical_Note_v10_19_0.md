## The result in one paragraph

A theoretical framework's standing is determined by the experiments that could break it. This note catalogues the external empirical surface where the framework presented in the broader corpus could fail, sorted by how distinctively the framework's prediction differs from the standard treatment. Three zero-parameter structural predictions sit at the sharpest end of the surface: the dark-matter-to-baryon ratio κ at 5.50, the atmospheric neutrino mixing angle θ₂₃ at exactly 45°, and the leptonic CP phase δ_J at ±π/2. The primordial spectral index n_s and tensor-to-scalar ratio r are not on this list — they are *consistent-with but not derived-from* the framework's structure, and are reported separately at their honest registers below. Three further structural commitments — gravity-mediated entanglement of macroscopic masses, directional correlation between the local Hubble parameter and line-of-sight void density, and a dark-energy equation of state above −1 at low redshift — sit at the next tier, each with a measurable falsifier footprint. Beyond those, a register of about 23 compatibility checks across precision quantum-mechanical experiments records lanes where no hard contradiction has appeared at current precision but where future improvements would tighten the gate. The note is not advocacy — it lists what could break the framework, not what supports it.

## Why this matters in one paragraph

A common pattern in modern theoretical physics is to develop a framework that fits a wide range of observations through a large number of free parameters, then point at the successful fits as evidence for the framework's correctness. This pattern is unconvincing precisely because the framework's flexibility makes it hard to identify what would falsify it. The present framework's commitments push in the opposite direction: most of the central numbers (the dark-matter ratio, the atmospheric mixing angle, the CP phase) are structural predictions with no parameter to adjust. If the framework is right, these numbers will agree with observation; if the framework is wrong, the disagreement will be specific and quantitatively bounded. The note is the catalogue of where to look — sorted by sharpness of disagreement that would falsify, with explicit naming of the upcoming experiments (CMB-S4, LiteBIRD, DUNE, Hyper-Kamiokande, DESI, Euclid, LSST) that will tighten the gates over the next decade.

## Three tiers of test

*Tier 1 — zero-parameter structural predictions.* These are the framework's sharpest predictions: specific numerical values produced by structural arithmetic with no adjustable parameter. Falsification at this tier means the framework is wrong about a structural commitment. **Three predictions occupy this tier.**

*Conditional register — consistent-with, not derived-from.* These are values the framework is consistent with and cannot rule out, but has not structurally forced. The inflation-sector observables n_s and r occupy this register. They are not Tier 1; calling them zero-parameter structural predictions was the corpus's prior overclaim, and the v10.19.0 audit corrects it.

*Tier 2 — structural commitments with falsifier footprints.* These are commitments the framework makes about what kind of physical signature should be present, without committing to an exact numerical value. Three predictions occupy this tier.

*Tier 3 — compatibility register.* These are precision experiments that, at current measurement accuracy, show no hard contradiction with the framework. About 23 lanes are catalogued, spanning quantum-mechanical precision experiments from neutron interferometry to time crystals.

## Tier 1 — the three sharpest predictions

*The dark-matter-to-baryon ratio κ at 5.50.* The framework's bilateral cosmology produces this ratio from the bare Hopf-bundle coupling 0.846 resummed via cross-orientation self-consistency. The observed value, from Planck 2018 cosmological measurements, is 5.38 ± 0.15 — a 1.03× ratio to the framework's prediction. CMB-S4 in the 2030s is expected to halve the uncertainty, tightening this gate substantially.

*The atmospheric neutrino mixing angle θ₂₃ at exactly π/4 (45°).* The framework's Z₂ exchange symmetry on the chirality-superposed Majorana sector forces this value at leading order. The observed value, from the NuFIT global oscillation analysis, is about 49.7° — within current uncertainty of 45° plus a sub-leading correction correlated with the reactor angle. DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande will project the angle to about ±0.5° accuracy.

*The leptonic Jarlskog invariant δ_J at ±π/2.* The framework's CP-violation structure forces this maximal value. Current observation is consistent with maximal CP violation but with substantial uncertainty. DUNE's precision δ_CP measurement is the key test.

## The conditional register — n_s and r

Two inflation-sector values travel with the framework's broader corpus and have sometimes appeared alongside Tier 1 predictions. They are not Tier 1 and the v10.19.0 corpus registers them honestly.

*The primordial spectral index n_s ≈ 0.9685.* The framework's slow-roll reading of the pre-temporal phase produces n_s = 1 − 2/N_e — consistent with the Planck 2018 observation 0.9649 ± 0.0042 (0.85σ). But the framework does not claim n_s as a derived structural prediction. The slow-roll reading imports a formula whose applicability in the Hartle–Hawking Euclidean regime is unestablished; the kinetic-term structure K(Φ) is open; and the no-boundary condition under-determines the tilt. The honest register is *consistent-with but not derived-from*.

*The tensor-to-scalar ratio r ≈ 4×10⁻³.* The framework's hilltop reading of the inflation sector gives r at this value, but only under the kinetic-term default K(Φ) = 1 — a candidate form whose derivation as forced rather than merely natural is open structural work. The a₄-based route that would have established K(Φ) = 1 is unavailable on the canonical S³×S¹ substrate (a₄ = 0 on the canonical operator). So r ≈ 4×10⁻³ is a *conditional* prediction on the K(Φ) = 1 default. The current bound from Planck and BICEP/Keck is r < 0.036 at 95% confidence — consistent with the framework's conditional prediction.

## The conditional gate — CLFV

There is a sixth gate of a different kind, ratified in 2026, that deserves naming here: *charged-lepton flavour violation* (CLFV). The framework's structural commitment is that two specific symmetry directions in the right-handed neutrino mass matrix coincide — the σ₁ and σ₂ Cartan modes are degenerate. That degeneracy freezes a usually-free internal mixing, forcing a definite numerical relation between two CLFV channels:

The ratio of the muon-to-electron amplitude (squared) to the tau-to-electron amplitude (squared) equals tan²θ₂₃ — the square of the tangent of the atmospheric neutrino mixing angle. For θ₂₃ at the framework's prediction of 45°, the ratio comes out to 1 exactly.

Six qualifiers travel with this prediction: it is *conditional* (the prediction is only useful if CLFV is observed at all; the framework's absolute rate prediction sits far below current experimental bounds); it is at the *amplitude level*, not the full branching ratio; the framework's odds versus anarchic seesaw are about 9 to 1; the width on the ratio prediction is about ±2–3%; the mechanism that produces the prediction is a specific framework-internal commitment, not a generic seesaw feature; and this is the framework's first ratified test of laboratory physics rather than cosmology.

## Tier 2 — three structural commitments

*Gravity-mediated entanglement of macroscopic masses (BMV experiments).* The framework's broader commitments require gravity to mediate cross-thickness coupling — which means at the macroscopic-mass scale, gravity-mediated entanglement must occur. A negative BMV result at the design sensitivity of next-generation experiments would falsify the framework's gravity coupling structure.

*Directional correlation between local Hubble parameter and line-of-sight void density.* The framework's void-enhanced cosmology predicts that observations along sight lines passing through significant cosmic voids will show systematically different local Hubble parameter values than observations along sight lines passing through clusters. The signature is testable with future galaxy surveys (DESI, Euclid, Vera Rubin / LSST).

*Dark-energy equation of state above −1 at low redshift.* The framework's structural prediction is that the observed w should be slightly above −1 at low redshift (z below about 0.5), with a specific time-dependent signature distinguishable from a pure cosmological constant. DESI, Euclid, and LSST are designed to measure w to about 1% precision across the relevant redshift range.

## Tier 3 — the compatibility register

The third tier records about 23 precision experiments where the framework has been checked for hard contradiction at current measurement sensitivity. These include: neutron interferometry experiments testing the 4π rotational structure of spin-1/2 particles; Aharonov-Bohm and Berry-phase precision tests; Bell-test and Hardy-paradox experiments; time-crystal experiments; Diósi-Penrose and CSL bounds on gravitational decoherence; and equivalence-principle tests.

## What's honest about the catalogue

The crucial change in this v10.19.0 note compared to the 05-30 predecessor: n_s and r are moved from Tier 1 to the conditional register. Calling them zero-parameter structural predictions was inaccurate — the framework is *consistent with* their observed values but does not *derive* them from structural arithmetic. Making this distinction explicit is the corpus's no-numerology discipline applied to its own inflation sector.

## What would break it

If any Tier 1 prediction shifts substantially outside the framework's value as measurements improve, the framework's structural derivation of that prediction is wrong. If the inflation-sector readings (n_s, r) shift so far from the conditional predictions that even the K(Φ) = 1 default can't accommodate them, the conditional register breaks. If any Tier 2 commitment fails its expected signature, the corresponding structural commitment fails. If any Tier 3 compatibility check surfaces a hard contradiction at improved sensitivity, the framework's broader structural commitments would have to be revisited.
