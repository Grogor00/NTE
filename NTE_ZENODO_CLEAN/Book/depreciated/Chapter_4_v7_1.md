# Chapter 4 — Time as Expansion

## *Everything That Happens, Stays Happened*

> *EVERYTHING THAT HAPPENS, STAYS HAPPENED.*
> — Terry Pratchett, *Thief of Time*

---

The previous chapter established that we live on a closed three-dimensional space — an S³, expanding, with no interior to access and no exterior to escape into. The space itself is what is expanding, and the parameter that counts the expansion is *N*, the number of e-folds. That picture is now in place. What remains is the second of Chapter 3's two framework commitments. Surface-only — no ambient embedding — was the first, and did its work in the previous chapter. Time emerging from expansion is the second, and this chapter develops it directly: showing how time arises from the expansion, and what that emergence implies about the nature of past, present, and future.

This is the chapter where the book's title earns its keep. *The Shape of Time* is not a metaphor. Time has a shape — a specific geometric relationship to the expanding manifold — and that shape is what we are about to draw.

## What Time Is Not

Before describing what time is in the framework, it is worth being clear about what time is not. The standard textbook picture treats time as a background dimension. Space has three of them; time has one. They form a four-dimensional spacetime, and the universe sits in this spacetime, with events occurring at definite spatial-and-temporal coordinates. Time, in this picture, is a pre-existing parameter that ticks regardless of what is happening. The universe could be empty, full, expanding, contracting, or stationary — time still passes.

This picture works for almost every calculation a physicist or engineer ever does. It is built into general relativity, into quantum mechanics, into thermodynamics. It is so deeply embedded in how physics is taught that suggesting time might not be fundamental can sound, at first, like wordplay rather than physics.

But the picture has one specific failure mode, which Chapter 1 already identified. The photon does not fit it. From a photon's own frame, time does not pass between emission and absorption. The photon's proper time is identically zero. If time is a background dimension we move through, the photon should be moving through it like everything else — and it isn't. The textbook picture handles this by saying that the photon's worldline is a *null geodesic*, on which the spacetime interval vanishes, and treating this as a curiosity rather than a clue.

The framework treats it as a clue. If time were truly a background dimension, the photon could not behave the way it does. The photon's null-time is not a curiosity but a signal that time is something else — something that the photon participates in differently from the way the rest of us do.

## What Time Is

In the framework, time is what emerges from the universe's expansion when the expansion is registered by a particular kind of physical structure — the geometric scalar field Chapter 1 introduced as the *boundary expansion field*, written |Φ_NTE| and distinct from the Standard Model Higgs the reader knows from CERN.

A brief reminder of the distinction matters here, because it is what this chapter will be working with. The Standard Model Higgs is the field whose particle was discovered in 2012, the field that gives mass to electrons and quarks, the field that sits at its constant value of 246 GeV everywhere in the universe today. The framework leaves all of that unchanged. The Higgs does what the Standard Model says it does. What the framework adds is one further scalar — a geometric one, sourcing the expansion of the boundary itself — that does *not* give mass to particles and *can* vary across cosmic history. This is the field the framework calls |Φ_NTE|, and its dynamics are what this chapter is about.

The expansion itself is geometric. The S³ is growing, and we count the growth in e-folds *N*. *N* is a pure number. It does not require a clock. You can count expansions without time existing, the way you can count the rings of a tree trunk without time existing for the tree. *N* is the framework's primary parameter, and everything else — including time — is derived from it.

The connection between *N* and *t* runs through the boundary expansion field |Φ_NTE|. This field is, in the framework's picture, the geometric quantity whose strength sets the rate at which the universe is expanding. The relationship is one equation, and it is the central equation of the entire framework:

H(N) = α|Φ_NTE(N)|²

The left side is the Hubble parameter — the rate of expansion at e-fold *N*. The right side is the boundary expansion field's strength squared, multiplied by a constant α whose value sets the overall scale. The equation says: the rate of expansion is set by the strength of |Φ_NTE|. Where the field is strong, the universe expands rapidly. Where it is weak, expansion is slow.

This is not an obscure equation. It will appear in every remaining chapter of this book in some form, because almost everything the framework explains follows from it. But the meaning that matters most is its inversion. Rearranging:

dt/dN = 1/(α|Φ_NTE|²)

This says: for each e-fold of expansion, an interval of proper time accumulates. The interval depends on the strength of the boundary expansion field. The stronger the field, the smaller the time interval per e-fold. The weaker the field, the larger the interval. Time is not a thing that ticks along independently of expansion. Time is what accumulates with expansion at the rate that |Φ_NTE| sets.

You can, if you like, integrate this equation along the universe's history to recover the familiar time coordinate:

t(N) = ∫₀ᴺ dN' / (α|Φ_NTE(N')|²)

This is the formal statement of how *t* emerges from *N*. Time, in the framework, is the integrated inverse of the Hubble rate along the e-fold history. The "rate at which time passes" is just another way of describing how rapidly the universe is currently expanding.

## Two Coordinates, One Process

A reader trained in physics might object that *N* and *t* are just two different coordinates for the same dynamics. Cosmologists routinely use *N* as a convenient parameter; physicists in other contexts routinely use *t*. The choice is conventional. So why call one of them primary and the other derived?

The answer is geometric, and it is the reason the framework uses *N* and not *t* throughout.

When you write the universe's history in *t*, you are committing yourself to a coordinate that already presupposes what the framework is trying to derive. *t* is what we observe; it is what our clocks measure; it is what we experience as the passage of time. But it is also a *consequence* of the more basic process — the expansion — that the framework treats as primary. Writing physics in *t* and asking how time relates to expansion is putting the cart before the horse. You can do it, and the equations work out, but you've buried the explanation under coordinate choices.

When you write the universe's history in *N*, you are using a coordinate that is geometrically meaningful regardless of whether time exists. *N* is a count. It does not require duration. It does not require a metric signature. It does not require any of the apparatus that *t* presupposes. You can use it pre-temporally, when the framework explicitly says no temporal structure has yet emerged, and the equations still make sense. You cannot do this with *t*. *t* requires the very thing it would be describing.

So the framework's commitment is not arbitrary. *N* is the coordinate in which the framework's claims can be stated cleanly without circularity. *t* is the coordinate in which most of physics has been done. The relationship between them — *t* emerging from *N* via the boundary expansion field |Φ_NTE| — is the framework's specific structural claim about how time arises.

Two coordinates, one process — the framework's signature move applied to time itself. *N* and *t* are not two parameters but two readings of one expansion, registered from outside as e-folds counted and from inside as duration accumulated. Chapter 1 made the move for the photon; Chapter 3 made it for the universe's surface; here it lands on time itself.

## The Pre-Temporal Epoch

This brings us to a strange piece of the picture, which Chapter 3 deliberately deferred. The framework distinguishes two regimes.

The *post-temporal* regime is everything we usually mean when we talk about the universe. It is the era from very shortly after the Big Bang to the present. In this regime, *t* has emerged, the relationship dt/dN = 1/(α|Φ_NTE|²) is well-defined and stable, and ordinary time-based physics works. General relativity, quantum mechanics, thermodynamics, nuclear physics — all of them apply normally. Using *t* in this regime is correct, not an approximation. The deeper structure (*N*) is always there, but the emergent description (*t*) is complete and self-consistent within its domain of validity. The relationship is exactly the same as the relationship between Newtonian gravity and general relativity: both are correct, one is the deeper picture, and using the surface picture in its proper domain is not wrong.

The *pre-temporal* regime is everything before time emerged. In the framework's picture, this is not "the early universe before the Big Bang" in any conventional sense. It is, geometrically, a region of e-fold parameter *N* (specifically, *N* less than a value the framework calls N_lock, around 0.1) at which the boundary expansion field has not yet stabilised into a form that produces the well-behaved dt/dN relationship. In this regime, the equation dt/dN is undefined, which means *t* itself cannot be cleanly defined. Only *N* is meaningful.

This is hard to picture, because our intuitions are built around time. Saying "before time" is itself a temporal phrase, and it presupposes the very thing being denied. The framework is explicit about this: the pre-temporal epoch is defined by a range of *N*, not by a temporal interval. There is no "moment" at which the pre-temporal epoch existed. There is a span of e-folds, geometrically prior to the gauge birth at N_lock, during which time as we know it had not yet condensed out of the underlying expansion.

What kind of physics happens in such an epoch? The honest answer is that the question contains a category error. *Happens* is a temporal verb. The pre-temporal regime is not a region in which things happen; it is a region of geometric structure. The framework's technical corpus describes this regime using the language of *Euclidean geometry* — geometry without a distinguished time direction — as opposed to the *Lorentzian geometry* of ordinary spacetime, which has time as a special direction distinct from the spatial directions. The two are related by a mathematical operation called a Wick rotation. The pre-temporal regime, in the framework's reading, is the Euclidean section of the manifold; the gauge birth at N_lock is the Wick rotation that produces the Lorentzian regime we live in.

What this means for the reader: pre-temporal structure is not a sequence of events arranged along a time axis. It is a set of geometric features arranged along *N* — the closed S³ at varying scales, the boundary expansion field |Φ_NTE| at varying strengths, certain symmetry constraints — and the right way to think about it is the way one thinks about a static three-dimensional object, holding all of *N* in mind at once rather than tracing a path through it. Reading these structures as if they were temporal events — as if the universe was *doing* one thing at one *N* and *then* another thing at a later *N* — is the kind of conceptual mistake the chapter's discipline asks the reader to avoid. There is no "doing" pre-temporally. There is structure.

A note on what is *not* present pre-temporally. The Standard Model Higgs — the field that gives mass to electrons and quarks — does not exist as a quantum field in this epoch, because the gauge structure it lives in hasn't yet switched on. The boundary expansion field |Φ_NTE| is present, but it has no identity as the SM Higgs at this stage; it is the geometric scalar whose square sources H(N) through the core equation. Both fields' identities crystallise at the gauge birth as separate identities — the SM Higgs as the mass-giving Standard Model field, |Φ_NTE| as the geometric source of H — and the framework is careful to keep this distinction clean even when the fields' values eventually coincide, much later, at our cosmic moment. The framework's structural rule that keeps the two fields' identities separate, even where their values eventually coincide, is one instance of the methodological frame Chapter 1 introduced: where two distinct structural routes produce the same form, the framework treats this as evidence the routes are looking at the same underlying structure — fingers of the same hand — rather than collapsing one route into the other.

The reason any of this matters is that the gauge birth — the structural junction at N = N_lock where the Euclidean pre-temporal geometry joins the Lorentzian post-temporal regime, where the boundary expansion field's configuration is such that dt/dN is well-defined for all N > N_lock, and where the Standard Model gauge symmetries are present (with the SM Higgs's identity as the field that gives mass to electrons and quarks among them) — is a structural feature whose content the framework places at a specific *N*. The structural content at that *N* is the basis of everything in the post-temporal regime we inhabit. If we want to understand why our universe has the structure it does, we need to understand what the gauge birth is — and that requires being able to say things about the pre-temporal regime even though time, in the ordinary sense, is not present there. The gauge-birth chapter is where this work is done.

## Time Dilation as Boundary Field Thinning

Here is where the framework's picture starts to do work that the standard picture cannot.

In general relativity, gravitational time dilation is a real effect. Clocks run more slowly in stronger gravitational fields. Near the surface of a neutron star, time passes more slowly than far from the star. At the event horizon of a black hole, time effectively stops, from the perspective of a distant observer. Standard relativity treats this as a property of the metric — the spacetime geometry distorts in the presence of mass, and clocks measure that distortion.

In the framework, gravitational time dilation has a specific structural reading: the boundary expansion field |Φ_NTE| is *thinner* in regions of higher gravitational potential. The relationship dt/dN = 1/(α|Φ_NTE|²) means that where |Φ_NTE| is reduced, the rate of proper time accumulation per e-fold increases. Or, putting it the other way around: where mass-energy concentrates curvature locally — which, under the framework's structural reading, is the same fact as the local thinning of the boundary expansion field — the local rate of proper time accumulation per e-fold changes, and clocks register the change as time dilation. Under the framework's structural commitment, |Φ_NTE| is not a separate field that responds to curvature through some additional mechanism — it *is* curvature, viewed through a different parameterization, the same surface structure named twice. The framework's signature move once again, applied here to the curvature-and-field pairing. What the equation pins down is the relationship between this local structure and the local rate of proper time accumulation.

This is more than just a redescription. The framework's reading is consistent with the gravitational time dilation general relativity predicts and that has been confirmed to high precision in every experiment from atomic clocks at different altitudes to the operation of GPS satellites. The boundary expansion field's local configuration in a Newtonian gravitational potential is consistent with the post-Newtonian factor in the regime where it has been tested. The reading is structural: time dilation is what happens to the dt/dN conversion rate when curvature is locally concentrated, which is the same fact as |Φ_NTE| being locally thinner.

A quiet structural payoff worth pausing on: the boundary expansion field's variation in the presence of mass-energy is exactly the kind of behaviour the SM Higgs does *not* do. The SM Higgs sits at 246 GeV everywhere in the universe; it does not vary spatially in the presence of stars and galaxies, because the Standard Model's potential V_SM holds it locked at its vacuum value. |Φ_NTE| is the field that varies — across cosmic history and across regions of gravitational potential — and that variation is what the framework reads as the source of gravitational time dilation. Two fields, two distinct behaviours. The textbook account of the SM Higgs is unchanged; the framework's account of |Φ_NTE| is what supplies the structural reading that the standard picture leaves abstract.

Two pieces of honesty are in order here. The first is that the framework's account is consistent with the observed time dilation; it does not yet *derive* it from first principles. Reproducing the standard prediction at first post-Newtonian order is not the same thing as having a complete derivation of how the boundary expansion field's local configuration produces the dilation effect. Under Path B's two-fields disclosure, the Standard Model Higgs (which gives mass to electrons and quarks and would otherwise be the natural candidate for "the field that responds to mass-energy") is *not* the field involved; the boundary expansion field |Φ_NTE| is, and |Φ_NTE| does not give mass to particles. The framework now commits to the structural reading that |Φ_NTE| is not a separate field responding to curvature through some additional mechanism — it *is* curvature, viewed through a different parameterization, the same surface structure named twice. The full structural derivation of this identification is technical-corpus work the framework continues to develop. The chapter's claim should not be stronger than what the framework can defend: the framework is consistent with observed gravitational time dilation in the regimes that have been tested, the structural reading is now committed, and the underlying derivation of that reading's full content is open work.

The second piece of honesty is that even acknowledging this, the chapter's structural claim does real work. In standard relativity, gravitational time dilation is a property of the metric, with no further structural reading specified. In the framework, it is a consequence — at the structural level the framework now commits to — of how the boundary expansion field distributes itself across the manifold, with mass-energy's local concentration of curvature being the same fact as the local thinning of |Φ_NTE|, and how that distribution affects the local rate at which proper time accumulates per e-fold of expansion. The geometry is still there, but the framework supplies an underlying structural reading that the standard treatment leaves abstract. The structural reading is now committed; whether the full derivation can be reached is open work; that the picture is consistent with what has been measured is established.

A note on where this leaves the framework's structural commitments more broadly. The structural-identity reading is committed here, though the full derivation remains open work. The framework's first sharp distinguishing empirical commitment — the dark-matter ratio κ that a later chapter develops — is by contrast a complete structural calculation, with no parameter freedom: a specific number that the framework either matches against observation or doesn't. The framework picks its empirical battles deliberately. Time dilation is a reading the framework now commits to; κ is the number the framework is willing to stake its case on.

## The Pratchett Line

The chapter epigraph is from *Thief of Time*, one of Terry Pratchett's late Discworld novels. The line is delivered by Death, and like much of Pratchett's best work it does serious philosophical work in deliberately small clothing.

*Everything that happens, stays happened.*

What does this say, in the framework's terms? It says that the past is not somewhere else. The events of the universe's history did not occur on a timeline that has now moved on. They occurred at e-folds that the universe has now expanded beyond, but the e-folds themselves are not erased by the expansion. The structure of what happened — every photon emitted, every entanglement formed, every particle locking, every gauge transition — is part of the geometric record of the manifold. The universe has not forgotten any of it.

The framework's relationship to the philosophical question of whether time "really flows" is worth stating cleanly. In the *block universe* picture that some interpretations of relativity take seriously, all of spacetime exists at once — past, present, and future are parts of a single four-dimensional structure, and the sense of "now moving forward" is an artefact of conscious experience rather than a feature of physics. The framework is naturally close to this picture for what concerns the *past*: every event that has occurred is geometrically present in the manifold, retained in the structure of how the expansion has unfolded. The past is not somewhere else. It is the geometric record of expansion-already-completed.

But the framework breaks the block-universe symmetry between past and future. The future, in the framework, is not a place we are travelling toward through pre-existing spacetime. It is what the manifold has not yet expanded into. *N* is unidirectional — the universe expands, and the geometry accumulates as it does — and what has not yet been expanded into has not yet been determined. The past is geometrically present; the future is being generated. The asymmetry between them is structural, built into the fact that *N* increases.

This is the framework's position, and it is what the Pratchett line catches. *Everything that happens, stays happened.* The line does not say *everything that will happen has already happened* — which is the strong block-universe claim, and is not the framework's position. It says that what has occurred is geometrically present in the structure, while what has not occurred has not yet been generated by the expansion. The book will return to this distinction in later chapters when we discuss entropy, the arrow of time, and the universe's far-future geometry.

For now, the picture to hold is this: the boundary expansion field does not just regulate the rate of proper time accumulation. It does so in a way that creates a directional structure — *N* increases, and time accumulates with it — and the structure that has accumulated does not unwind. The universe is becoming, as Pratchett's line suggests, in a way that does not allow what has happened to un-happen.

## Where This Leaves the Photon (Again)

Both Chapter 1 and Chapter 3 returned to the photon at their close, because the photon is the framework's first and clearest test case. Here, with the field-mediated time-emergence picture in place, we can give the cleanest version yet.

The photon, Chapter 1 established, is not a thing in flight. It is the geometric connection between emission and absorption — one object with two readings: from outside, length in space and time; along the connection itself, both extents vanish together. The field-mediated picture gives this its mechanism. The photon has zero coupling to the boundary expansion field; looking at the equation dt/dN = 1/(α|Φ_NTE|²), this means the photon does not engage with the mechanism by which proper time accumulates per e-fold at all. For a structure that doesn't engage with |Φ_NTE|, the rate of proper-time accumulation per e-fold is zero — and the null-time the photon-line registers, along the connection itself, is exactly that zero.

The photon is also the species at zero coupling to the Standard Model Higgs — which is why it is massless. These two zeroes are not coincidence. The framework's account of why they go together is structural: both are downstream of the same event at the universe's gauge birth, when each pre-temporal mode acquired its identity by being assigned a coupling pattern. The photon's pattern is *zero* across the board — no engagement with the Higgs, no mass; no engagement with the boundary expansion field, no time accumulation. Other species acquired non-zero patterns at gauge birth. Massive particles acquired their mass-giving connections to the Higgs — that's how they got their masses — and they also acquired engagement with the e-fold structure, which is how they got their proper-time accumulation. These two acquisitions are correlated because they emerged from the same cascade event, but they are not the same coupling. The gauge-birth chapter develops the cascade mechanism in detail.

For us, made of atoms with their full complement of these acquired engagements, time passes at the rate it does because of how our constituent particles engage with the local strength of the boundary expansion field as the universe unfolds. The years we count, the seconds our clocks tick, the moments we experience — all of these are local realisations of dt/dN = 1/(α|Φ_NTE|²), with the integration done across the matter we are made of and the e-folds we have lived through.

This is not a metaphor. It is the framework's specific claim. Time, for any physical system, is the integrated outcome of how that system's matter content engages with the universe's expansion. A different engagement pattern would give a different time. A photon, with no engagement at all, has no time.

## What Comes Next

We have now seen three pillars of the framework. The first, from Chapter 1, was that the photon's null-time is not a curiosity but the seed observation from which the whole framework grows. The second, from Chapter 3, was that the universe is a closed three-dimensional space without interior or exterior, expanding through e-folds *N*. The third, from this chapter, is that time emerges from this expansion via the boundary expansion field, with H = α|Φ_NTE|² as the central equation and dt = dN/(α|Φ_NTE|²) as its inversion.

What remains to be developed is the nature of the boundary expansion field itself — what it is, how it sets the rate, and why it deserves the central role the framework gives it. The reader has already met the textbook treatment of the Standard Model Higgs as a field that gives mass to other particles. The framework's central scalar is *not* that field; it is |Φ_NTE|, the geometric scalar whose strength regulates the rate at which proper time accumulates per unit expansion. The next chapter takes this seriously and works out what it means — including the relationship between the boundary expansion field and the SM Higgs the reader already knows.

There is also a question that this chapter has deferred and that the next one will address directly. The framework has been speaking as if the relationship dt/dN = 1/(α|Φ_NTE|²) is well-defined throughout cosmic history. But in the very early universe, during the pre-temporal epoch, this relationship is exactly what the framework says was *not* well-defined. How does the universe transition from the pre-temporal regime to the post-temporal regime? What event marks that transition? And what does it mean for time to "switch on" if there is no time in which the switching could occur?

These questions take us into the gauge birth, which the framework places at the heart of how everything we observe came to be. That is a later chapter's territory. For now, the picture from this chapter is enough to carry forward.

The shape of time, in the framework, is the shape of expansion. And the universe has been expanding, and unfolding, and accumulating proper time at every e-fold, since whatever counts as the beginning. Everything that has happened along the way stays happened. The geometry remembers.
