## 6. Pedagogical strategies and risk points

### Where lay register is most fragile

**§4 (the bare coupling).** The prose draft compresses the chain to "angular uniformity + three modes + sphere-area normalisation gives 3/√(4π)." Lay reader walks away with: "the framework calculates a specific number from pure geometry, and the number is 0.846." Technical reader gets a footnote pointing to corpus §9.3 and the κ derivation papers.

**§5 (the closure).** The geometric series resummation needs careful pedagogical handling. The strategy: §3's reframing (geometric coupling acting on a structure with no gauge quantum numbers, total-stress-energy coupling structurally forced) makes the §5 closure feel inevitable rather than postulated. Then the geometric series κ₀ + κ₀² + κ₀³ + ... → closed form does the algebra the structure requires.

### Risk: overclaiming

The chapter is making a real prediction with a real match. The temptation is to oversell the match as confirmation. The §7 honesty pass and §8 load-bearing analysis are the chapter's discipline against this. **The chapter must not say or imply that the κ match "proves" the framework.** The match is consistent with the framework being correct; the framework's overall standing depends on its full prediction record (Ch 11).

### Risk: misframing the residual

v2 closes the v1 risk at this point: the finite-y_ν candidate is dropped (corpus has closed it as exactly zero). The residual stays open without a positive candidate — that's the corpus's actual position, and the prose-draft session must not invent a positive candidate to fill the rhetorical gap. *"The framework does not currently identify a positive candidate; CMB-S4 will resolve."*

### Risk: misframing the two-step derivation

The corpus's v10.5.8 reframing is in force: one structural derivation followed by one algebraic closure, *not* two independent confirmations. v2 §6 carries this discipline at lay register without invoking the historical record (technical readers know).

### Risk: lay reader losing the through-line

The chapter is the longest in the book at ~5,500 words and covers a lot of structural ground. The §6 recap (Where the Number Comes From) is the strategic anchor that pulls the chain together before the §7 punchline lands. If a reader gets lost in §4 or §5, §6 is where they re-find the argument.

### Visualisations (per Greg's Q5 — two art-grade visuals)

Both visuals are art-grade pieces (poster-size wall-art, t-shirt, coffee cup quality) — not workmanlike textbook diagrams. Style direction is wide open: cubist, surrealist, abstract expressionist, op-art, or something else that catches the structural fact in a way text can't. Specific execution is downstream session work. The outline names the placeholders so the prose-draft session knows where they sit in the chapter and writes around them rather than working around their absence.

**Visual A — The Bilateral Bridge (supports §§2–3):** Two CPN-conjugate orientations of one manifold's N-thickness, sharing the Janus boundary at N=0. The bilaterally-supported structure (the unresolved neutrino) visible as something that lives across both orientations. Curvature registering across the thickness as visual element. The conceptual content the visual must capture: *one geometric structure with two faces, connected by a topological coupling that becomes manifest where there's a bilateral structure to register against*.

**Visual B — The Geometric Series (supports §5):** The κ₀ → κ₀² → κ₀³ → κ resummation as a fixed-point geometry. Distinct from a chain diagram. The conceptual content: *infinite recursion converging to a finite answer — a self-referential feedback that doesn't run away because κ₀ < 1, instead landing on a fixed point*. Surrealist / cubist / abstract expressionist all natural fits; an Escher-adjacent or Op-art treatment could land powerfully here. Style choice is for the visual session.

The chain diagram (v1's "Pathway B → κ₀ → Pathway A → κ" technical-derivation-chain visual) is dropped: it duplicates §6's prose recap without adding pedagogical value, and CR/AC concur.
