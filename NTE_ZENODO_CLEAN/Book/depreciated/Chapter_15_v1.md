## §1 The Question

Take the chapter title literally for a moment. What is the worst that could happen to this framework?

Quite a lot, actually. The framework has, over fourteen chapters, made specific claims about the universe — claims that go beyond what the Standard Model says, claims that other frameworks do not make, claims that have specific numerical content and specific empirical consequences. Each one of those claims is a way for the framework to be wrong. And each one is, by the same fact, a way for the framework to be tested. This chapter is the catalogue.

The phrase at the head of this chapter is the one you say right before something bad happens. It is famous last words. The book has put it where it belongs: at the chapter that consolidates the framework's specific exposure to test. Everything the framework has staked is in this chapter. The next decade of cosmological and particle-physics measurements will determine which of these stakes are paid out and which are lost.

There are three categories to walk through, and the chapter is going to walk them in order.

The first is what already matches. The framework has six quantitative predictions whose values it derives from its structural commitments and that current observation has already found agreement with, within statistical uncertainty. None of these matches settles the framework one way or another — single matches are not verdicts — but each one is the kind of agreement that, if the framework were wrong, would have to be there by coincidence. The chapter will state each prediction, recap where it came from, name the current observation, and — most importantly — name what would falsify it. Even the matches are vulnerable to future sharpening.

The second is what is still open. The framework has another half-dozen predictions whose tests are scheduled but not yet run. Some of these are sharp empirical bets that will resolve in the next decade as the next generation of experiments comes online. Others are softer — they have current observational status that is consistent with the framework's reading but does not yet sharpen it. Each one is a specific way the framework could be shown wrong, and the chapter will name the conditions under which it would be.

The third is what the framework does not yet predict. The chapter is going to be honest about what the framework inherits from observation rather than derives, where the framework's structural argument has identified a pathway but not yet completed it, and which open questions in fundamental physics the framework has not resolved despite touching on them. The list is not short, and it is not hidden. A framework that pretends to answer questions it has not answered is a framework that will eventually be caught. The cleaner discipline is to state the open work plainly.

The previous chapter placed the framework's structural commitments in mathematical territory that has been mapped by other hands — the Hopf fibration of 1931, the Hurwitz classification of 1898, Connes and Rovelli's thermal time hypothesis, Penrose's twistors, the Marcolli–Penrose Palatial substrate, Pati and Salam's algebra, the bilateral cosmologies developed by Boyle, Turok, Barbour, Koslowski, and Mercati. The framework's distinctive contribution, established there, is the choice of physical commitments rather than the invention of new mathematics. This chapter is what that choice exposes to test. The mathematics that follows from the commitments produces specific numerical predictions. The numerical predictions are what the universe will settle.

What's the worst that could happen? The framework's predictions could break. Several of them, across independent regimes, with the kind of high-confidence margin that does not leave room for further structural correction. If that happens, the framework's working bet is lost, and the structural-reading method that the framework embodies continues with other attempts. This chapter is what would constitute the loss. The next chapter is what the bet is, in posture.

The chapter is also what would constitute success, if the predictions hold. The two cases are the same catalogue read from opposite ends.

## §2 What Already Matches

Six predictions, currently in statistical agreement with observation.

**The dark matter to ordinary matter ratio.** The framework derives κ = 5.50 from one structural chain — the Hopf monopole projection produces the bare coupling κ₀ = 3/√(4π) ≈ 0.846 from the angular geometry of the unresolved neutrino's quantum-mechanical density on the shared three-sphere boundary; the CPN-symmetric resummation closes κ₀ → κ = κ₀/(1 − κ₀) ≈ 5.50 through the self-referential feedback that the bilateral structure forces. The Planck satellite measures 5.38 ± 0.15. The framework's prediction sits within 0.83 standard deviations of the observed value. This is the framework's sharpest current empirical match, derived in Chapter 9. CMB-S4, the next-generation cosmic microwave background experiment scheduled to begin returning data toward the end of this decade, will improve measurement precision on this ratio by roughly a factor of two. The framework's prediction faces its sharpest test there. If CMB-S4 measures κ at 4.5, or at 6.5, or anywhere significantly away from 5.50 with high confidence, the framework's geometric derivation has missed something fundamental, and Chapter 9's structural argument collapses. The current match is real and informative; the next round of measurement is when the test sharpens.

**The primordial spectral index.** The cosmic microwave background carries an imprint of the universe's earliest density fluctuations, and the spectral index *n*_s — the slight departure from perfect scale-invariance in those fluctuations — measures how the fluctuations evolved during the period that produced them. The framework derives *n*_s = 0.9685 from the same single inherited constant α that sets the present cosmic expansion rate. The Planck measurement is 0.9649 ± 0.0042. The framework's prediction sits within 0.85 standard deviations of observation. There are no free parameters in the derivation: the slow-roll formula relating *n*_s to α, run on the framework's specific dynamics, produces this number. CMB-S4 and LiteBIRD will sharpen the measurement substantially. If either finds *n*_s significantly different from 0.9685 with high confidence, the framework's account of how the early universe produced its fluctuations has missed something — or, alternatively, the slow-roll formula's applicability to the framework's pre-temporal epoch has not been correctly handled, which is a piece of open work the chapter will return to.

**The atmospheric neutrino mixing angle.** Neutrinos oscillate between three flavours as they propagate, and the mixing angles that govern those oscillations are measured by neutrino-detection experiments. The atmospheric mixing angle θ₂₃ — the one most relevant to neutrinos produced in cosmic-ray interactions in the upper atmosphere — is observed at 45.0° ± 1.0°. The framework derives θ₂₃ = 45° exactly, from a structural symmetry of the algebra organising the unresolved neutrino's three mass eigenmodes. This is not a fit; it is a theorem about the symmetry of S³, established in Chapter 8. The match is essentially exact. Future neutrino experiments — DUNE at Fermilab, JUNO in China, Hyper-Kamiokande in Japan — will sharpen the measurement of θ₂₃ over the next decade. If the sharper measurement finds θ₂₃ significantly off 45° (say, 43° or 47° with high confidence), the framework's symmetry argument has missed something, and Chapter 8's account of the unresolved neutrino's mass structure needs revision.

**Dark energy as a strict cosmological constant.** The simplest cosmological model treats dark energy as a perfect cosmological constant, with an equation of state parameter *w* = −1 exactly. Recent results from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) have hinted that *w* may be slightly above −1 at low redshift, which would imply dark energy is evolving rather than constant. The framework's reading is that *w* is exactly −1 — the true dark-energy density is the geometric floor of the boundary expansion rate, set by the asymptotic value of |Φ_NTE| in cosmic voids — and that the apparent departure DESI sees is a measurement bias caused by the spatial variation of |Φ_NTE| between voids and matter-rich regions. The framework's prediction is therefore both a value (*w* = −1) and a structural explanation for why the measurement deviates from the value at low redshift. The chapter on the Hubble tension developed this in detail; the empirical resolution is coming as DESI's surveys integrate over more cosmic structure. If DESI eventually confirms genuine dynamical dark energy — *w* evolving with redshift in a way that is not consistent with void-enhancement bias — the framework's reading is wrong, and dark energy needs an account other than the geometric floor of the boundary expansion rate.

**The cosmic-mean Hubble parameter.** The Hubble tension — the discrepancy between *H*_0 measured from the cosmic microwave background (about 67 km/s/Mpc) and *H*_0 measured from local supernovae (about 73 km/s/Mpc) — has resisted explanation for nearly a decade. The framework's reading is that this is not a tension between two independent measurements of one quantity; it is two different measurements of two different quantities. The CMB-derived value is the cosmic mean *H*_0, averaged over the full expansion history. The local supernova value is the void-biased *H*_0, elevated by the spatial variation of |Φ_NTE|² in the relatively low-density regions through which the supernova measurements are made. The framework's prediction for the cosmic mean is *H*_0 = 67.6 km/s/Mpc. The Planck CMB-derived value is 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc. The framework's prediction sits within 0.4 standard deviations of observation. The Hubble tension, on the framework's reading, is not a problem to be resolved by new physics; it is a structural prediction that *H*_0 measured locally and *H*_0 measured cosmically should differ in this specific way, and the discrepancy is real but expected. The test of this prediction is whether the discrepancy resolves toward the framework's specific value when the measurement is properly weighted for void fraction along lines of sight. If void-corrected supernova measurements continue to give 73 km/s/Mpc instead of 67.6, the framework's reading is wrong, and the Hubble tension needs an explanation other than the boundary expansion field's spatial variation.

**Exactly three generations of fermions.** The Standard Model contains three generations of fermions — quarks and leptons that repeat in a pattern of three identical structures with different masses. The Standard Model itself does not say why there are three rather than two or four; the count is an empirical input. The framework derives the count from the topology of the unresolved neutrino's mass-eigenmode structure: dim(su(2)) = 3 is a topological count, not a phenomenological fit. A fourth generation would require a fourth eigenmode that the framework's geometry forbids: the cross-thickness coupling's geometric series, which requires the bare coupling to remain less than unity, would diverge if a fourth eigenmode were added to the count. The Large Hadron Collider has searched extensively for fourth-generation fermions at energies up to several TeV and found none. The framework's prediction is that no fourth generation exists at any accessible collider energy. Future colliders will continue the search; if a fourth generation appears, the framework's three-eigenmode argument has missed something, and the κ derivation that depends on it collapses with it.

Six predictions, six current matches. The matches are not, on their own, evidence the framework is correct. They are evidence the framework is producing the kind of numbers the universe also produces, in regimes where the framework's structural commitments would have to be reading something real for the agreement to be expected. A skeptic can attribute any one of these matches to coincidence. The framework's response is the catalogue: not one number, six. The probability that the framework is reading the universe correctly is not the probability of any single match, but the probability of all the matches together, with no parameters tuned to make any of them work.

## §3 What's Still Open

Six predictions where the bet has not yet been called.

**The tensor-to-scalar ratio of primordial gravitational waves.** The same single constant α that produces the framework's spectral index *n*_s also produces a specific prediction for *r* — the tensor-to-scalar ratio that measures the strength of primordial gravitational waves relative to the density fluctuations. The framework's prediction is *r* = 0.003. This is small enough to be invisible to current observations but well above what some inflationary models predict. CMB-S4 will measure *r* at a precision approaching 0.001 over the next decade. LiteBIRD, scheduled for launch in 2032, will sharpen further. If *r* is measured at or near 0.003, the framework's account of how the early universe produced its perturbations has passed a substantial test. If *r* is measured at 0.05 (the upper end of what some inflation models predict) or below 0.001 (consistent with no detectable primordial gravitational waves), the framework's prediction has failed, and the same structural argument that produces *n*_s correctly has produced *r* incorrectly. Both predictions come from the same α; whether they hold together or break together is the test.

**No right-handed W boson at any accessible collider energy.** The Standard Model's weak force is left-handed: the W boson couples only to left-handed fermions, and the apparent handedness asymmetry of the weak interaction has been a puzzle since Wu's 1956 cobalt-60 experiment. Pati–Salam extensions of the Standard Model propose to restore left-right symmetry by adding a right-handed W boson — the *W*_R — at some energy scale accessible to future colliders. The framework gives a different account. The apparent handedness asymmetry is, on the framework's reading, a phase-sampling consequence of which orientation of the bilateral manifold we occupy: our orientation's weak force catches the neutrino's chirality superposition at one phase of its underlying rhythm; the conjugate orientation's mirror weak force, if we could observe it, would catch the same superposition at the opposite phase. The framework therefore predicts no *W*_R at any collider energy. The Large Hadron Collider has searched for the *W*_R at energies up to several TeV and not found it. The high-luminosity LHC run, beginning in 2030, will extend the search. Future colliders — the Future Circular Collider, if built — will extend further. The framework's prediction stays on the line for as long as the searches keep returning null results. If the *W*_R is ever found, the framework's phase-sampling reading of parity violation is wrong, and Chapter 8's account of why the neutrino's chirality superposition produces handed phenomenology fails.

**Neutrinos as Majorana particles.** The framework reads the neutrino as a Majorana particle in a specific sense: not "its own antiparticle" in the way the textbook description suggests, but a species whose Hopf-projection completion never finished — its particle/antiparticle binary remains topologically unresolved. The empirical signature is the same as the textbook Majorana signature: neutrinoless double beta decay should occur in certain heavy nuclei, at rates determined by the neutrino's mass and the Majorana phases. An international effort — KATRIN, KamLAND-Zen, LEGEND, CUPID, others — is searching for the process. If they find it, the framework's reading of the neutrino's structural status is supported (and several other Majorana scenarios are also supported; the discovery does not single NTE out, but it is consistent with NTE). If they conclusively do not find it — if the neutrino is shown to be a Dirac particle with an independent antiparticle — the framework's account of how the unresolved neutrino produces dark matter through bilateral support fails. Several of the framework's other predictions depend on the unresolved Majorana structure; this test is upstream of them.

**The solar neutrino mixing angle.** The framework's geometric derivation of the leading-order solar mixing angle gives θ₁₂ ≈ 35.3°, the tribimaximal value that falls out of the Hopf geometry of S³. The observed value is 33.4°. The discrepancy is 2°, which is within the framework's leading-order approximation but not within the precision of the current measurement. The framework attributes the discrepancy to the charged lepton mass hierarchy — a deeper algebraic calculation that the framework's structure makes precisely formulable but that has not yet been completed. This is one of the framework's softer open bets. If the deeper calculation closes the 2° gap, the prediction is fully confirmed. If the deeper calculation cannot close the gap, the framework's account of how the neutrino's three mass-eigenmodes map to the observed mixing structure needs revision. This test will resolve gradually as the calculation proceeds and as the measurement is sharpened by neutrino experiments — DUNE, JUNO, T2HK — over the next decade.

**The cosmological lithium-7 problem, partially.** Standard Big Bang nucleosynthesis overpredicts the abundance of lithium-7 in the early universe by a factor of about 2.9 — a discrepancy that has resisted explanation for two decades. The framework's reading is that the cross-thickness coupling enhances lithium-destroying nuclear reactions through additional neutrino flux from the conjugate orientation, reducing the overprediction from a factor of 2.9 to a factor of 1.58. This is a substantial improvement with no free parameters, and Chapter 9 walked through the structural argument. The improvement is not a full resolution. The remaining factor of 1.58 is real, and the framework has not yet identified the structural origin of the residual. If improved measurements of primordial lithium-7 abundance push the discrepancy back toward 2.9 (i.e., the cross-thickness enhancement is not actually present in observation), the framework's account has failed. If they push it toward 1.0 (i.e., the discrepancy has resolved and the framework's reduction is not enough or is wrong), the framework's structural argument needs revision. The test is in measurement programmes that are slow but ongoing.

**The existence and sign of the baryon asymmetry.** The universe contains matter rather than antimatter, in a specific ratio η = 6.1 × 10⁻¹⁰. The Standard Model has Sakharov's conditions for a baryon asymmetry to arise but does not derive the magnitude or, in the simplest treatments, the sign. The framework's geometric structure — the Pin⁻ structure of the bilateral manifold, the CPN-symmetric sorting at the gauge-birth cascade, and the Janus phase δ_J = π/2 — together force a baryon asymmetry to exist and force it to favour matter. The existence and the sign are zero-parameter geometric predictions. The magnitude η requires the leptogenesis mass scale M₁ as input, which the framework currently inherits from observation rather than deriving. The framework's prediction is therefore: there is an asymmetry; matter wins; the magnitude depends on M₁ which is currently constrained by observation but not by structure. If a future cosmological survey finds evidence that the universe could have been arbitrarily close to a matter–antimatter balance, or that the sign of the asymmetry is structurally arbitrary, the framework's geometric account fails. The test is harder to make sharp than the others, but it is real.

The six open bets sit alongside the six current matches. None of them is settled yet. Each one is an exposed flank.

## §4 What NTE Doesn't Predict

The framework is honest about the open work. The list of questions it does not yet answer is part of the framework's empirical posture as much as the list of questions it does. A framework that pretends to answer questions it has not answered is a framework that will eventually be caught; the cleaner discipline is to state the open work plainly.

**The specific Yukawa magnitudes.** The framework gives an ontology for how particles acquire mass — the gauge-birth cascade maps each pre-temporal mode's geometric embedding depth to a Yukawa coupling to the Standard Model Higgs — but it does not derive the specific values. Why the top quark is 340,000 times heavier than the electron is not predicted by the framework. The masses are inherited from observation, as they are in the Standard Model itself. A pathway exists — the J₃(𝕆) Jordan algebra structure, which Chapter 7's algebraic content engages — but the computation that would derive the Yukawa magnitudes from first principles has not been completed. This is the framework's largest piece of open work in fermion physics.

**The unconstructed action principle.** Chapter 9 named this as Gap 4b. The framework's dark matter prediction runs on the two-metric Einstein equations — the gravitational equations that describe how curvature on one orientation of the manifold registers on the other through the cross-thickness coupling. These equations work in the effective theory and produce the closure step's algebraic content correctly. What the framework does not yet have is the ground-up derivation of those equations from a single action principle — the foundational construction that would ensure Bianchi consistency, close the formalism, and place the κ derivation on the same kind of footing as derivations in established field theories. Gap 4b is the framework's foundational vulnerability for the dark-matter prediction specifically. The numerical content of κ holds within the framework's stated approximations even with Gap 4b open; the foundations would be substantially stronger when it closes. Closing Gap 4b is one of the framework's long-term programmes.

**The leptogenesis mass scale.** The baryon-to-photon ratio η = 6.1 × 10⁻¹⁰ depends on the leptogenesis mass scale M₁ ≈ 6 × 10⁹ GeV. The framework's structure suggests that M₁ should be derivable from the geometric scale of the gauge birth, but the derivation has not been completed. M₁ is therefore currently constrained by observation rather than predicted by structure. If the derivation completes and produces a value consistent with observation, η becomes a fully zero-parameter prediction. If the derivation produces a value inconsistent with observation, the framework's account of leptogenesis needs revision.

**The absolute neutrino mass spectrum.** Chapter 8's account of the unresolved neutrino explains why neutrino masses are small but does not derive the specific values. The framework's structure makes the calculation precisely formulable — the engagement of the boundary expansion field with the neutrino's geometric configuration at the Janus boundary, combined with the cascade mapping that determines the Yukawa coupling — but the computation has not been completed. The current measurements of neutrino mass differences (from oscillation experiments) and the upper bound on the absolute scale (from KATRIN and cosmological observations) are consistent with the framework's qualitative picture but do not yet test it quantitatively.

**The slow-roll formula's applicability to the pre-temporal epoch.** The framework's prediction *n*_s = 0.9685 uses the slow-roll formula relating the spectral index to the inflationary potential's parameters. The slow-roll formula was developed in the context of standard inflationary cosmology, where the inflaton field rolls down a potential during inflation, and the formula's applicability to the framework's pre-temporal epoch — where the boundary expansion field has not yet stabilised and the temporal structure has not yet crystallised — has not been verified from first principles. The framework's numerical prediction matches observation within 1σ, so the formula is producing the right number in some operational sense. Whether the formula is correctly grounded in the framework's specific dynamics is an open question that has not been resolved.

**The residual lithium-7 discrepancy.** The cross-thickness mechanism reduces the standard factor-of-2.9 problem to a factor of 1.58, as the previous section noted. The remaining factor of 1.58 is real, and the framework has not identified its origin. This is a partial resolution rather than a full one. Closing the residual is open structural work.

Six pieces of open work, sitting alongside the six current matches and the six open bets. The framework is the kind of structural argument that produces precisely formulable questions where the standard treatments have either silence or fitting parameters. This list is the framework's exposure: the places it has named and not yet closed. Closing any one of them would advance the framework further. Failing to close them does not, by itself, falsify the framework — they are open work, not broken claims — but the framework's eventual standing depends partly on whether the open work converges.

## §5 What Would Settle It

Eighteen items: six current matches, six open bets, six pieces of open work. The framework's empirical exposure is on the table.

Some of these will resolve in the next decade. CMB-S4, returning data toward the end of the 2020s, will sharpen κ, *n*_s, and *r* substantially. LiteBIRD, scheduled for the early 2030s, will sharpen *r* further. DESI's follow-up surveys are running now and will continue refining the dark-energy equation of state. The high-luminosity Large Hadron Collider, beginning in 2030, will extend the search for the *W*_R and any fourth-generation fermions to higher energies. KATRIN's measurement of the absolute neutrino mass scale will sharpen further. Neutrinoless double beta decay experiments — KamLAND-Zen, LEGEND, CUPID — will either find the process or place increasingly tight upper bounds on it. JUNO, beginning operations now, will measure neutrino mass hierarchy and refine the mixing angles. DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande, coming online over the next decade, will extend the neutrino programme further.

Some of these will take longer. The Future Circular Collider, if built, will probe collider physics at energies an order of magnitude beyond the LHC, with implications for the *W*_R search and the fourth-generation question. CMB-S5 and beyond, if pursued, will continue sharpening the cosmological parameters. The leptogenesis mass scale derivation, the J₃(𝕆) Jordan algebra calculation for Yukawa magnitudes, and the action principle that would close Gap 4b are theoretical programmes whose completion timelines are harder to predict. The slow-roll formula's applicability to the pre-temporal epoch is similarly a theoretical question.

The reader's job is not to decide which of these tests is the framework's "real" test. There is no single test. The framework is a structural argument whose specific empirical exposure is, by design, distributed across many tests in many regimes. The framework either passes most of them, with the kind of pattern that is hard to attribute to coincidence, or it does not. The next chapter will return to the methodological posture this kind of test sits inside. This chapter has done the empirical work of making the exposure concrete.

So: what is the worst that could happen?

The worst that could happen is what would happen to any theoretical framework that has staked itself: the predictions break, across multiple regimes, with the kind of high-confidence margin that does not leave room for further structural correction. The framework's working bet is lost. The structural-reading method that the framework embodies continues with other attempts. The eighteen items above are what would constitute the loss, each one a specific place where the universe could say no.

The worst that could happen is also, of course, not the most likely thing to happen. The framework's six current matches were not chosen to be matches — they came out of structural arguments whose physical commitments were made for other reasons — and a framework whose six independent predictions are all within statistical agreement with observation is the kind of framework that, by the standards of empirical science, has earned its right to be tested further. The open bets are how the testing continues. The open work is what closes the framework's structural argument from underneath.

A reader who has reached this chapter has done the work of following the framework's argument from the photon's null-time through the gauge birth, the neutrino's incomplete projection, the bilateral manifold, the dark matter ratio, the universe-as-instrument, the strange silence of the cosmological constant, and the structural rhymes that place the framework in well-explored mathematical territory. The catalogue above is what that argument has cost the framework — what it has put on the table — in concrete empirical terms.

The framework's bet is staked. The data are being gathered. The resolution is in progress.

Ripples in the world die away when the world stops responding to them. NTE has, so far, kept causing the kind of ripples the world responds to with specific numbers that match the framework's specific predictions. Whether the ripples will keep matching is what the next decade's measurements will determine.

The next chapter is the framework's posture in this intermediate state, where the bet has been made and the resolution is in progress.
