There is a fact buried in your physics textbook that nobody has fully followed to its conclusion.

A photon of light — any photon, ever — experiences zero time between the moment it's created and the moment it's absorbed. Not very little time. Zero. From the photon's perspective (to the extent that word means anything for an object with no clock), emission and absorption are the same instant. The entire journey from a star four hundred light-years away to your eye happens, for the photon, in no time at all.

Every physicist knows this. It follows directly from Einstein's special relativity, one of the most tested theories in all of science. And then, almost universally, physicists set it aside and get on with their calculations.

This paper is what happens when you don't set it aside.

The production-flavoured language is an effective description; the framework's underlying claim is that time and the boundary expansion field are two readings of the same geometric structure, related by the constraint.
