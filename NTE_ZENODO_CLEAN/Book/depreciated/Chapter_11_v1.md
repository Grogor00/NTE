## §1 The Octavo

The Octavo is, in Pratchett's Discworld, the most powerful spellbook in existence — a grimoire containing eight Great Spells with which the Creator made the universe and, presumably, with which any sufficiently powerful magician could make another. The number is not negotiable. There are eight spells. Anything more would be untidy; anything fewer would not be enough. The number is part of the structure, in a way that Pratchett's narrators are casual about and his characters take seriously.

The framework of this book has, by an accident of structural arithmetic, also produced eight major predictions. The arithmetic was not designed; the predictions were each derived for their own structural reasons in different chapters of the book, and they came out, when collected at the end, to number eight. The framework's bilateral manifold and Higgs-cascade structure produces eight specific quantitative claims about reality that the universe either honours or doesn't. Whether the resonance with Pratchett's spellbook is meaningful or coincidental is a question for editorial whimsy; what matters is that there are eight, and that each one stakes a piece of the framework on a specific observation.

This chapter lays out the eight in one place. It also lists the framework's subsidiary predictions — claims that are weaker, partial, or pending future measurement — and the conditions under which each major prediction would break. The chapter's purpose is not to argue for the framework. It is to make the framework's empirical posture visible, in detail, so the reader can weigh the totality.

The book has been honest throughout that single matches do not prove a framework. A prediction that lands at 0.83σ from the best measurement is consistent with the framework being correct, and is also consistent with the framework being wrong but coincidentally producing this number for reasons the framework's structural argument doesn't capture. This is what one match is worth. What multiple matches are worth — particularly when each match is a specific number predicted from inputs not chosen to match it — is the question this chapter is built to make tractable. The answer is for the reader to weigh, not for the chapter to assert.

The framework either reads the universe correctly or doesn't, and the test is the totality of the eight. If most hold up under measurement and the residuals are within statistical noise, the framework continues. If several break decisively, the framework's structural argument has missed something fundamental, and the framework is wrong in a way that would need to be corrected or replaced.

What follows is the catalogue.

## §2 How to Read the Catalogue

Each entry in the catalogue has the same structure.

*The prediction.* A specific quantitative claim — a number, a sign, a structural fact — that the framework's machinery produces from inputs the framework has independently committed to.

*The current measurement.* What observation says, with the best precision currently available.

*The match.* The agreement between prediction and measurement, expressed in standard deviations of the measurement uncertainty. This is the standard way physicists describe how well a prediction agrees with observation.

*The structural origin.* Where in the framework the prediction comes from. This matters because the structural commitments the prediction depends on are themselves constraints on the framework — if the structural commitments are required for other reasons, the prediction is not free, and a successful match is meaningful evidence; if the structural commitments are loose, the match is less strongly evidentiary.

*The break point.* What observation, if it occurred, would falsify the prediction. The framework is committed to specific outcomes; the chapter is explicit about what would have to be different to make the framework wrong on this point.

A note on parameters. The framework predicts each of the eight numbers with no parameters tuned to match observation. Each prediction is a structural consequence of independently-motivated geometric commitments. The single inherited constant the framework uses — α, the coupling constant in the equation H = α|Φ_NTE|² that has been with us since Chapter 4 — is fixed by present-day cosmological measurement of the Hubble rate; the predictions are then derived from that fixed value, and they cannot be retuned without breaking the H₀ match. The framework runs on the smallest possible set of inputs.

A note on tone. The chapter is more reference-style than the rest of the book. Each entry is short, focused, and gives the reader what they need to assess the framework's standing on that prediction. The reader should feel free to skim entries they have already encountered in detail in earlier chapters — κ in Chapter 9, the cosmic mean Hubble parameter in the popular paper, the baryon asymmetry in earlier sections — and read more carefully the entries that are new. The chapter is built to be a reference more than a narrative.

## §3 The Eight Predictions

### 3.1 The Dark-Matter-to-Baryon Ratio: κ = 5.50

*The prediction.* The cosmic ratio of dark matter to ordinary matter is exactly κ = 3/(2√π − 3) = 5.50. Derivation: Chapter 9.

*The current measurement.* Planck 2018 measured κ = 5.38 ± 0.15 from the relative heights of the acoustic peaks in the cosmic microwave background.

*The match.* 0.83σ. Within statistical agreement.

*The structural origin.* The framework's bilateral manifold (Chapter 7); the unresolved neutrino's bilaterally-supported wavefunction (Chapter 8); the cross-thickness coupling acting on total stress-energy (Chapter 9 §3); the Hopf-fibration projection on the shared S³ boundary (Chapter 6). Two structural steps: the bare coupling step κ₀ = 3/√(4π) from angular uniformity + three Majorana eigenmodes + sphere-area normalisation; the closure step κ = κ₀ / (1 − κ₀) from the geometric series the bilateral structure forces.

*The break point.* If CMB-S4 (next-generation cosmic microwave background experiment, expected to deliver roughly twice Planck's precision in the early 2030s) measures κ at a value more than 3σ from 5.50 with its tighter uncertainty, the framework's central empirical prediction fails, and the structural argument that produces 5.50 has missed something. The current 0.36% κ₀-level residual has no positive structural candidate explanation; CMB-S4 will reveal whether the residual is real or measurement noise.

### 3.2 The Cosmic Mean Hubble Parameter: H₀ = 67.6 km/s/Mpc

*The prediction.* The Hubble parameter, measured as the cosmic mean over volumes large enough to average the spatial variation of the boundary expansion field |Φ_NTE|, is H₀ = 67.6 km/s/Mpc. Derivation: popular paper §12.

*The current measurement.* Planck's CMB-derived value is H₀ = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc. This is the cosmic-mean measurement, integrating over the universe's full expansion history rather than being biased by void-rich lines of sight.

*The match.* 0.4σ. Within statistical agreement, very tight.

*The structural origin.* The equation H = α|Φ_NTE|² (Chapter 4); the spatial variation of |Φ_NTE| with local mass-energy density (Chapter 5 / popular paper §12). Most of the universe by volume is in cosmic voids where |Φ_NTE| is at its asymptotic value; the cosmic mean H₀ is set by that asymptotic value.

*The break point.* If the local supernova measurement (currently ~73 km/s/Mpc, the Hubble tension's high value) does not converge toward 67.6 as supernova surveys integrate over more cosmic volume — particularly if surveys restricted to overdense lines of sight do not show suppressed H₀ values — the framework's void-enhancement explanation of the Hubble tension fails, and the framework owes the universe a different account of why the local and CMB values disagree.

### 3.3 The Spectral Index: n_s = 0.9685

*The prediction.* The primordial scalar spectral index — the slight tilt of the inflationary power spectrum toward larger scales — is n_s = 0.9685.

*The current measurement.* Planck's measurement is n_s = 0.9649 ± 0.0042.

*The match.* 0.85σ. Within statistical agreement.

*The structural origin.* The framework's slow-roll-style derivation produces n_s as a function of the single inherited constant α. The same α that fixes the present-day Hubble parameter fixes the spectral index, with no additional free parameter introduced.

*The break point.* If CMB-S4 measures n_s at a value more than 3σ from 0.9685 with its tighter uncertainty, the framework's slow-roll-style derivation in the pre-temporal regime is wrong, and the framework owes a different account of the primordial perturbation spectrum.

### 3.4 The Tensor-to-Scalar Ratio: r = 0.003

*The prediction.* The ratio of tensor to scalar perturbations from the inflationary epoch — measuring the relative power in primordial gravitational waves — is r = 0.003.

*The current measurement.* The current upper bound from BICEP/Keck and Planck is r < 0.06 at 95% confidence. The framework's prediction sits well below the current upper bound, in territory the next-generation CMB experiments will probe.

*The match.* Untested. The current data are consistent with the prediction but cannot yet confirm it.

*The structural origin.* As with n_s, the framework produces r from the single inherited constant α. The relationship between r and n_s is a structural consequence of the framework's pre-temporal dynamics.

*The break point.* CMB-S4 and the LiteBIRD satellite (planned for the 2030s) will deliver sensitivity to r at the 0.001 level. If r is measured at significantly above 0.003 (e.g. r > 0.01 at high confidence) or below 0.001 (e.g. r < 0.0005 at high confidence), the framework's prediction fails. This is one of the framework's most distinctive sharp predictions: a specific, falsifiable, very-soon-to-be-tested number.

### 3.5 The Atmospheric Neutrino Mixing Angle: θ₂₃ = 45°

*The prediction.* The atmospheric neutrino mixing angle — one of the three angles in the Pontecorvo-Maki-Nakagawa-Sakata matrix that describes neutrino oscillation — is exactly 45°.

*The current measurement.* Combined oscillation experiments (Super-Kamiokande, T2K, NOvA, IceCube) give θ₂₃ = 45.0° ± 1.0°.

*The match.* Direct match within measurement uncertainty.

*The structural origin.* The Z₂ symmetry of the su(2) algebra's generators transverse to the Standard Model Higgs vacuum direction. This is a theorem about the framework's S³ structure, not a fit. The angle is forced to be 45° by the geometry; nothing the framework could do would let it produce a different value.

*The break point.* If future neutrino oscillation experiments (DUNE, Hyper-K, JUNO) measure θ₂₃ at a value more than 3σ from 45° with their improved precision, the framework's identification of the atmospheric mixing with the Z₂ symmetry of the relevant su(2) generators is wrong. This would have implications beyond the prediction itself; the same structural argument is involved in the framework's reading of neutrino oscillation more broadly.

### 3.6 Three Generations of Fermions

*The prediction.* The Standard Model has exactly three generations of fermions — three sets of quarks and leptons with the same gauge structure but different masses. Not two, not four.

*The current measurement.* Particle physics has confirmed three generations through decades of collider and oscillation experiments. The Z boson decay width is consistent with exactly three light neutrino species. No fourth generation has been observed despite extensive search.

*The match.* Direct match. The universe has exactly three generations.

*The structural origin.* The Jordan algebra J₃(𝕆) — the algebra of 3×3 Hermitian matrices over the octonions — has a specific structural role in the framework's algebraic reduction at the gauge birth (Chapter 7). The three generations are the three independent rows of this 3×3 algebra. The algebra J₂(𝕆) has only two rows, which would give two generations; J₄(𝕆) is not a Jordan algebra (Albert's theorem), which forecloses four. Three is the structurally accessible value, and it is what the universe has.

*The break point.* If a fourth generation of fermions is discovered (e.g. at higher LHC energies or future colliders), the framework's J₃(𝕆) reduction is wrong, and the framework has badly mis-identified the algebraic structure operating at the gauge birth. The framework's bet is that no fourth generation exists; this bet is observationally tested by every search that does not find one.

### 3.7 The Baryon Asymmetry: Existence and Matter-Favouring Sign

*The prediction.* The universe contains a baryon asymmetry — a small excess of matter over antimatter that survived to produce the present-day matter content — and the asymmetry favours matter rather than antimatter. Both predictions are zero-free-parameter consequences of the framework's geometry.

*The current measurement.* The observed baryon-to-photon ratio is η = 6.1×10⁻¹⁰. The universe is overwhelmingly made of matter rather than antimatter; this has been confirmed by direct observation (the absence of substantial annihilation radiation that would accompany large-scale antimatter regions) and by primordial nucleosynthesis (the observed light-element abundances require approximately the η measurement).

*The match.* The existence and sign predictions are direct matches. The magnitude prediction (η specifically) is currently inherited rather than derived — the leptogenesis mass scale M₁ ≈ 6×10⁹ GeV that fixes η is observationally constrained, not predicted from the framework's geometry.

*The structural origin.* The Pin⁻ structure of the bilateral manifold (a specific topological property of how the two orientations relate); the CPN-symmetric sorting at the gauge-birth cascade (Chapter 7); the Janus-phase angle δ_J = π/2 (the geometric phase fixing which orientation lands on which side of the boundary). These three together force a baryon asymmetry to exist between the orientations and force the sign of the asymmetry to favour matter.

*The break point.* The existence and sign predictions are not currently testable in a way that could falsify them — the universe being matter-dominated is a fact already observed, and the predictions reproduce it. The magnitude prediction would become testable if the framework's broader programme derives M₁ from the geometric scale of the gauge birth (open work). At the existence-and-sign level, the prediction is already confirmed by what the universe is. At the magnitude level, the prediction is currently held back by the framework's inability to derive M₁; if M₁ is derived and the resulting η does not match observation, the framework's claim to predicting the magnitude fails. If observed antimatter regions are eventually detected at cosmological scale, both the existence and the matter-favouring-sign predictions break.

### 3.8 The Dark Energy Equation of State: w = −1

*The prediction.* The true equation-of-state parameter of dark energy is exactly w = −1, corresponding to dark energy as a pure cosmological constant.

*The current measurement.* The DESI (Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument) survey, with results released in 2024, hints at w slightly above −1 at low redshift, with the deviation potentially statistically significant. If the deviation persists, it would suggest dark energy is evolving rather than being a pure cosmological constant.

*The match.* The framework predicts that the *true* w is exactly −1, but that the *observed* low-redshift w may differ because of the same void-enhancement effect that produces the Hubble tension. Specifically, supernovae at low redshift are observed predominantly through cosmic voids, where |Φ_NTE| is at its asymptotic value. This biases the inferred local expansion rate upward, which appears (when fitted to a w-CDM model) as an apparent w slightly greater than −1 at low redshift. The DESI signal is, on the framework's reading, the same void-bias effect that produces the Hubble tension viewed through a different observational window.

*The structural origin.* The framework's reading of dark energy: the geometric floor of the boundary expansion rate, the value the Hubble parameter would take if all matter were removed. This floor is exactly constant; the true equation of state is exactly w = −1. The apparent low-redshift departure is the void-bias effect (popular paper §12).

*The break point.* If the apparent w deviation does not track measured void fraction along lines of sight (i.e. surveys restricted to overdense lines of sight do not show w closer to −1), the void-enhancement explanation fails, and the framework owes an account of why dark energy appears to evolve. If the running of w with redshift does not match the framework's specific prediction (where w → −1 as the redshift integration covers more cosmic volume), the framework's reading is wrong.

## §4 Other Predictions

The eight predictions above are the framework's major structural commitments — each one a specific quantitative claim derived from independent geometric commitments. The framework also makes several subsidiary predictions that are weaker, partial, or that involve more interpretive flexibility.

*The solar neutrino mixing angle.* The framework predicts sin²θ₁₂ = 1/3 at leading order — the so-called tribimaximal value — derived from the Hopf geometry of S³. The observed value is 0.307, about 2° below the leading-order prediction. The framework attributes the residual to the charged-lepton mass hierarchy, which requires a deeper algebraic calculation that has not been completed. The leading-order prediction is consistent with observation in the right ballpark; the residual is open work.

*Three exactly neutrino species.* The dimension of the unresolved neutrino's mass-eigenmode su(2) is three; the framework predicts exactly three neutrino species (no sterile neutrino). This is observationally established at high confidence — the Z boson decay width and direct oscillation measurements both confirm three light species — but is repeated here because it is a separate structural prediction from the three-generations claim.

*Neutrinos are Majorana particles.* Rather than Dirac. The framework reads this as a topological fact about the unresolved neutrino's non-projection (Chapter 8). Experimental tests through neutrinoless double beta decay are underway, with the most sensitive current limits placing the effective Majorana mass below ~0.1 eV. If neutrinoless double beta decay is observed, the prediction is confirmed. If improved experiments definitively rule it out, the framework's reading of the neutrino's non-resolution is wrong.

*Direct-detection nulls continue.* The framework predicts that no underground dark-matter detector will produce a positive signal, because dark matter is not a particle in our orientation. Every additional null result from XENON-nT, LZ, PandaX-4T, and successor experiments confirms the prediction; a positive detection (if confirmed across multiple independent experiments) would break it.

*Dwarf-galaxy dark-matter scatter.* The framework predicts wide scatter in dwarf-galaxy dark-matter fractions and tight convergence to κ = 5.50 in galaxy-cluster systems, with the transition occurring at the coherence length ~40 megaparsecs (Chapter 10). Future surveys (Vera Rubin Observatory, Euclid) will test this distribution.

*Lithium-7 partial resolution.* The standard Big Bang nucleosynthesis prediction overshoots the observed lithium-7 abundance by a factor of 2.9. The framework's cross-thickness mechanism reduces this discrepancy to a factor of 1.58 — a substantial improvement with no free parameters, though not a complete solution. If the residual factor of 1.58 turns out to have a separate explanation (e.g. astrophysical depletion mechanisms), the framework's lithium-7 contribution is consistent. If the residual is decisively traced to a mechanism inconsistent with the framework, this prediction fails.

## §5 Where the Framework Would Fail

The chapter has been explicit about the break point of each prediction. Two further layers of break point are worth flagging because they involve the framework as a whole rather than individual predictions.

*A persistent multi-prediction residual.* If multiple predictions land at residuals at or above the 2σ–3σ level under improved precision — particularly if the residuals share a common feature (all biased high, or all biased low, or all departing in a specific direction from the framework's predicted values) — the framework has a systematic error somewhere in its structural commitments. The predictions are derived from a small set of geometric inputs; if those inputs are wrong, multiple predictions will go wrong together. A single prediction at 2σ–3σ residual is statistically unremarkable; eight predictions all at the same direction of 2σ residual would be the framework breaking.

*A direct contradiction at the structural level.* If the framework's foundational commitments are observationally falsified independently of any quantitative prediction — for example, if the universe is shown to be topologically open (a torus or hyperbolic space) rather than closed (S³), or if cosmological observations require a non-CPN bilateral structure, or if a fourth generation of fermions is discovered — the framework's structural argument breaks at the foundation, and most of the predictions follow it down. The framework's bet is that none of these things is the case. The bet is staked on observation, and observation will eventually settle it.

*The Gap-4b vulnerability.* Chapter 9 §8 named explicitly: the framework's two-metric Einstein equations describe the cross-thickness coupling consistently in the working effective theory, but the action principle from which they would follow rigorously has not yet been constructed. This is the framework's foundational vulnerability for the κ derivation specifically. If the action-principle construction fails — if the framework's broader research programme cannot show that the two-metric equations follow from a fundamental Lagrangian rigorously — the κ prediction is not necessarily wrong, but its theoretical foundation is incomplete in a way that should worry careful readers. Open structural research; flagged here for completeness.

The framework does not ask the reader to accept all its commitments on faith. It asks the reader to track what the commitments are, what they imply for observation, and what would have to be different to make them wrong. Chapter 9's full disclosure pattern — robust under the framework's reading, open structural research, where the prediction would fail — is the same pattern this chapter applies across all eight predictions. The framework is committed to a specific, testable account of the universe, and has identified explicitly the conditions under which it would fail.

## §6 The Posture

What the chapter has done is make the framework's empirical posture visible in detail.

Eight predictions, each a specific quantitative claim derived from independent structural commitments. Six of the eight currently match observation within statistical agreement: κ at 0.83σ, H₀ at 0.4σ, n_s at 0.85σ, θ₂₃ as a direct match, three generations as a direct match, and the existence and sign of the baryon asymmetry as direct matches. Two are awaiting decisive measurement: r in the next-generation CMB experiments, and w through the DESI follow-up surveys. The framework has staked specific outcomes on both, and the measurements are coming.

Whether this is impressive or unimpressive depends on the reader's prior expectations. A prediction at 0.83σ from observation, taken alone, is unremarkable; a coincidence the framework happened to land on. Six predictions simultaneously matching observation across independent physical regimes — cosmological, particle-physics, neutrino-oscillation — with no parameters tuned to make any of them work, is a different kind of pattern. It is the kind of pattern that, if it persists across the remaining tests, would constitute evidence the framework is reading something real about the structure of the universe.

The chapter does not assert this. The reader is the only person who can weigh what the catalogue is worth. The framework's bet is that the pattern persists. The bet, like the framework, is observationally testable.

In Pratchett's Discworld, the Octavo's eight Great Spells are what the Creator used to make the world. The framework's eight predictions are not making anything; they are reading what is already made. The framework either reads correctly or doesn't, and the test is the totality of the eight under measurements that are still being carried out.

The reader is invited to follow the news.
