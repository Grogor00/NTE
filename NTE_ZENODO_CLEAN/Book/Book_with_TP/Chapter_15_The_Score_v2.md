## §3 Cosmology Beyond the Chord

Five more cosmological predictions follow from the framework's broader structural commitments. They are not all part of the chord — the unification calculation does not place them at specific slots in the heat-kernel computation — but they follow directly from the same closed three-sphere geometry and the dynamical relation H = β_NTE|Φ_NTE|² that the chord shares.

**The Hubble parameter H₀ = 67.6 km/s/Mpc.** The cosmic-mean expansion rate of the present universe. The framework derives this from the relation H = β_NTE|Φ_NTE|² applied to the present-day cosmological value of the boundary expansion field. Observations from the cosmic microwave background — Planck, ACT, SPT — give H₀ ≈ 67–68 km/s/Mpc. *Matched.* There is a famous Hubble tension: late-universe direct measurements (using supernovae and Cepheid variables, the SH0ES collaboration in particular) give a higher value, around 73 km/s/Mpc. The framework's reading is that the CMB-derived value is the correct cosmic mean, and the late-universe local measurements are affected by void enhancement — matter underdensities in our cosmic neighbourhood that locally accelerate the apparent expansion. Whether this is the right reading of the tension is one of the framework's pending tests, with the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's upcoming surveys positioned to map the cosmic void structure precisely enough to settle it.

**The tensor-to-scalar ratio r ≈ 4 × 10⁻³ (hilltop reading — conditional survivor).** A measure of how strongly primordial gravitational waves contribute to the CMB compared to the density fluctuations. Standard inflationary models leave this as a free parameter that depends on the inflaton potential's shape. The framework's hilltop reading of the pre-temporal sector — the same inflation slot that delivers n_s in §2 — gives **r ~ 4×10⁻³**, comfortably below current bounds. *Honest scope:* r's value depends on a specific assumption about the boundary expansion field's kinetic energy — the simplest assumption (the standard "canonical" form natural to a minimally-coupled constraint-bound real scalar) is what the framework currently uses, but the framework has not yet derived that this is the form deeper geometry requires; the technical companion papers carry the open derivation. So r is a *conditional survivor* — consistent at clean tier with no observational input feeding the derivation, but conditional on the open kinetic-term default. *Test: CMB-S4 / LiteBIRD in the early 2030s*, with sensitivity approaching r ≈ 10⁻³. Two pathways to failure: r measured far above the hilltop value would mean the framework's inflationary reading has missed something at the dynamics level; a different kinetic-term default derived from deeper geometry would shift the r prediction without breaking the framework's structural reading.

**The dark-energy equation of state w = −1.** A clean cosmological constant: vacuum energy with negative pressure equal to its energy density. The framework's reading of dark energy is that it *is* the cosmological constant — a structural quietness on the universe-instrument's geometry rather than a time-varying field — with no dynamical dark-energy component. The recent observational hint of w slightly different from −1 (the DESI 2024 result, suggesting w at low redshift may be more negative than −1) the framework reads as the void-enhancement signature rather than as genuine cosmological deviation. *Test: DESI's full survey through 2026 onwards will sharpen the picture.* If w stays at −1 cleanly under DESI's full-survey corrections for void enhancement, the framework's reading holds. If w is genuinely time-varying — if some kind of evolving dark-energy field is required to fit observation — the framework's pure cosmological-constant reading has to be substantially revised.

**The coherence-length transition at ~40 megaparsecs.** Chapter 10 developed this one. The cross-thickness coupling, mediated through the unresolved neutrino's bilateral wavefunction, has a coherence length set by the neutrino's free-streaming distance. Below this scale, the dark-to-ordinary mass ratio in individual systems should show wide scatter. Above it, the ratio should converge tightly to the cosmic mean of 5.50. *The test is the next decade of galaxy surveys* — Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey, Euclid, the future spectroscopic surveys — which will map dark-matter fractions across hundreds of millions of systems at all relevant scales. If the transition appears at ~40 Mpc cleanly, the prediction is confirmed. If the transition appears at a substantially different scale, or if the scatter pattern is wrong, the framework's specific coherence-length claim breaks.

## §4 Particle Physics

Several of the framework's predictions live on the particle-physics side rather than the cosmological side. Some are positive predictions of *existence* — specific things should be observed. Others are negative predictions of *absence* — specific things should not be observed, in increasingly sensitive experiments, for decade after decade.

**Exactly three generations of fermions.** The Standard Model has three generations of quarks and leptons: the electron and its corresponding quark and neutrino partners; the muon and its partners; the tau and its partners. Why three? The framework derives the count from the structure of its octonionic algebra under the gauge-birth reduction — specifically, the three is the number of independent generations the Jordan algebra J₃(O) supports as a complete fermionic content. *Matched* — there are three generations, and no fourth has been observed despite extensive searches at the LHC.

**The atmospheric mixing angle θ_23 = 45°.** One of the three angles in the neutrino mixing matrix (the PMNS matrix), governing how electron-, muon-, and tau-flavoured neutrinos mix during propagation. The framework derives the maximal-mixing value θ_23 = 45° from the structural symmetry of the unresolved neutrino's bilateral wavefunction. Current observational global fits give θ_23 around 47–49°, depending on which experiments are weighted and how systematic uncertainties are handled. *Consistent with the framework's prediction at current precision* — the framework's 45° sits within the natural systematic uncertainty of the global fits, though the fits are slightly off the framework's exact prediction and tightening will eventually settle whether the agreement holds.

**The existence and matter-favouring sign of the baryon asymmetry.** The universe is made of matter rather than antimatter, with a ratio of roughly six baryons per ten billion photons. The framework reads this as a consequence of leptogenesis at the M_1 mass scale (see §2). *Matched* — the asymmetry exists, with the sign and order of magnitude the framework predicts. The specific value of the asymmetry parameter η ≈ 6 × 10⁻¹⁰ the framework still treats as observationally inherited rather than fully derived — the structural commitments fix where in the calculation the leptogenesis decay channel sits, but the precise magnitude depends on parameters not yet closed at zero-parameter tier.

**Majorana 0νββ detection in the inverted-hierarchy regime.** If neutrinos are Majorana particles — particles that are their own antiparticles — then a rare nuclear process called neutrinoless double-beta decay should be observable, with a specific rate determined by the effective Majorana mass. The framework's structural commitment to the unresolved neutrino's CPN-self-conjugacy makes the Majorana character a structural consequence, not a free choice. *Test: KamLAND-Zen, LEGEND, and successor experiments* are reaching the sensitivity to either detect 0νββ in the inverted-hierarchy regime or push the limits to where the inverted hierarchy is ruled out within the standard mass-hierarchy schemes. If 0νββ is detected with the expected rate, the framework's structural reading of the neutrino is confirmed. If the inverted hierarchy is ruled out and 0νββ remains unobserved at higher precision, the framework's commitment to the Majorana neutrino has to accommodate a non-Majorana neutrino — which would be a substantial revision.

**No fourth generation of fermions.** The framework's algebraic structure permits exactly three generations and forbids a fourth. *Test: the LHC's high-luminosity run, and successor colliders, will push the lower mass bounds on hypothetical fourth-generation fermions higher and higher.* If a fourth-generation particle is unambiguously detected, the framework's algebraic three-generation count is wrong, and the J₃(O) structure has to be reconsidered. As of 2026, no such particle has been seen, consistent with the framework's prediction.

**No right-handed W boson (W_R).** Pati-Salam-type unification schemes typically postulate a right-handed analog of the W boson, mediating gauge interactions in the right-handed (mirror) sector at high mass. The framework's reading of the Pati-Salam structure under the chirality-superposition mechanism removes this prediction: the right-handed gauge interactions act in the mirror sector — the other orientation of the bilateral manifold — not as a separate observable particle in our sector. *Test: LHC searches for W_R signatures will continue to push the lower mass bound higher.* If a W_R is unambiguously detected at any mass, the framework's reading of the right-handed sector as the mirror orientation is wrong. As of 2026, no W_R has been seen.

**No WIMP direct detection.** The dominant theoretical approach to dark matter for the past four decades has postulated a weakly-interacting massive particle, the WIMP, that should be detectable in deep underground experiments. The framework's reading of dark matter — as the gravitational signature of the mirror sector's matter, registered through the cross-thickness coupling — predicts that no such particle exists in our orientation. *Test: XENON, LUX, PandaX, and successor experiments continue to refine their sensitivity, finding nothing.* Each null result tightens the framework's prediction. A positive unambiguous detection would break this reading. As of 2026, the standard parameter space of WIMP candidates has been definitively ruled out, with the remaining bits of theory space progressively less natural.

The catalogue pulls together. Of the framework's roughly fifteen distinct predictions, many are already matched. The dark-to-baryon ratio sits within 0.83σ of Planck, now backed by the Janus action. The atmospheric mixing angle sits near maximal, consistent with the framework's structural symmetry at current precision. The WIMP that the standard treatment expects is absent, decade after decade. The right-handed W boson that Pati-Salam constructions predict is absent. No fourth generation has shown up at the LHC.

Pending tests cluster around the next ten years. CMB-S4, scheduled for first results in the early 2030s, will tighten the dark-matter-to-baryon ratio by a factor of roughly two, settling the small κ₀-level residual that the framework's current structural reading does not explain through any positive mechanism. An r measurement substantially off the hilltop value (~4×10⁻³) would break the inflationary picture. A coherence-length pattern that doesn't appear at ~40 Mpc, or appears at a substantially different scale, would break the cross-thickness coupling's specific empirical signature.

Each break would be a specific structural failure. The framework's commitments are tied tightly enough that some failures would propagate widely. A κ break compromises the bilateral picture; the bilateral picture supports the closed manifold; the closed manifold supports the spectral triple. The catalogue's predictions are not independent test cases. They are pressure points on the same architecture.
