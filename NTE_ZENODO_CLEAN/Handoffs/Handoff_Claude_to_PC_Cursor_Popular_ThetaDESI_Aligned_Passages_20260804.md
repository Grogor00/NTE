## Passage 1 — Solar mixing angle (replaces any "sin²θ₁₂ = 1/3 / one-third, awaiting corrections" framing)

Until 2026 this account carried a clean-looking prediction for the solar mixing angle — the number that says how strongly two of the three neutrino types blend. The framework put it at exactly one-third and presented that as a structural result awaiting small corrections. Then the JUNO experiment measured it directly: 0.3092, give or take 0.0087 — which excludes one-third by three-and-a-half to nearly four standard deviations. Pressed on where the one-third had come from, it proved to rest on an assumption — an even-handed democracy among the generations in one particular direction — that the framework's deeper structure does not actually force. So the framework has not tried to rescue the number with a fresh correction; it has withdrawn it. The solar angle is now an open problem, waiting on a specific piece of structural homework rather than on a fit. A value close to the measurement does exist in the model-building literature, and the framework declines to adopt it: taking a number because it sits near the data, before the structure that would compel it is in hand, is exactly the number-fitting this framework forbids itself everywhere else. Losing a prediction cleanly is better than keeping one dishonestly.

## Passage 2 — Dark energy / DESI (replaces any "void mechanism explains / hints at the DESI signal" framing)

The framework's sharpest current disagreement with observation is dark energy. It predicts that dark energy is exactly constant — an equation of state of precisely minus one, with no freedom to adjust. Through 2025–2026 the DESI survey, combined with the microwave background, began to prefer a dark energy that changes over time — roughly three standard deviations away from constant in the supernova-independent combination. There is no internal dial the framework can turn to absorb this, and it does not pretend otherwise. The framework has its own mechanism involving cosmic voids, and one might hope to recruit it to explain the DESI signal; but when that mechanism is actually run forward it pushes the wrong way, and it cannot touch the part of the data that carries the signal. So the framework says so, and lets the prediction stand naked. The verdict will come between 2027 and 2029, from DESI's full survey and from Euclid and Rubin. If the evolving-dark-energy signal firms up, this prediction fails — cleanly, and on the record. (The void mechanism does still make a testable mark of its own: supernovae seen along void-dominated lines of sight should look slightly brighter. Finding that would test the mechanism; it would not rescue the constant-dark-energy prediction.)
