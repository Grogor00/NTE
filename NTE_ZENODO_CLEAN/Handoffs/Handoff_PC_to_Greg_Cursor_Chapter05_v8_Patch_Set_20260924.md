## 0. The finding that made Ch 5 the right chapter to take next

G3 predicted Ch 5 would be the most exposed chapter to the rest-energy correction, because its whole argument is the two-field comparison. It is exposed, but not the way I expected. **Chapter 5 contradicts Chapter 4, and it also contradicts itself.**

Ch 5's opening says the photon accumulates no time because it has *"no engagement with |Φ_NTE| at all"*. Four pages later the same chapter says, correctly and emphatically, that **|Φ_NTE| does not couple to particles at all** — that is the chapter's central structural claim about the field, repeated three times and used to argue why the two scalars cannot be one.

Both cannot be true. If nothing couples to |Φ_NTE|, then "no engagement with |Φ_NTE|" does not distinguish the photon from an electron; every particle has exactly that much engagement, which is none. The photon's zero is not a zero of engagement with the boundary expansion field. It is a zero of **rest energy**, which is what sets embedding depth — and |Φ_NTE| then sets the rate for whatever is embedded. Ch 4 v9 now says this; Ch 5 v7 says something else; and the version Ch 5 says would, if taken seriously, collapse the two-field argument the chapter exists to make.

So this is not a propagation of the Ch 4 patch. It is a defect Ch 4's correction exposed, one level down, in the chapter that had the most to lose from it. The correction makes Ch 5's own argument work better: rest energy decides who rides, |Φ_NTE| decides how fast the ride runs, and the reason the Higgs cannot be |Φ_NTE| is that one is locked and the other has descended through eight and a half orders of magnitude. Those are three clean statements about three different things, and v7 had them tangled.

The rest of the pass is the cosmic-time correction, the emergence register, and one small physics slip.
