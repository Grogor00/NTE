## 3. **AY1–AY3 — the Chapter 12 narrowing**

AC's ruling, which the drafting follows exactly: **the chord's claim is common origin, not common failure; a κ drift refutes the κ chain and the claim that one computation produced all three, not each of the three severally.**

### AY1 — REQUIRED. The exposure paragraph.

> **OLD:** *"This is more exposed than a list of separate predictions would be, and the exposure is deliberate. A list can retreat: maybe the dark matter comes from one mechanism and the neutrino scales from another, and if one fails the others survive. A chord cannot retreat that way. **The notes share their source, so a genuine failure in one propagates to the commitments underneath them all** — the closed three-sphere, the bilateral structure, the Hopf fibration, the boundary expansion field, the gauge-birth cascade."*

> **NEW:** *"This is more exposed than a list of separate predictions would be, and the exposure is deliberate — but it is worth being exact about what is exposed, because the obvious strong reading is not the one the framework can defend. A list can retreat: maybe the dark matter comes from one mechanism and the neutrino scales from another, and if one fails the others survive. The chord's claim is that they do **not** come from separate mechanisms — that one computation on one geometry produced all of them. **What a failure would refute is that claim of common origin, together with the chain the failed note sits on. It would not, by itself, refute the other notes severally.** Jointly produced is not the same as jointly falsified, and the framework should not borrow the second from the first."*

### AY2 — REQUIRED, and it keeps a real link the loose version buried.

> **OLD:** *"If the dark-matter ratio drifts away from 5.50 as CMB-S4 tightens the measurement, the first note is wrong — and because the bare coupling beneath it also forces the Majorana condition, **the failure would reach into the neutrino sector's foundations**, not just the cosmology."*

> **NEW:** *"If the dark-matter ratio drifts away from 5.50 as CMB-S4 tightens the measurement, the first note is wrong — and the chapter should be exact about how far the damage travels, because the honest answer is more interesting than the loose one. κ's derivation uses **no gauge-birth temperature, no seesaw scale and no thickness magnitude**; it runs on the Hopf projection and the algebra's three directions, and nothing else. So a κ drift would refute the κ chain and the claim that one computation delivered it alongside the others — and it would **not**, by itself, reach the temperature or the mass scales. What it **would** reach is the **Majorana condition**, for a specific reason rather than a general one: the same κ₀ < 1 that makes the feedback series converge is what forbids the Dirac count, so those two stand or fall on one inequality. That is a real link, and the chapter names it rather than asserting a wholesale propagation it cannot support."*

**Why this is the better paragraph and not merely the safer one.** The loose version says the failure reaches "the neutrino sector's foundations" — vague, and unsupported. The narrowed version names **one specific coupled pair** and gives the reason: κ₀ < 1 both converges the series and excludes the Dirac count at 6/√(4π) > 1. **That is in Part 2 §IX, verbatim, and I verified it this session.** The chapter trades an impressive-sounding claim for a demonstrable one.
