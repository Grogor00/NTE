## PART 1 — Ω_k recheck. **The sign claim survives; the explanation does not.**

### Why it no longer holds

**Curvature dilutes only under accelerated expansion.** Ω_k ∝ 1/(aH)²; it falls only while the comoving Hubble radius shrinks, which requires ä > 0. **Under decelerating expansion Ω_k grows.** That is the flatness problem, and inflation is the standard answer to it.

**The corpus's accelerated epoch was the inflationary one**, which the 2026-09-26 ratification withdrew.

**And the 10²⁰ mismatch reaches the expansion history itself, not only the observables.** Companion 8 §2 places the de Sitter sub-phase *"at H ≈ M_Pl"*. The constraint H = β_NTE|Φ|² at φ* ≈ 16 M_Pl gives **H ≈ 3.6 × 10⁻⁸ GeV**. The corpus cannot currently state the Hubble rate of its own de Sitter sub-phase consistently with its own constraint — so "the e-fold run" is not a specified history to lean on.

With Φ_NTE unable to inflate, **NTE may have no inflaton at all** — and then *"what reheats the universe after inflation?"* is the wrong question.

Inflation exists to solve the **horizon** and **flatness** problems. A boundary-only ontology in which every point shares one S³ may not have a horizon problem to solve. Flatness would still need an account (Part 1 above), but that account need not be inflationary.

**Three routes, in AC's order of preference:**

1. **Ask whether NTE needs inflation at all.** Cheapest, most NTE-native, and it would resolve the reheating gap, the ordering contradiction and the Ω_k explanation together. If the boundary ontology solves the horizon problem and something else handles flatness, the whole inflationary sector may be surplus rather than broken.
2. **Find a reheating mechanism compatible with ξ = 1/6.** Hard, per the above, and requires either breaking conformality somewhere or exploiting the mirror sector.
