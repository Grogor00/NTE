## Scope of this note

The mathematics here is **standard**: a compact Riemannian manifold has a *discrete* Laplace and Dirac spectrum, and the harmonic oscillator's evenly-spaced energy levels follow from the representation theory of the Heisenberg algebra. **No novelty is claimed.** This is an explanatory note for the curious reader, recording the one framework-specific reading the Non-Temporal Emergence (NTE) framework attaches to that standard fact: that the discreteness of quantum states is the compactness of the $S^3$ boundary, seen from the inside.

## 1. Compact means discrete

On an infinite line, a free particle's momentum can take any real value: the spectrum is continuous. On a circle of circumference $L$, periodicity forces $p_n = 2\pi n/L$ — a *discrete* ladder. The same is true in higher dimensions: the Laplacian on a closed (compact) manifold has a discrete set of eigenvalues, and so does the Dirac operator. Discreteness is what compactness *does* to a spectrum. This is textbook spectral geometry; on the round $S^3$ it gives the familiar tower of eigenvalues with their degeneracies.

The harmonic oscillator tells the same story algebraically. Its ladder operators $a, a^\dagger$ generate the Heisenberg algebra, and the requirement of a lowest state (a normalisable ground state) forces the spectrum to be the evenly spaced $E_n = \hbar\omega(n + \tfrac12)$. The discreteness is forced by the algebra plus a boundedness-below condition — again, a standard result.

## 2. The framework-specific reading

NTE's spatial universe is the compact 3-sphere $S^3$ (a core commitment, not derived here). The reading this note records is then immediate: **the discreteness of quantum states is not an extra postulate layered onto a continuous world — it is what a compact $S^3$ universe looks like from within.** Energy levels are quantised because the boundary is closed; the "graininess" of quantum mechanics is the finiteness of the sphere expressed in spectral terms. Where standard quantum mechanics takes discreteness as a feature to be reproduced, the boundary-only ontology takes it as a consequence of the one thing it already assumes — that space is a compact surface.

This is a *reading*, not a new derivation: the spectral mathematics is unchanged and uncredited to NTE. What the framework adds is only the identification of "quantum discreteness" with "spatial compactness of $S^3$" — a one-line ontological economy that the curious reader may find appealing, and which costs nothing because the framework was already committed to $S^3$.
